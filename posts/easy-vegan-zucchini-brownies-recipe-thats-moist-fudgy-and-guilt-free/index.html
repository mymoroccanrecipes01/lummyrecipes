Fudgy Vegan Zucchini Brownies That Surprise Every Time

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 29, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These brownies are shockingly moist and fudgy thanks to zucchini’s magic. No eggs or dairy here! They’re perfect for an afternoon snack or when you want a healthier twist on chocolatey goodness.

Introduction

I first stumbled on zucchini brownies when I was trying to find a way to sneak some veggies into my kids' snacks without them noticing. Honestly, I wasn’t expecting much, but the result totally blew me away! These brownies are incredibly moist and fudgy, thanks to the zucchini, which adds moisture without any weird veggie taste. What makes my version special is squeezing out the zucchini well to avoid sogginess and using coconut oil for a lovely richness that feels indulgent but is still vegan. I’ve tried other recipes that ended up dry or too cakey, but this one strikes the perfect fudgy balance. When these come out of the oven, they’re shiny on top with a tender crumb inside, and they hold together beautifully. They’re great for an afternoon treat or dessert when you want something chocolaty but a bit healthier. Plus, they’re so easy to whip up on a weeknight—no eggs or dairy means no fuss, just pure deliciousness. I hope you love them as much as my family does!

What You'll Need

8 brownies

  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup cocoa powder, unsweetened
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1/3 cup coconut oil, melted
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/4 cup almond milk (or any plant milk)
  • 1/2 cup dairy-free chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because too much liquid will make the brownies soggy and prevent them from setting properly. Removing the excess water helps ensure a fudgy but firm texture once baked. Take your time here—press firmly and twist the towel to get the best results.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels dry to the touch and no dripping water when squeezed
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper. This prevents sticking and helps lift the brownies out easily. Mixing the dry ingredients first—flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt—in a bowl ensures even distribution, which is key for consistent flavor and texture throughout the brownies. Whisking these together breaks up any lumps and aerates the mixture slightly for a better crumb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined with no visible clumps
Step 3
In a separate large bowl, whisk the brown sugar and melted coconut oil until smooth and combined. The coconut oil adds richness and moisture but using it melted helps it blend seamlessly with the sugar and wet ingredients. Stir in the vanilla extract and almond milk next; the milk loosens the batter and helps hydrate the flour for a tender bite. Mixing these wet ingredients thoroughly ensures a smooth base before adding the zucchini and dry mix.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet mixture is smooth and glossy with no grainy sugar
Step 4
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently until just combined. Overmixing here can develop gluten and make the brownies tough, so stop as soon as you don’t see any flour streaks. Next, fold in the grated zucchini along with any residual moisture you left on purpose for extra fudginess. If using, stir in the dairy-free chocolate chips now for melty pockets of chocolate throughout.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is thick, fudgy, and evenly mixed with zucchini and chips distributed
Step 5
Pour the batter into your prepared pan and spread it out evenly with a spatula. This step ensures even cooking and a uniform thickness, which helps the brownies bake consistently. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any air bubbles trapped inside the batter, preventing holes and uneven texture in the final bake.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and level in the baking pan
Step 6
Bake in the preheated oven for 25 to 30 minutes. Start checking around 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out with a few moist crumbs but not wet batter. Baking too long will dry them out, while underbaking leaves a gooey, unstable center. The edges will pull away slightly from the pan, and the top will look set and slightly shiny.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges firm and toothpick test shows moist crumbs, not raw batter
Step 7
Remove the brownies from the oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling is important because the brownies firm up as they come to room temperature, making them easier to cut cleanly without crumbling. Patience here really pays off—cutting while warm risks a messier, gooey result.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brownies feel set and firm to the touch, not sticky or jiggly
Step 8
Once cooled, use the parchment paper to lift the brownies out onto a cutting board. Cut into squares with a sharp knife, wiping the blade clean between cuts for neat edges. Serving these at room temperature lets the fudgy texture shine, but they’re also delicious slightly chilled.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Squares hold shape and edges are clean, not crumbly
Step 9
Store any leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for longer freshness. These brownies actually taste great chilled or warmed slightly before serving. If reheating, just pop a piece in the microwave for 10-15 seconds to revive that melty chocolate goodness.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Brownies remain moist and soft after storage and reheating

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the zucchini thoroughly before adding it to the batter.
Excess water from zucchini can make the brownies too wet and prevent them from setting properly. Removing most moisture ensures the batter stays thick enough to bake into fudgy, not soggy, brownies.
Use melted coconut oil instead of other oils or vegan butter.
Coconut oil solidifies at room temperature, helping the brownies hold their fudgy texture and shape. Other oils remain liquid and can make brownies greasy or too soft.
Don’t overmix the batter once the dry ingredients are added.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, which toughens brownies and ruins their tender, fudgy texture. Mix just until combined for the best crumb.
Line your baking pan with parchment paper with overhang on sides.
This makes removing the brownies easy and prevents sticking. It also helps cut them into neat squares without crumbling.
Check brownies a few minutes before the recommended bake time.
Ovens vary, and overbaking dries out brownies. A toothpick with moist crumbs indicates perfect doneness.
Let brownies cool completely before cutting.
Cooling allows the brownies to firm up, making slicing easier and reducing crumbling.
Add dairy-free chocolate chips for extra melty pockets.
They add texture and bursts of rich chocolate, enhancing the fudginess and flavor complexity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing zucchini enough

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added directly, but its high water content can ruin texture.

Fix Use a towel to press out as much moisture as possible before mixing. This keeps batter thick and brownies fudgy.

Overbaking brownies

Why it happens Waiting the full bake time without checking can dry them out.

Fix Start checking with a toothpick at 25 minutes. Pull them out once a few moist crumbs stick to the toothpick.

Overmixing batter

Why it happens Trying to get a perfectly smooth batter can lead to tough brownies.

Fix Mix dry and wet ingredients until just combined to maintain tenderness.

Using the wrong oil

Why it happens Substituting coconut oil with liquid oils affects texture.

Fix Stick to melted coconut oil to ensure the right consistency and firmness.

Cutting brownies before they cool

Why it happens Impatience leads to messy, crumbling slices.

Fix Allow brownies to cool completely in the pan for clean, neat squares.

Variations

Nutty Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a crunchy texture that contrasts nicely with the fudgy brownie. The nuts add a toasty flavor and make these brownies extra satisfying as a snack or dessert.

Note: Fold nuts in gently with the zucchini and chocolate chips to evenly distribute without overmixing.

Spiced Zucchini Brownies

Stir in 1 teaspoon cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper for a warm, spicy twist that pairs beautifully with chocolate. This variation adds depth and a subtle kick, perfect for cooler months or holiday baking.

Note: Add spices with the dry ingredients to ensure even flavor distribution.

Gluten-Free Vegan Brownies

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend to make these brownies suitable for gluten-sensitive diets. The texture will be just as fudgy but slightly more tender, so handle gently when cutting.

Note: Choose a gluten-free flour blend that contains xanthan gum for best structure.

Peanut Butter Swirl Brownies

Before baking, dollop 1/4 cup peanut butter over the batter and swirl with a knife for a rich, nutty flavor contrast. This adds creaminess and a beautiful marbled look that’s irresistible.

Note: Swirl gently to avoid deflating the batter and keep the fudgy texture.

Orange Zucchini Brownies

Add 1 tablespoon freshly grated orange zest to the batter for a bright, citrusy note that lifts the chocolate flavor. This variation feels fresh and perfect for spring or summer gatherings.

Note: Add zest with the vanilla extract to infuse the batter evenly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat to maintain moisture. They will stay soft and fudgy but are best eaten fresh.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 5 days. The brownies may firm up and taste slightly denser when cold. Let them come to room temperature or warm briefly before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Wrap brownies tightly in plastic wrap and place in a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or warm gently in the microwave. Freezing may slightly change texture but they remain delicious.

Reheating

Warm individual brownies in the microwave for 10-15 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 5-7 minutes. Adding a small splash of plant milk before reheating can help restore moistness. Avoid overheating to prevent drying.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini 1 day ahead and store it wrapped in the fridge. Mix the batter and bake the day of serving for freshest results. Alternatively, bake brownies and store them refrigerated, reheating before serving.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Yes, you can use frozen zucchini, but thaw it completely and squeeze out as much water as possible before adding it to the batter. Frozen zucchini tends to be wetter, so thorough draining is essential to avoid soggy brownies.
What if I don’t have coconut oil? Can I substitute it?
Coconut oil is preferred for its solidifying properties, but you can substitute melted vegan butter or vegetable oil. Keep in mind this may slightly change the texture and firmness of the brownies.
How do I know when the brownies are done baking?
Use the toothpick test: insert a toothpick in the center, and it should come out with moist crumbs but no raw batter. The edges will look set and start to pull away from the pan slightly.
Can I make these brownies gluten-free?
Absolutely! Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free flour blend containing xanthan gum. The texture will remain fudgy but may be slightly more delicate, so handle carefully when cutting.
Why do I need to squeeze the zucchini so much?
Removing excess moisture prevents the batter from becoming too wet and ensures the brownies bake up fudgy but not soggy or runny. It’s a crucial step for texture balance.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Yes! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or dairy-free chocolate chips work great. Fold them in gently with the zucchini to keep the batter texture intact.
How should I store leftover brownies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I make these brownies ahead of time?
You can prep the zucchini a day ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix and bake the brownies the day you want to serve them for best texture and flavor.
