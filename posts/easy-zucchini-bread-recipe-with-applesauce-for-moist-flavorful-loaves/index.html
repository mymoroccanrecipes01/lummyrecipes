Moist Zucchini Bread with Applesauce – A Cozy, Healthy Twist

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 11, 2026
15 min Prep Time
45 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini bread is delightfully moist thanks to the applesauce, which replaces oil and adds natural sweetness. It’s perfect for a lazy weekend breakfast or a comforting afternoon snack. Super simple and wholesome!

Introduction

I first made this zucchini bread years ago when I wanted a healthier way to enjoy my garden zucchini harvest without feeling guilty about all the oil and sugar in typical recipes. The trick I discovered was using applesauce instead of oil, which keeps the bread moist but lighter, plus it adds a subtle natural sweetness that pairs beautifully with the cinnamon and nutmeg. Over time, I've tweaked the spices and sugar balance to get a bread that’s tender, flavorful, and never dry. Sometimes I add walnuts for crunch, but it’s just as good plain. What I love most is how easy it is to throw together, no fancy equipment needed—just a good grater and a loaf pan. The finished bread has a lovely golden crust with a soft, tender crumb inside that feels just right with a smear of butter or a cup of tea. It’s become a go-to for weekend breakfasts or an afternoon pick-me-up, and my family always asks for seconds. If you’ve ever struggled with dry zucchini bread, give this one a try—you might just find your new favorite!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 1 ½ cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1 cup unsweetened applesauce
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 ¾ cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • ½ teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • ½ cup brown sugar, packed
  • ½ cup chopped walnuts (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because excess water can make the bread soggy and prevent it from baking properly. Drying the zucchini ensures the batter has the right consistency and the bread will hold its shape with a tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping wet; no visible pools of water.
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the applesauce, eggs, and vanilla extract until smooth. The applesauce replaces oil here, so combining these wet ingredients thoroughly helps create a uniform batter that will rise evenly and stay moist. Be sure to whisk well to avoid clumps of egg whites or uneven texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks smooth and slightly glossy without streaks.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, sift together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, cinnamon, and nutmeg. Sifting aerates the dry ingredients, breaking up clumps and ensuring even distribution of the leavening agents and spices. This step helps the bread rise nicely and gives a consistent flavor throughout.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are light, fluffy, and well combined.
Step 4
Slowly add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, folding gently with a spatula or wooden spoon. Avoid overmixing here because that can develop gluten and result in a tough bread. You want just enough mixing to combine everything without streaks of flour.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no pockets of dry flour, but still slightly lumpy.
Step 5
Fold in the grated zucchini and brown sugar, mixing just until evenly distributed. The sugar helps balance the mild flavor of zucchini and applesauce, while adding moisture and tenderness. Make sure the zucchini is evenly spread throughout so every bite has that fresh veggie goodness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and sugar are mixed evenly without overworking the batter.
Step 6
If using, fold in the chopped walnuts for added texture and a nutty flavor contrast. This is optional but highly recommended for a bit of crunch and extra richness. Be gentle to avoid breaking the nuts into tiny pieces.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Nuts are evenly scattered through the batter.
Step 7
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or nonstick spray. Pour the batter into the pan and smooth the top with a spatula. The even surface helps the bread bake uniformly and develop a nice crust.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread with no air pockets on top.
Step 8
Bake in the preheated oven for about 45 minutes. Start checking at 40 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center; it should come out clean or with just a few moist crumbs. Baking too long dries out the bread, while underbaking leaves it gummy inside.
⏱ Time: 40-45 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust and toothpick comes out mostly clean.
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. Cooling is important because it allows the bread to set and prevents it from breaking apart when sliced. Plus, it enhances flavor as the steam escapes.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm and slightly springy to the touch.

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from grated zucchini.
Removing water prevents the batter from becoming too wet, which can cause the bread to be dense and gummy. This step ensures the bread holds together and bakes evenly with a tender crumb.
Use room temperature eggs and applesauce for better mixing.
Cold ingredients can cause the batter to curdle or mix unevenly, affecting texture. Room temperature ingredients blend more smoothly, resulting in a uniform batter and consistent rise.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add flour.
Overmixing develops gluten strands that make the bread tough and chewy instead of soft. Mixing just until combined keeps the crumb tender and light.
Check for doneness with a toothpick starting at 40 minutes.
Ovens vary, and overbaking dries the bread. Testing early helps catch it at the perfect moist, fluffy stage before it becomes dry.
Let the bread cool in the pan before removing.
Cooling lets the bread set and prevents it from crumbling when sliced. It also helps the flavor develop as moisture redistributes.
Add chopped nuts for texture, but fold them in gently.
Nuts add crunch and richness, but vigorous mixing can break them down and alter the batter’s texture.
Use fresh spices for the best aroma and flavor.
Older spices lose potency and can make the bread taste flat. Freshly ground cinnamon and nutmeg brighten the flavor beautifully.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out zucchini moisture

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be used as-is, but it contains a lot of water that can ruin the bread’s texture.

Fix After grating, wrap zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove water. This step is essential to prevent soggy bread.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens It's tempting to mix until perfectly smooth, but too much mixing develops gluten, toughening the bread.

Fix Mix dry ingredients into wet just until no dry flour remains. Some lumps are okay and help keep a tender crumb.

Skipping the leavening agents

Why it happens Sometimes people reduce baking soda or powder to make it 'healthier' or forget to add one.

Fix Use both baking soda and baking powder as specified to get a good rise and light texture.

Baking at the wrong temperature

Why it happens Ovens can be off or preheating skipped, leading to uneven baking or dry crusts.

Fix Always preheat oven fully to 350°F (175°C) and use an oven thermometer if needed for accuracy.

Not letting the bread cool before slicing

Why it happens Eagerness to eat can lead to cutting hot bread, which often crumbles or sticks.

Fix Let the bread cool at least 10 minutes in the pan, then transfer to rack to finish cooling for cleaner slices.

Variations

Banana Zucchini Bread

Swap the applesauce for mashed ripe bananas for a sweeter, fruitier bread. This adds moisture and a subtle banana flavor, making it extra tender and perfect for banana lovers.

Note: Bananas add more moisture than applesauce, so reduce other liquids slightly to avoid sogginess.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Bread

Use a gluten-free flour blend instead of all-purpose flour to make this bread suitable for gluten intolerance. Expect a slightly denser texture but still moist and flavorful.

Note: Add 1/4 teaspoon xanthan gum if your flour blend doesn’t contain it to help with structure.

Spiced Pumpkin Zucchini Bread

Add 1/2 cup pumpkin puree and increase cinnamon to 1 1/2 teaspoons for a fall-inspired twist. The pumpkin adds richness and a deeper flavor profile.

Note: Reduce applesauce to 1/2 cup to balance added pumpkin moisture.

Chocolate Chip Zucchini Bread

Fold in 1/2 cup mini chocolate chips to the batter for a sweet surprise. The chocolate pairs wonderfully with the cinnamon-spiced zucchini bread.

Note: Add chips last and fold gently to prevent melting or sinking.

Vegan Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs and use a plant-based yogurt instead of applesauce for a vegan-friendly version. The texture remains moist, though slightly denser.

Note: Mix 1 tablespoon ground flaxseed with 3 tablespoons water per egg and let sit before adding.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the zucchini bread covered loosely at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it wrapped in foil or in a bread box to maintain moisture but avoid sogginess from trapped steam.

Refrigerator

Keep in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up slightly; warm slices briefly in the microwave or toaster oven for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and then foil or in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature for a few hours or overnight in the fridge. Reheat gently to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds. Adding a small pat of butter or a light spray of water before reheating helps keep it moist.

Make Ahead

You can grate zucchini and mix dry ingredients 1-2 days ahead and store separately. Combine wet ingredients and assemble the batter the day you plan to bake for best freshness and rise.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
Yes, but thaw and squeeze out excess moisture thoroughly before using. Frozen zucchini holds more water, so extra squeezing helps prevent soggy bread and ensures the right texture.
What if I don’t have applesauce?
You can substitute with mashed banana or pumpkin puree, but these will alter the flavor and moisture level. Adjust sugar and liquids slightly to compensate for the change.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend and add xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it. The texture may be slightly denser but still delicious.
Why does my zucchini bread turn out dry sometimes?
Common causes include overbaking, not enough moisture (like applesauce), or overmixing the batter which develops gluten. Follow timing closely and be gentle mixing.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Yes, folding in nuts or chocolate chips adds texture and flavor. Add them at the end of mixing to avoid breaking them up or melting.
How should I store leftover zucchini bread?
Wrap leftovers tightly and store at room temperature for a couple of days or refrigerate up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze well-wrapped for up to 3 months.
Can I double this recipe?
You can, but bake in two loaf pans to ensure even cooking. Baking time may be similar, but check doneness carefully with a toothpick.
Is it okay to use brown sugar instead of white sugar?
Yes, brown sugar adds moisture and a richer flavor. It’s the preferred choice here for a moister, more flavorful bread.
