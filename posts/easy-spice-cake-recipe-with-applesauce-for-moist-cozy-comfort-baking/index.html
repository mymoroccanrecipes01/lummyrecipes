Cozy Spice Cake with Applesauce That Stays So Moist and Tender

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 13, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This spice cake uses applesauce to keep it moist and naturally sweet without any extra oil. It’s tender, flavorful, and perfect for chilly afternoons or casual family dessert nights. I love how it feels like a warm hug in cake form!

Introduction

I first made this applesauce spice cake on a chilly autumn afternoon when I wanted something comforting but without the heaviness of a traditional butter-rich cake. My family loves spice cakes, but I was curious if using applesauce could keep it moist without adding extra oil or butter. After a few trial runs, I nailed a version that’s tender, naturally sweet, and bursting with warm spices. What makes this cake stand out is the applesauce — it adds moisture and a subtle fruity note that pairs beautifully with cinnamon and nutmeg. Plus, I use buttermilk for a slight tang and fluffy crumb. The trick I learned is to mix the dry and wet ingredients gently to keep the texture light yet moist. The finished cake has a golden crust with a soft, tender inside that’s just perfect with a cup of tea or coffee. Honestly, it’s become my go-to when I want a cozy dessert that’s not too heavy but still feels indulgent. I hope you enjoy baking and eating it as much as my family does!

What You'll Need

One 9-inch round cake, 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 teaspoons ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground cloves
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground ginger
  • 1 cup unsweetened applesauce
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup buttermilk

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease and flour a 9-inch round cake pan to prevent sticking. This step ensures your cake releases easily after baking and keeps the crust intact. Proper pan preparation can be overlooked, but it’s essential for a neat finish.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased with no bare spots; light dusting of flour coats the surface
Step 2
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and all the spices (cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, ginger). Sifting or whisking these dry ingredients helps distribute the leavening agents and spices evenly, which prevents uneven rising and flavor pockets.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dry mix looks uniform with no clumps of baking powder or spices
Step 3
In a separate bowl, combine the applesauce with both sugars. Stir until the sugars start dissolving into the applesauce. This mix provides moisture and sweetness, and dissolving the sugar first helps create a smooth batter and tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Sugar visibly dissolves slightly into the applesauce, mixture looks smooth
Step 4
Beat in the eggs one at a time to the applesauce-sugar mixture, then add the vanilla extract. Incorporating eggs gradually helps emulsify the mixture, trapping air that contributes to the cake’s light texture.
⏱ Time: 4
✓ Doneness: Mixture is glossy and slightly thickened, no streaks of egg white remain
Step 5
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry mixture and stir gently until just combined. Avoid overmixing here to prevent gluten development, which would make the cake tough. The batter should be thick but smooth with no dry spots.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color and texture, no flour pockets visible
Step 6
Slowly add the buttermilk to the batter and fold it in gently. The acidity in buttermilk reacts with baking soda to help the cake rise and adds a subtle tang that balances the sweetness. Mixing gently preserves the air bubbles needed for a tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Batter loosens slightly but remains smooth and homogenous
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared cake pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the cake bake evenly and prevents doming or cracking on top. Tap the pan lightly on the counter to release any large air bubbles.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Batter evenly spread with a smooth surface, no large bubbles
Step 8
Bake the cake in the preheated oven for about 30 minutes. Check doneness by inserting a toothpick into the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Avoid opening the oven door too often as it can cause the cake to sink.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted in center comes out clean or with moist crumbs
Step 9
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. Cooling in the pan helps the cake set, but removing it too late can cause sogginess. Once on the rack, it cools evenly preventing condensation that could make the crust soggy.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Cake feels firm to the touch and releases easily from the pan

Pro Tips

Always use unsweetened applesauce to control the sweetness level.
Using unsweetened applesauce lets you balance the sugar in the recipe precisely. Sweetened versions can make the cake overly sweet and affect texture by adding extra moisture.
Don’t overmix the batter after combining wet and dry ingredients.
Overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the crumb. By mixing gently until just combined, you keep the texture tender and soft.
Use room temperature eggs for better emulsification.
Room temperature eggs blend more smoothly with the wet ingredients, trapping air and creating a lighter batter. Cold eggs can cause the batter to seize or not mix evenly.
Sift or whisk your dry ingredients together before adding liquids.
This ensures even distribution of baking powder, baking soda, and spices, which prevents uneven rising or flavor pockets in the cake.
Let the cake cool in the pan for 10 minutes before removing.
This allows the cake to set and reduces the chance of it breaking apart. Removing it too soon can cause the cake to crumble.
Tap the cake pan gently before baking to release air bubbles.
Air bubbles can create holes or uneven texture. Tapping the pan smooths the batter for an even crumb.
Use fresh spices for the best flavor impact.
Old spices lose their potency, and fresh spices bring vibrant aroma and warmth that make the cake feel truly cozy.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Many home bakers think mixing more will make the batter smoother, but it actually develops gluten, making the cake dense.

Fix Mix just until the dry ingredients are moistened. Stop as soon as you don’t see flour streaks. Use a spatula and fold gently instead of beating vigorously.

Using sweetened applesauce

Why it happens It’s easy to grab whatever applesauce is on hand, but sweetened versions add unexpected sugar and moisture.

Fix Always check labels and choose unsweetened applesauce. Adjust sugar in the recipe if you must use sweetened.

Not measuring flour correctly

Why it happens Scooping flour with a cup can pack it too tightly, adding too much flour and drying the cake.

Fix Spoon flour into your measuring cup and level with a knife for accurate measurement. This keeps the cake tender.

Opening the oven door too often while baking

Why it happens Curiosity or impatience can cause temperature drops, which affect rising.

Fix Resist opening the door until the last 5 minutes. Use the oven light to check progress instead.

Removing cake from pan too soon

Why it happens Removing immediately can cause the cake to break or stick.

Fix Allow the cake to cool in the pan for about 10 minutes to firm up before turning out onto a rack.

Variations

Add Chopped Walnuts or Pecans

Stir in 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts or pecans to the batter before baking for a crunchy texture contrast. The nuts add a lovely nutty flavor that pairs well with the warm spices and moist crumb.

Note: Fold nuts gently to avoid overmixing. Toasting nuts enhances their flavor but is optional.

Swap Applesauce for Pumpkin Puree

Replace the applesauce with an equal amount of pumpkin puree for a fall-inspired twist. This changes the flavor profile slightly, giving it a deeper, earthier taste while keeping the moist texture.

Note: Pumpkin puree is thicker, so batter might be slightly denser; monitor baking time closely.

Add a Cream Cheese Glaze

Top the cooled cake with a simple cream cheese glaze made from cream cheese, powdered sugar, and vanilla. This adds a tangy sweetness that complements the spicy flavors beautifully.

Note: Make sure the cake is fully cooled before glazing to prevent melting.

Make It Gluten-Free

Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend in place of regular flour. This allows those with gluten intolerance to enjoy the cake without sacrificing moisture or flavor.

Note: Check that your baking powder is gluten-free. The texture may be slightly different but still delicious.

Add Diced Apples for Texture

Fold in 1 cup of peeled, diced apples into the batter for extra fruity texture and bursts of sweetness throughout the cake. It enhances the apple flavor and adds moisture.

Note: Toss diced apples in a little flour before adding to prevent sinking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Keep the cake covered tightly with plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. This prevents the cake from drying out and keeps it fresh for casual snacking.

Refrigerator

Store leftover cake in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The cake may firm up and become a bit denser when cold, so it’s best served at room temperature or warmed slightly before eating.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled cake tightly in plastic wrap and aluminum foil before freezing. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator and bring to room temperature before serving to restore the best texture.

Reheating

Warm slices gently in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a low oven (300°F) for 10 minutes. Adding a small splash of milk before warming can help restore moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent drying out.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a few hours ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh when ready for best texture. The baked cake also freezes well if made a day or two in advance.

FAQ

Can I use oil instead of applesauce?
Yes, you can substitute applesauce with an equal amount of vegetable oil, but the cake will be richer and less naturally sweet. Applesauce keeps it lighter and adds subtle fruit flavor.
My cake came out dry. What went wrong?
Dry cake usually means overbaking or too much flour. Make sure to measure flour correctly and check the cake a few minutes before the baking time ends to avoid drying it out.
Can I make this cake vegan?
To make it vegan, replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based milk with a splash of vinegar instead of buttermilk. The texture may vary slightly.
Why is my cake dense and not fluffy?
Overmixing the batter or using expired leavening agents can cause density. Mix gently and check your baking powder/soda for freshness to ensure a light rise.
Can I add frosting or glaze?
Absolutely! A cream cheese glaze or simple powdered sugar glaze pairs wonderfully with this cake, adding extra sweetness and moisture.
How do I store leftover cake?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly for up to 3 months.
Can I make cupcakes instead of a cake?
Yes, bake the batter in lined muffin tins at 350°F for about 18-22 minutes. Keep an eye on them and test for doneness with a toothpick.
Why does the cake crack on top?
Cracking is usually due to oven temperature being too high or batter placed too high in the oven. Bake in the middle rack and check your oven’s temperature accuracy.
