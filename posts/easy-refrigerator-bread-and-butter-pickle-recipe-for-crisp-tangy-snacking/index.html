Simple Tangy Bread and Butter Pickles Ready in Hours

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 31, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These quick bread and butter pickles are a perfect blend of sweet, tangy, and a little spice from mustard seeds. I love how fast they come together—ready in just a few hours! Great for sandwiches or just snacking.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon bread and butter pickles during a summer trip visiting my aunt, who had jars of them lining her pantry. They were unlike any pickles I'd had before — sweet, tangy, with just a touch of spice that made them addictive. Since then, I've been perfecting my own quick version that doesn't require weeks of waiting. What makes this recipe special is how it balances the sweetness with a gentle vinegar tang and the subtle heat from mustard seeds. Plus, it takes just a few hours from start to finish, so you can enjoy fresh pickles the same day. The trick I learned was to slice the cucumbers thin and salt them beforehand, which helps pull out excess moisture and keeps the pickles crisp. When done right, these pickles stay crunchy and flavorful, perfect to brighten up sandwiches or just munch on straight from the jar. I love how simple and forgiving this recipe is, making it a go-to for whenever I want a quick pickle fix.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 4 cups thinly sliced cucumbers, peeled or unpeeled
  • 1 cup thinly sliced onions
  • 1 1/2 cups white vinegar
  • 1 1/4 cups granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon mustard seeds
  • 1 teaspoon celery seeds
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 tablespoon salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing 4 cups of cucumbers and 1 cup of onions. The thin slices allow the brine to penetrate quickly, ensuring your pickles get flavorful fast. Toss them in a large bowl with 1 tablespoon of salt and let sit for about an hour. This step draws out excess water from the cucumbers, which helps them stay crisp when pickled. Don’t skip this or your pickles might turn out soggy. After an hour, rinse the cucumbers and onions under cold water to remove the salt, then drain well to avoid watering down your brine.
⏱ Time: 60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers will look slightly wilted but still firm; excess water will have been released.
Step 2
While the cucumbers are sitting, combine 1 1/2 cups white vinegar, 1 1/4 cups sugar, 1 tablespoon mustard seeds, 1 teaspoon celery seeds, and 1/2 teaspoon turmeric in a saucepan. Heat over medium heat, stirring occasionally, until the sugar dissolves and the mixture just begins to simmer. This hot brine will infuse your pickles with the classic sweet and tangy flavor. Be careful not to boil vigorously as it can dilute flavors and toughen the cucumbers.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sugar is fully dissolved, and the brine is steaming with a gentle simmer.
Step 3
Place the drained cucumbers and onions into a clean glass jar or bowl. Pour the hot brine over the vegetables, making sure they’re fully submerged. The heat helps the flavors meld quickly and preserves the pickles. If you’re using a jar, seal it tightly. Let it cool to room temperature, then refrigerate. The pickles will be ready to enjoy in about 3-4 hours but taste even better after overnight chilling.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables fully covered by brine, jar sealed or bowl covered.
Step 4
After chilling, check the pickles for flavor. If you want them sweeter, add a bit more sugar next time. For extra crunch, make sure the cucumbers are fresh and firm when you start. Also, the mustard seeds add a lovely subtle spice that develops as the pickles sit, so don’t rush eating them too soon.
⏱ Time: Optional tasting after 3-4 hours
✓ Doneness: Pickles have a balanced sweet-tart flavor with a mild mustard seed aroma.
Step 5
Serve these bread and butter pickles chilled alongside sandwiches, burgers, or even as a tangy snack on their own. They’re a great way to add brightness to rich or heavy meals. I find they pair especially well with grilled meats or creamy salads.
⏱ Time: No cooking time
✓ Doneness: Pickles cooled and ready to complement your dish.
Step 6
If you want to store the pickles longer, keep them refrigerated in an airtight container. They’ll stay fresh and crunchy for up to 2 weeks. Always use a clean utensil to avoid contamination and extend shelf life.
⏱ Time: No cooking time
✓ Doneness: Pickles remain crisp and flavorful during storage.
Step 7
For a little extra zing, you can add a pinch of crushed red pepper flakes or a few slices of fresh jalapeño to the brine before heating. This adds a nice subtle heat without overpowering the sweet and tangy balance.
⏱ Time: Add before heating brine
✓ Doneness: Brine infused with a hint of spice and aroma.
Step 8
To keep the pickles as crisp as possible, avoid over-soaking them in the brine. If you prefer softer pickles, leave them longer in the fridge, but for that classic crunch, enjoy them within the first week.
⏱ Time: Monitor during storage
✓ Doneness: Pickle texture matches your preference.
Step 9
Finally, when serving, let the pickles come to just above fridge temp for the best flavor release. Cold temperatures can mute flavor, so a short rest outside the fridge helps the spices and sweetness shine.
⏱ Time: 10-15 minutes before serving
✓ Doneness: Pickles taste vibrant and balanced.

Pro Tips

Salt cucumbers for at least an hour before pickling.
Salting pulls excess water from cucumbers, preventing soggy pickles and helping them stay crisp. Without this step, the brine dilutes the cucumber texture, resulting in mushy pickles. This simple trick is key for perfect crunch.
Use thin, even slices of cucumbers and onions.
Thin slices increase surface area, allowing the brine to permeate quickly and evenly. This speeds up flavor absorption, so your pickles are ready in hours, not weeks. Uneven slices can lead to inconsistent texture and taste.
Heat the brine just until sugar dissolves and it simmers gently.
Heating activates the spices and dissolves sugar, but boiling too vigorously can toughen cucumbers and mellow flavors. A gentle simmer preserves delicate aromas and ensures a balanced brine.
Let pickles cool to room temperature before refrigerating.
Placing hot pickles directly in the fridge can cause condensation and dilute flavors. Cooling allows flavors to meld and prevents softening caused by rapid temperature change.
Store pickles in a clean, airtight container.
Proper storage prevents contamination and preserves crunch and flavor. Exposure to air can cause pickles to spoil faster or lose texture. Always use clean utensils to avoid introducing bacteria.
Add mustard seeds and celery seeds for authentic flavor.
These spices contribute the signature tang and warmth of bread and butter pickles. Omitting them results in a bland brine lacking depth and complexity.
Try a pinch of turmeric for color and subtle earthiness.
Turmeric gives the pickles their classic golden hue and adds a slight warmth that complements the sweet and sour base, enhancing overall flavor without overpowering.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting step.

Why it happens Many cooks want to speed things up and skip salting, assuming it’s unnecessary. This leads to watery, soggy pickles.

Fix Always salt cucumbers for at least an hour beforehand. This draws out moisture and firms them up, which is essential for crisp pickles. Rinse well afterward to remove excess salt.

Using the wrong vinegar.

Why it happens Some use flavored or malt vinegar thinking it will add complexity, but it changes the pickle’s classic taste and color.

Fix Stick to plain white vinegar with 5% acidity for balanced tang and color. Avoid other vinegars unless you want a different pickle style.

Boiling the brine too hard.

Why it happens Rushing to dissolve sugar or activate spices by boiling vigorously can toughen cucumbers.

Fix Heat the brine gently until sugar dissolves and it just starts simmering. This keeps flavors delicate and cucumbers tender-crisp.

Not draining cucumbers well before adding hot brine.

Why it happens If cucumbers are too wet, the brine gets diluted and flavor weakens.

Fix After salting and rinsing, drain cucumbers thoroughly to avoid watering down your brine and ensure punchy flavor.

Using thick cucumber slices.

Why it happens Thick slices take longer to pickle and can be unevenly flavored.

Fix Use a mandoline or sharp knife to slice cucumbers thinly and evenly for quick, consistent pickling.

Variations

Spicy Jalapeño Bread and Butter Pickles

Add thin slices of fresh jalapeño peppers to the cucumbers before pickling for a spicy kick. The heat balances the sweetness and adds complexity. Expect a bit more bite and warmth in the final pickles.

Note: Add jalapeños to the jar before pouring brine. Adjust amount depending on desired spice level.

Dill-Infused Bread and Butter Pickles

Add a few sprigs of fresh dill or 1 teaspoon dried dill seed to the brine for an herby twist. This variation pairs wonderfully with sandwiches and adds a fresh aroma.

Note: Include dill with the mustard and celery seeds when heating the brine to infuse flavor.

Sugar-Free Bread and Butter Pickles

Replace sugar with a natural sweetener like stevia or monk fruit to make a low-calorie version. The tang and spice remain, but sweetness is milder. Texture stays crisp with same salting technique.

Note: Adjust sweetener quantity carefully, as some are more potent than sugar. Dissolve fully in brine.

Quick Refrigerator Bread and Butter Pickles

Skip the heating step by mixing sugar, vinegar, and spices cold, then pouring over cucumbers. These pickles take longer to develop flavor but are simpler and still tasty after a day or two in the fridge.

Note: Use finely ground mustard powder instead of seeds for better flavor extraction without heating.

Bread and Butter Pickle Relish

Chop the cucumbers and onions finely after salting and mix with the brine for a relish-style condiment. Great on hot dogs or burgers with a sweet-tangy crunch.

Note: Reduce soaking time slightly as smaller pieces absorb flavors faster.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Bread and butter pickles should not be left at room temperature for more than 2 hours. Keep them covered in a clean container if serving out for short periods to avoid contamination.

Refrigerator

Store pickles in an airtight jar or container in the fridge for up to 2 weeks. Flavor intensifies over time, but they remain crunchy best within the first week. Serve chilled or slightly warmed to release aromas.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as it breaks down cucumber texture, resulting in soggy pickles. For longer storage, can or process using proper pickling methods.

Reheating

No reheating needed. If desired, let pickles sit at room temperature for 10-15 minutes before serving to enhance flavor. Avoid microwaving as it softens texture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the cucumbers and onions salted and drained 1 day ahead, then add brine and refrigerate on serving day. This shortens overall prep time and improves crispness.

FAQ

Can I use regular cucumbers instead of pickling cucumbers?
Yes, but pickling cucumbers are firmer and have fewer seeds, which helps keep pickles crunchy. Regular slicing cucumbers can work but may result in softer pickles due to higher water content.
How long do these pickles last in the fridge?
Stored properly in an airtight container, bread and butter pickles stay good for up to 2 weeks. They taste best within the first week when they’re at their crispiest and most flavorful.
Can I make these pickles without sugar?
You can substitute sugar with natural sweeteners like stevia or monk fruit, but the texture and sweetness might differ slightly. Sugar also helps preserve texture, so results may vary.
Why are my pickles soggy?
Sogginess usually happens when cucumbers aren’t salted first to draw out moisture, or when the brine is too diluted. Make sure to salt and drain cucumbers well before adding brine.
Can I can these pickles for longer storage?
This recipe is designed for refrigerator pickles. For canning, you’ll need to follow proper sterilization and processing guidelines to ensure safety and shelf stability.
What if I don’t have mustard seeds?
You can use ground mustard powder as a substitute, but add it to the brine after heating to avoid bitterness. Mustard seeds provide a milder, more balanced flavor and texture.
Can I add other spices?
Absolutely! Feel free to experiment with cloves, allspice, or cinnamon for a unique twist. Just start with small amounts to avoid overpowering the classic flavor.
How thin should I slice the cucumbers?
Aim for about 1/8 inch thick slices. Thin slices absorb flavors quickly and stay crunchy. Thicker slices take longer to pickle and may become soft.
