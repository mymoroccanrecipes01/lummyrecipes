Refreshing Cucumber and Onion Salad with Tangy Dill Dressing

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 1, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crisp cucumber and onion salad is a summer favorite. The tangy vinegar and sugar dressing with fresh dill gives it a bright, balanced flavor. I love making it ahead so the flavors really come together.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this cucumber and onion salad recipe during a hot summer cookout a few years ago, and it quickly became a staple in my kitchen. What I love most is how incredibly simple it is to throw together, yet the flavors are so vibrant and refreshing. Unlike heavier salads, this one feels light and crisp, making it the perfect companion for grilled meats or just a quick snack on its own. The trick I learned is to slice the cucumbers and onions very thinly, almost paper-thin, which allows the dressing to soak in beautifully and keeps every bite crunchy. Also, letting the salad sit in the fridge for at least an hour before serving helps the flavors meld into this perfect sweet and tangy harmony. I’ve tried variations with different herbs, but dill always wins in my book—it adds that subtle grassy note that makes this salad stand out. The finished salad looks inviting with its vibrant green and purple hues and tastes like a fresh breeze on a warm day. Honestly, sometimes I make a big batch and it disappears before the main dish even hits the table!

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 cups, 4 servings

  • 3 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup white vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup fresh dill, chopped
  • Freshly ground black pepper, to taste

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing and drying your cucumbers thoroughly. Then, using a sharp knife or mandoline, slice them as thinly as possible. Thin slices ensure the dressing clings well and the salad stays crisp rather than watery. Be careful with the mandoline if you use one—always use the guard to protect your fingers. Thin slicing also helps the cucumbers absorb the dressing quickly, making every bite flavorful.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers should be uniformly thin, almost translucent slices.
Step 2
Peel and slice the red onion thinly, matching the cucumber slices in thickness. Thin onion slices prevent overpowering sharpness and blend nicely with the cucumber’s mild flavor. If you prefer a milder onion taste, soak the slices in cold water for 10 minutes before draining. This step mellows the onion’s bite without losing its crunch.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onion slices should be thin and flexible but not mushy.
Step 3
In a small bowl, whisk together the white vinegar, granulated sugar, and salt until the sugar dissolves completely. This simple dressing balances tartness and sweetness, which is key to the salad’s bright flavor. Stirring until sugar is fully dissolved prevents grainy texture in the dressing and ensures even flavor distribution.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing is smooth and slightly glossy with no sugar granules.
Step 4
Place the sliced cucumbers and onions in a large mixing bowl. Pour the vinegar dressing over the vegetables and toss gently but thoroughly to coat every slice. Use a large spoon or your hands to mix, but be gentle to avoid bruising the cucumbers. Even coating helps the flavors infuse evenly throughout the salad.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: All slices appear shiny and evenly coated with dressing.
Step 5
Add the chopped fresh dill and freshly ground black pepper to the salad. Dill adds a fresh, herbaceous note that brightens the overall flavor profile. Pepper adds a subtle kick without overwhelming the delicate balance. Toss again gently to distribute the herbs evenly.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Herbs are evenly scattered and the salad smells fragrant.
Step 6
Cover the bowl tightly with plastic wrap or transfer the salad to an airtight container. Refrigerate for at least one hour before serving. This resting time allows the cucumbers and onions to soak up the dressing, softening the sharpness and blending the flavors beautifully. Skipping this step will result in a less flavorful salad.
⏱ Time: 60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad looks slightly wilted but still crisp and vibrant.
Step 7
Before serving, give the salad a gentle toss to redistribute any settling dressing and herbs. Taste and adjust seasoning with extra salt or pepper if needed. Sometimes the cucumbers absorb a lot of the dressing, so a quick flavor check helps balance it perfectly.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad tastes balanced with a bright sweet-tart flavor.
Step 8
Serve chilled as a refreshing side dish alongside grilled meats, sandwiches, or as part of a picnic spread. Its crisp texture and lively flavor complement heavier dishes well, cutting through richness and adding brightness. If you’re making it for a crowd, double the recipe — it disappears fast!
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad is cool and crisp, perfect for immediate enjoyment.
Step 9
If you want to prepare ahead, keep the salad refrigerated and stir gently before serving. This salad holds well for up to two days but is best fresh to maintain that crunchy texture. Avoid adding herbs too early if you plan to store it longer, as they can wilt and discolor.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad remains fresh-looking with vibrant colors after storage.

Pro Tips

Slice cucumbers and onions as thin as possible for best texture.
Thin slices increase surface area allowing the dressing to coat evenly and penetrate quickly. This keeps the salad crisp and flavorful rather than soggy. Thick slices won’t absorb flavors well and can become watery.
Let the salad rest refrigerated for at least 1 hour before serving.
Resting allows the cucumbers and onions to soften slightly and absorb the dressing, blending flavors. It mellows the onion’s sharpness and makes the salad more harmonious. Serving immediately results in less depth of flavor.
Use fresh dill instead of dried for maximum flavor impact.
Fresh dill releases essential oils that add brightness and complexity. Dried dill can taste flat and powdery, reducing the salad’s freshness. Adding dill too early when storing can cause it to wilt and discolor, so add it just before chilling.
Dissolve sugar completely in vinegar before mixing with vegetables.
Undissolved sugar can create grainy texture and uneven sweetness. Stirring until fully dissolved ensures a smooth dressing that coats the salad evenly. It also helps balance the vinegar’s acidity perfectly.
Season with salt and pepper after tossing to taste.
Salt helps draw out moisture and balance flavors, while pepper adds subtle heat. Adding them after mixing lets you adjust seasoning precisely based on how much dressing the vegetables have absorbed.
Use a mandoline slicer for consistent thin slices.
A mandoline provides uniform slices which cook and absorb flavors evenly. Uneven slices can cause inconsistent texture and flavor in the salad. Just be careful to use the safety guard to avoid injury.
Serve chilled for the most refreshing experience.
Cold temperature enhances the salad’s crispness and bright flavors, making it especially satisfying on warm days. Serving it warm can make the cucumbers soggy and dull the fresh taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using thick cucumber or onion slices.

Why it happens People often slice thickly to save time or because they don’t have proper tools, not realizing this affects texture and flavor absorption.

Fix Use a sharp knife or mandoline to slice as thinly as possible. Thin slices soak up dressing better and stay crisp, making the salad more enjoyable.

Skipping the resting time before serving.

Why it happens Impatience or time constraints lead to serving immediately, missing the flavor melding step.

Fix Plan ahead and refrigerate the salad for at least an hour. This allows the flavors to blend and the onion’s sharpness to mellow for a balanced taste.

Not dissolving sugar fully in vinegar.

Why it happens Rushing the dressing prep or stirring insufficiently can leave sugar undissolved.

Fix Whisk the vinegar and sugar together until completely smooth and glossy. This ensures even sweetness and prevents graininess.

Using dried dill instead of fresh.

Why it happens Convenience or lack of fresh dill leads to substitution, but dried dill lacks brightness.

Fix Always opt for fresh dill chopped finely and add it just before chilling to preserve its aroma and color.

Not adjusting seasoning after chilling.

Why it happens Flavor changes during chilling are overlooked, resulting in bland or overly sharp salad.

Fix Taste the salad after resting and add salt or pepper as needed to balance flavors perfectly.

Variations

Add Cherry Tomatoes and Feta Cheese

For a Mediterranean twist, toss in halved cherry tomatoes and crumbled feta cheese. The juicy sweetness of tomatoes and creamy tang of feta add richness and color, making it a more substantial side or light salad.

Note: Add tomatoes and feta just before serving to avoid sogginess and preserve texture.

Spicy Kick with Red Chili Flakes

If you like a bit of heat, sprinkle in some red chili flakes or finely chopped fresh chili peppers. This adds a spicy contrast to the sweet-tart dressing and livens up the flavor profile.

Note: Adjust quantity to taste and add chili when mixing dressing to infuse flavor evenly.

Use Apple Cider Vinegar Instead of White Vinegar

Swap white vinegar for apple cider vinegar for a milder, fruitier acidity. This variation softens the sharpness and adds a subtle depth that pairs well with fresh dill.

Note: Apple cider vinegar may require slightly less sugar to maintain balance.

Add Fresh Mint for Extra Herbaceous Notes

Mix in chopped fresh mint along with dill to brighten the salad with a cooling, aromatic twist. Mint complements the cucumber’s freshness beautifully and adds complexity.

Note: Add mint just before chilling to keep it vibrant and prevent wilting.

Make It Creamy with a Dollop of Greek Yogurt

For a creamy version, stir in a few tablespoons of Greek yogurt into the dressing. This softens the vinegar’s sharpness and adds a silky texture, turning the salad into a light, creamy side.

Note: Add yogurt last and mix gently to avoid breaking the texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad can be left out at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered, but it's best served chilled. Avoid leaving it longer to prevent sogginess and bacterial growth.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 2 days. The salad will continue to soften and flavors meld, but cucumbers may release more water. Give it a gentle toss before serving to redistribute dressing.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become watery and lose their crisp texture upon thawing.

Reheating

This salad is best served cold and should not be reheated, as heat will wilt the cucumbers and alter the fresh flavors.

Make Ahead

You can slice the cucumbers and onions and prepare the dressing 1 day ahead. Store them separately and combine just before chilling to maintain maximum freshness and texture.

FAQ

Can I use regular cucumbers instead of English cucumbers?
Yes, you can use regular cucumbers, but they have thicker skin and more seeds, which can make the salad slightly more bitter and watery. To reduce bitterness, peel the cucumbers and scoop out the seeds before slicing.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
The salad keeps well for up to 2 days refrigerated in an airtight container. After that, the cucumbers may become too soft and watery. For best texture, consume within the first 24 hours.
Can I substitute apple cider vinegar for white vinegar?
Absolutely! Apple cider vinegar provides a milder, fruitier acidity that works nicely. You might want to reduce the sugar slightly since apple cider vinegar is less sharp than white vinegar.
What if I don’t have fresh dill? Can I use dried?
While dried dill can be used in a pinch, it lacks the bright, fresh flavor of fresh dill and can taste a bit powdery. If using dried, add less and mix it directly into the dressing rather than the salad for better distribution.
How can I make the onions less sharp?
Soak the thinly sliced onions in cold water for 10 minutes before mixing them with cucumbers. This helps mellow their pungency without losing crunch.
Is it okay to add salt before mixing the salad?
It’s better to add salt after tossing with dressing and resting. Salt draws out moisture, so adding it too early can make the salad watery. Adjust seasoning after chilling for best balance.
Can I add other herbs to this salad?
Yes! Fresh mint or parsley work well as additions. Add them just before chilling to keep their color and flavor fresh.
Can I double the recipe for a larger group?
Definitely. Just be sure to mix the dressing and vegetables thoroughly so all slices are evenly coated. Also, use a large enough container to allow proper tossing.
