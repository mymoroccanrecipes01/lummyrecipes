Bright and Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread with Cake Mix Twist

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 2, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is a quick, moist treat made with cake mix for extra tenderness. Fresh zucchini and lemon zest add a bright, fresh flavor. It's perfect for breakfast or an afternoon pick-me-up!

Introduction

I stumbled on this lemon zucchini bread recipe when I was craving something fresh but easy to bake on a busy weekend morning. Using a white cake mix as the base totally simplifies the process and guarantees a moist, tender crumb every time. The fresh zucchini adds subtle moisture without making the bread heavy, and the lemon zest really brightens the flavor in such a refreshing way. I’ve tried a few zucchini bread recipes before but this one stands out because it’s quick, foolproof, and tastes like a little burst of sunshine in every bite. The trick I learned is to squeeze out the zucchini well to avoid sogginess and to fold the batter gently to keep it light and airy. When it comes out of the oven, the bread has a lovely golden crust with a soft, delicate crumb that's perfect for breakfast or an afternoon snack with tea. It’s become a family favorite that I keep coming back to whenever zucchini is in season.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 1 box white cake mix (about 15.25 oz)
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini), squeezed dry
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/3 cup vegetable oil
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or nonstick spray. This prep ensures your bread won't stick and bakes evenly. It’s important to preheat fully so the bread rises properly and the crust forms nicely. Avoid skipping this step because baking in an unheated oven can lead to dense texture and uneven cooking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated and loaf pan evenly greased with no dry spots
Step 2
Grate your zucchini finely and then place it in a clean kitchen towel. Squeeze out as much moisture as possible. This step prevents your bread from becoming soggy by removing excess water. Don’t skip this or the batter will be too wet, which affects texture and baking time.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but not dripping; about half the original moisture pressed out
Step 3
In a large bowl, combine the cake mix, baking powder, and salt. Stir these dry ingredients together to ensure even distribution of leavening agents. This helps the bread rise uniformly. Mixing them well avoids pockets of baking powder that can cause uneven texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully mixed with no visible clumps
Step 4
In a separate bowl, whisk together the eggs, vegetable oil, lemon zest, lemon juice, and vanilla extract until smooth. This wet mixture adds moisture and flavor. Whisking well helps emulsify the oil and lemon juice, creating a homogenous mixture that blends easily with the dry ingredients.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Wet ingredients are fully combined and slightly frothy
Step 5
Pour the wet ingredients into the dry mix and stir gently with a spatula until just combined. Overmixing can develop gluten in the cake mix flour and make the bread tough. The batter should be thick and slightly lumpy. Fold in the grated zucchini carefully to distribute it evenly without deflating the batter.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform in color with no large flour pockets; zucchini is evenly dispersed
Step 6
Transfer the batter into your prepared loaf pan and spread it evenly. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles. This step helps ensure an even crumb and prevents large holes from forming in the bread.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter surface is smooth and level in the pan
Step 7
Bake the bread in the preheated oven for 30 to 35 minutes. Start checking at 30 minutes by inserting a toothpick into the center. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, it’s done. Baking too long will dry the bread, so watch closely after 30 minutes.
⏱ Time: 30-35 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust with toothpick coming out mostly clean
Step 8
Remove the bread from the oven and let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes. This resting time allows the bread to set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking. Avoid removing it too early or the bread might crumble.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm to the touch and slightly pulls away from pan edges
Step 9
After cooling slightly, transfer the bread to a wire rack to cool completely. This prevents condensation from making the crust soggy. Cooling fully also improves slicing and flavor development. Patience here really pays off!
⏱ Time: At least 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is cool to the touch and the crust remains crisp

Pro Tips

Squeeze zucchini thoroughly to remove excess water before adding to batter.
Zucchini contains a lot of moisture that can make the bread soggy or dense if not drained properly. Removing excess water ensures the bread maintains a tender but firm crumb and bakes evenly without collapsing.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice for the best bright flavor.
Fresh lemon zest contains essential oils that provide a vibrant aroma and taste, which dried zest can't replicate. The juice adds acidity that balances sweetness and activates the baking powder, aiding rise and flavor complexity.
Don’t overmix the batter once wet and dry ingredients are combined.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, leading to a tough, chewy texture rather than a soft crumb. Gently folding ingredients preserves air bubbles that make the bread light and tender.
Let the bread cool completely on a wire rack before slicing.
Cooling allows the steam to escape, preventing the crust from becoming soggy. It also helps the bread set fully so slices hold together better without crumbling.
Use a toothpick to check doneness starting at 30 minutes of baking.
Checking early prevents overbaking, which dries out the bread. The toothpick should come out with a few moist crumbs, indicating perfect moisture balance.
Grease your loaf pan thoroughly or use parchment paper.
Proper greasing prevents sticking and makes it easier to remove the bread intact. Parchment paper can also make lifting the loaf out simpler without damage.
Add a handful of chopped nuts or a sprinkle of sugar on top for texture.
Nuts add crunch and flavor contrast, while a sugar topping caramelizes to create a delicate crust that enhances the eating experience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not draining zucchini enough

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added straight to the batter without squeezing, thinking the moisture helps keep the bread soft.

Fix Always wrap grated zucchini in a clean towel and press or squeeze firmly to remove excess water. This prevents the batter from becoming too wet and ensures the bread bakes to a tender but not soggy texture.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Some cooks think mixing more will make the batter smoother and the bread better, unaware it activates gluten development.

Fix Mix wet and dry ingredients until just combined, then fold in zucchini gently. Stop as soon as no dry flour is visible to keep the bread tender.

Skipping preheating the oven

Why it happens In a rush, preheating is sometimes overlooked, causing bread to bake unevenly or rise poorly.

Fix Always preheat the oven fully to 350°F before baking. This ensures the bread rises properly and develops a golden crust.

Using bottled lemon juice or dried zest

Why it happens Convenience leads some to use bottled juice or dried zest, which lack fresh citrus oils.

Fix Use fresh lemon zest and juice for the best flavor and aroma. The oils in fresh zest brighten the bread and enhance the lemon character.

Baking too long

Why it happens Leaving the bread in the oven too long dries it out, especially since cake mix breads can overbake quickly.

Fix Start checking at 30 minutes with a toothpick. Remove the bread as soon as it comes out mostly clean with a few moist crumbs.

Variations

Lemon Poppy Seed Zucchini Bread

Add 2 tablespoons of poppy seeds to the batter for a delightful crunch and subtle nuttiness that pairs beautifully with lemon. This variation adds texture and visual appeal without changing the moist crumb.

Note: Fold poppy seeds in with the zucchini at the end to evenly distribute. Baking time remains the same.

Gluten-Free Lemon Zucchini Bread

Use a gluten-free white cake mix and ensure the baking powder is gluten-free. This swap keeps the bread tender and moist while accommodating dietary needs. Expect a slightly different crumb texture but equally delicious flavor.

Note: Mix gluten-free ingredients gently and avoid overmixing, as gluten-free batters can become gummy if overworked.

Lemon Blueberry Zucchini Bread

Fold in 1 cup fresh or frozen blueberries to the batter for bursts of juicy sweetness. Blueberries complement the lemon and zucchini nicely and add a lovely color contrast.

Note: If using frozen berries, toss them in a little flour before folding in to prevent sinking. Baking time may increase by 2-3 minutes.

Vegan Lemon Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a dairy-free cake mix. The lemon and zucchini keep the bread moist and flavorful even without eggs.

Note: Allow flax eggs to thicken for 5 minutes before mixing. The bread may be denser but still tender.

Lemon Zucchini Bread with Cream Cheese Glaze

Top the cooled loaf with a simple cream cheese glaze made from cream cheese, powdered sugar, and lemon juice. This adds a tangy sweet finish perfect for special occasions or brunch.

Note: Make sure the bread is completely cool before glazing to prevent melting.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the bread covered loosely with a clean kitchen towel or in a bread box for up to 2 days. This keeps the crust from becoming too soft while maintaining moisture inside.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and the crust will soften; reheat briefly in the oven or toaster oven to refresh the texture.

Freezer

Wrap the bread tightly in plastic wrap and then in aluminum foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat slices gently to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven or regular oven at 325°F for 5-8 minutes. Avoid microwaving as it can make the bread rubbery. Adding a light brush of butter before warming enhances flavor and moisture.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini a day ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix the batter and bake the bread on the day you plan to serve for the freshest taste and texture.

FAQ

Can I use yellow squash instead of zucchini?
Yes, yellow squash works well as a substitute and has a similar moisture content and mild flavor. Just grate and squeeze it the same way you would zucchini to avoid soggy bread.
Why is my bread dense or gummy?
This usually happens if the zucchini wasn’t drained well or if the batter was overmixed. Make sure to squeeze out excess moisture and mix until just combined to keep the bread light.
Can I make this bread dairy-free?
Absolutely! The recipe uses vegetable oil and cake mix, which is often dairy-free, but check the cake mix label. Substitute with dairy-free cake mix if needed and use plant-based milk if you add any liquids.
How do I know when the bread is done?
Insert a toothpick into the center after 30 minutes of baking. If it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the bread is ready. If the toothpick is wet with batter, bake a few minutes longer.
Can I freeze this bread?
Yes, wrap it tightly in plastic and foil for up to 3 months. Thaw before serving and warm slices in the oven for best texture.
Can I use homemade cake mix or scratch flour instead?
You can, but the texture may differ. Cake mix has a specific balance of sugar and leavening that makes this bread tender. If using scratch ingredients, adjust sugar and baking powder accordingly.
What if I don’t have fresh lemons?
Fresh lemon zest and juice are best, but in a pinch, bottled lemon juice can be used though the flavor won’t be as bright. Avoid dried zest as it lacks essential oils.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Yes! Fold in 1/2 cup chopped nuts or chocolate chips with the zucchini for extra texture and flavor. This won’t affect baking time much.
