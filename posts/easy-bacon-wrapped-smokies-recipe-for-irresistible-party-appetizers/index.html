Sweet and Smoky Bacon Wrapped Smokies Perfect for Game Day Snacks

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 14, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These bacon wrapped smokies are the ultimate finger food—smoky, juicy, and glazed with brown sugar for a sweet-savory kick. I love making them for casual hangouts or game nights when everyone’s craving something easy and tasty!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon bacon wrapped smokies at a friend's party years ago, and they've been a staple in my snack repertoire ever since. What makes this version stand out is the brown sugar glaze that adds a subtle sweetness balancing the smoky bacon and savory sausages. I’ve tried just wrapping and baking them plain, but glazing them halfway through cooking brings the flavor to a whole new level. Plus, the touch of Dijon mustard in the glaze adds a nice depth without overpowering. The trick I learned is to secure the bacon tightly with toothpicks and bake on a wire rack so the bacon crisps evenly all around. When they come out, they’re perfectly caramelized and juicy inside. They look simple but taste like you spent hours prepping. These are my go-to for casual get-togethers or game days because everyone loves finger foods that are both sweet and savory. Trust me, once you try this glaze, you’ll never go back to plain smokies.

What You'll Need

Approximately 24 pieces, serves 4 as appetizer

  • 24 mini smoked sausages (cocktail smokies), thawed if frozen
  • 12 slices thick-cut bacon, cut in half
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1 tablespoon Dijon mustard
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • Toothpicks for securing

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Line a baking sheet with foil for easy cleanup and place a wire rack on top. The wire rack lets air circulate under the smokies so the bacon crisps up evenly instead of steaming. This step is key to getting that perfect bacon texture that’s crispy without being burnt. Avoid skipping the rack or placing the smokies directly on the foil, as the bacon will cook unevenly and could get soggy.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven preheated and rack positioned properly with foil lining the tray
Step 2
Wrap each mini sausage with half a slice of bacon, starting at one end and spiraling it around snugly. Secure with a toothpick through the middle to hold the bacon tight during baking. Wrapping tightly ensures the bacon crisps nicely and the smokie stays juicy inside. If the bacon is loose, it can shrink away from the sausage and leave chewy spots. Try not to overlap the bacon too much or it won’t cook evenly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: All smokies are evenly wrapped and secured with toothpicks, ready for the tray
Step 3
Arrange the wrapped smokies on the wire rack spaced apart so heat circulates around each piece. This prevents them from sticking together and helps bacon crisp on all sides. If they’re too crowded, steam will build up and the bacon won’t get that golden brown color. Make sure none are touching.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smokies neatly placed on rack with space between each piece
Step 4
In a small bowl, mix together the brown sugar, Dijon mustard, and smoked paprika. This glaze is what makes these smokies special. The brown sugar caramelizes, the mustard adds tang, and paprika gives a subtle smoky warmth. Stir until smooth and set aside.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Glaze is smooth and well combined without lumps
Step 5
Bake the smokies uncovered for 15 minutes. This initial baking cooks the bacon partway so it can crisp up nicely. Don’t glaze them yet—if you do, the sugar might burn since it’s exposed early. Watch for the bacon to start looking firm and slightly golden, but not fully crisp.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bacon looks set and starting to brown, not raw or pale
Step 6
Remove the tray carefully from the oven and brush each smokie generously with the brown sugar glaze. Make sure to coat all sides. This step adds that beautiful sticky sweet layer that caramelizes further. If you rush or skip glazing, you miss the signature flavor and texture contrast that makes these addictive.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each smokie is evenly coated with a shiny glaze
Step 7
Return the tray to the oven and bake for another 10-15 minutes until the glaze is bubbly and caramelized and the bacon is crisp. Keep an eye to prevent burning, as sugar can brown quickly toward the end. The smokies should be sizzling and have a deep golden color when done.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Glaze bubbling, bacon crispy, and smokies fully cooked inside
Step 8
Once cooked, remove from oven and let the smokies rest for 5 minutes on the rack. This helps the glaze set so it’s sticky but not runny. It also cools them just enough to handle without losing juiciness inside. Resist the urge to eat immediately—they’re better after resting.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Glaze looks set and tacky, smokies slightly cooled but warm
Step 9
Serve warm on a platter with extra toothpicks for easy grabbing. These smokies pair great with a tangy dipping sauce like spicy mustard or ranch if you want a little extra kick. They’re perfect finger food for parties or casual snacks that impress without fuss.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smokies arranged and ready for serving

Pro Tips

Use a wire rack on the baking sheet for even cooking and crispy bacon.
The wire rack elevates the smokies, allowing hot air to circulate underneath which crisps the bacon evenly. Without it, bacon cooks unevenly and can become soggy from sitting in its fat. This simple step transforms the texture and prevents greasy spots.
Wrap bacon tightly and secure with toothpicks to prevent unraveling.
Bacon shrinks as it cooks, so wrapping tightly ensures it stays snug around the smokie. Toothpicks keep everything in place, preventing bacon from sliding off and ensuring even cooking. Loose bacon can lead to uneven texture and exposed sausage.
Apply the brown sugar glaze halfway through baking, not before.
If you glaze too early, the sugar may burn due to prolonged heat exposure. Baking first allows the bacon to set and partially cook, then glazing adds caramelization without burning. This timing protects flavor and appearance.
Let the smokies rest a few minutes after baking before serving.
Resting lets the glaze set, becoming sticky but not runny, which improves handling and presentation. It also helps redistribute juices inside, keeping the smokies moist and flavorful.
Use thick-cut bacon for the best crispiness and flavor balance.
Thick-cut bacon renders fat more slowly, crisping up without burning quickly. Thin bacon cooks too fast and can dry out, while thick slices add a satisfying chew and robust flavor that complements the smokies.
Space the smokies apart on the rack to avoid steaming.
Crowding causes moisture to build up, leading to soggy bacon instead of crisp. Proper spacing ensures hot air circulates freely, promoting even cooking and a perfect crispy finish.
Use a mix of sweet and smoked spices in the glaze for complexity.
Combining brown sugar with smoked paprika and mustard balances sweet, smoky, and tangy flavors. This layered seasoning profile makes the smokies more interesting and delicious than a plain sugar glaze.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Wrapping bacon too loosely around the smokies

Why it happens Many assume bacon will shrink tightly during cooking, but if it’s loose initially, it can unravel or cook unevenly.

Fix Make sure to wrap the bacon snugly and secure with a toothpick. This keeps the bacon in place and ensures an even crisp texture all around.

Skipping the wire rack and baking smokies directly on foil

Why it happens It's easier to just place them on foil, but this traps fat and moisture underneath, causing soggy bacon.

Fix Always use a wire rack over a foil-lined tray to let air circulate and fat drip away for crispy bacon.

Applying glaze too early in the cooking process

Why it happens Some think glazing from the start saves time, but sugar burns quickly under high heat.

Fix Bake smokies plain for the first 15 minutes, then brush on glaze and finish baking to get perfect caramelization without burning.

Overcrowding the baking rack

Why it happens Trying to fit all smokies on a small rack can seem efficient but causes steaming instead of crisping.

Fix Give each smokie enough space to breathe—spread them out in a single layer for best results.

Using low-quality or very thin bacon

Why it happens Thin bacon cooks too fast and can become brittle or dry, missing the chewy balance.

Fix Choose thick-cut bacon with a good fat-to-meat ratio for optimal texture and flavor.

Variations

Spicy Sriracha Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Add a teaspoon of sriracha to the brown sugar glaze for a spicy kick that balances sweetness and smokiness. These are perfect for those who love a little heat in their snacks. Expect a slightly sticky, fiery glaze with an added layer of complexity.

Note: Reduce sugar slightly to prevent burning due to the added moisture from sriracha. Watch the glaze closely near the end of baking.

Maple Glazed Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Swap brown sugar for pure maple syrup in the glaze for a richer, woodsy sweetness. This variation brings a mellow, natural sweetness that pairs beautifully with the smoky bacon and savory sausages.

Note: Because maple syrup is more liquid, apply glaze in thinner layers to avoid sogginess and bake a bit longer for caramelization.

Garlic Herb Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Mix minced garlic and chopped fresh herbs like rosemary and thyme into the glaze for an aromatic twist. This adds a savory depth that complements the smoky bacon and juicy smokies, great for herb lovers.

Note: Fresh herbs can burn, so add garlic and herbs to the glaze right before brushing and keep baking time shorter after glazing.

Cheesy Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Before wrapping, insert a small cube of cheddar or pepper jack cheese inside each smokie for a gooey surprise. The melted cheese adds richness and a creamy texture contrast to the crispy bacon.

Note: Seal the smokies well to avoid cheese leakage. Bake slightly longer at a lower temp if needed to melt cheese without burning bacon.

Honey Mustard Bacon Wrapped Smokies

Combine honey with Dijon mustard in the glaze for a sweeter, tangier flavor profile. This variation is perfect if you prefer a milder sweetness and a silkier glaze finish.

Note: Honey browns faster than sugar, so monitor closely during the final baking stage to prevent burning.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Bacon wrapped smokies can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond this, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The glaze may harden and bacon lose some crispness, so reheat gently to restore texture. They can be eaten cold but are best warmed.

Freezer

Freeze cooked smokies in a single layer on a baking sheet first, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat in a preheated oven at 350°F (175°C) for 10-12 minutes to crisp bacon and warm through. Avoid microwaving as it softens bacon. Adding a splash of water to the tray can help keep smokies moist during reheating.

Make Ahead

Prepare the smokies wrapped and secured on the wire rack a day ahead, cover and refrigerate. Apply glaze and bake fresh on serving day for best texture and flavor.

FAQ

Can I use regular hot dogs instead of mini smoked sausages?
You can, but hot dogs are larger and have a different texture, so cooking times will increase and the bacon-to-sausage ratio changes. Mini smoked sausages are preferred for bite-sized portions and better flavor balance.
How do I prevent the bacon from burning while cooking?
Using thick-cut bacon and baking at a moderate temperature (375°F) helps. Also, apply the brown sugar glaze halfway through cooking rather than at the start to avoid burning the sugar.
Can I make these smokies ahead of time and reheat?
Yes! You can assemble and store them wrapped but uncooked in the fridge up to 24 hours. Bake and glaze fresh before serving. Leftovers reheat well in the oven to restore crispiness.
What if I don’t have smoked paprika?
You can substitute with regular paprika, but you’ll lose some smoky depth. Adding a few drops of liquid smoke to the glaze can help replicate that flavor.
Is there a vegetarian alternative for this recipe?
You could try vegetarian sausages wrapped in plant-based bacon alternatives, but cooking times and textures will vary. The glaze works well on both, so experiment with your favorite brands.
How long can I leave the cooked smokies out during a party?
It’s safe to leave them out for about 2 hours covered. After that, refrigeration is recommended to avoid food safety issues.
Can I grill these instead of baking?
Grilling works great for a smoky flavor but watch closely to avoid flare-ups from bacon fat. Use indirect heat and a grill basket to keep them from falling through.
What dipping sauces pair well with bacon wrapped smokies?
Spicy mustard, ranch, barbecue sauce, or even a honey mustard mix are all fantastic. Choose based on whether you want tangy, creamy, or sweet complements.
