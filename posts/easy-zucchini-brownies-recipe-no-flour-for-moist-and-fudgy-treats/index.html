Fudgy Flourless Zucchini Brownies That Actually Taste Amazing

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 29, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These zucchini brownies are moist and fudgy without any flour. The zucchini sneaks in moisture without a veggie flavor. Great for a healthier treat any time you want some chocolatey goodness!

Introduction

I stumbled upon zucchini brownies a few years ago when I wanted to sneak some veggies into a dessert without anyone noticing. The first attempt was a total flop — they came out dry and crumbly. But after some trial and error, I figured out that squeezing out the zucchini moisture just right and balancing it with enough fat and cocoa powder made all the difference. What I love about this recipe is how the zucchini adds natural moisture that keeps the brownies super fudgy without any veggie taste at all. Plus, there’s no flour, so it feels a bit lighter but still totally indulgent. I usually bake these when I want a healthier dessert that still satisfies my chocolate cravings or when I want to bring something a little unexpected to a potluck. The texture is dense and fudgy, with just enough richness to feel like a proper brownie, but the zucchini keeps them from being too heavy or dry. I think you’ll be surprised how much you enjoy these — they might just become your new go-to treat!

What You'll Need

8 rich, fudgy brownies

  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini), squeezed dry
  • 3/4 cup cocoa powder, unsweetened
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1/2 cup melted coconut oil or butter
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup dark chocolate chips (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating your zucchini finely, then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much water as possible. This step is crucial because zucchini holds a lot of moisture, and if you don’t squeeze it well, your brownies will be soggy and won’t set properly. You want the zucchini moist but not dripping wet. This moisture helps keep the brownies fudgy but too much will ruin the texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but no water drips when you squeeze it firmly
Step 2
Preheat the oven to 350°F (175°C). In a large bowl, whisk together the eggs and sugar until combined and slightly thickened. This aerates the eggs a bit, helping the brownies have a nice structure despite no flour. Then stir in the melted coconut oil (or butter) and vanilla extract. Mixing these wet ingredients thoroughly ensures an even texture and rich flavor throughout the batter.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, shiny, and slightly thickened
Step 3
In a separate bowl, sift together the cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt. Sifting helps avoid lumps and ensures even distribution of the baking powder and salt, which are important for texture and flavor balance. Slowly add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, folding gently to combine. Overmixing can make brownies tough, so mix just until you see no dry streaks.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform and thick but not overworked
Step 4
Fold the squeezed zucchini into the batter carefully. The zucchini will add moisture and texture without a veggie taste. If you’re using chocolate chips, fold them in now. Adding chips at this stage gives pockets of melted chocolate that add richness and texture. Don’t overmix; just fold to evenly distribute.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini and chips are evenly mixed without overworking the batter
Step 5
Line an 8x8 inch baking pan with parchment paper or grease it well. Pour the batter into the pan and spread evenly with a spatula. This ensures even baking and a nice crust on top. The batter will be thick but spreadable. Smooth the surface to help the brownies bake uniformly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread with a smooth top
Step 6
Bake in the preheated oven for 25-30 minutes. Start checking at 25 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center. You want it to come out with a few moist crumbs but not wet batter. Overbaking will dry the brownies out, so watch closely near the end. The edges should look set and start pulling away slightly from the pan.
⏱ Time: 25-30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out with moist crumbs, edges slightly pulling away
Step 7
Remove brownies from oven and let them cool completely in the pan on a wire rack. Cooling is key because the brownies will firm up as they cool, making them easier to cut and enhancing their fudgy texture. Don’t try to cut them warm or they’ll crumble and fall apart.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Brownies are firm to the touch and easily sliced
Step 8
Once cooled, use the parchment edges to lift the brownies out of the pan. Cut into 8 squares using a sharp knife, wiping the blade clean between cuts for neat edges. Serving them slightly chilled or at room temperature works best to highlight the fudgy texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Squares have clean edges and hold together well
Step 9
Store leftover brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days or refrigerate for longer freshness. They keep their fudgy texture best when not left out too long. Reheat briefly if you prefer them warm, but they’re great cold too!
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Brownies remain moist and fudgy after storage

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the zucchini thoroughly before adding it to the batter.
Zucchini contains a lot of water that can make the brownies soggy or prevent them from setting properly. Removing excess moisture ensures the brownies remain fudgy but firm, preserving the ideal texture.
Use room temperature eggs for better mixing and texture.
Room temperature eggs emulsify more easily with fats and sugars, resulting in a smoother batter and a more tender crumb. Cold eggs can cause the batter to seize or mix unevenly.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the dry ingredients.
Overmixing can develop gluten from any residual flour or cause the batter to become dense and tough. Folding gently just until combined preserves the tender, fudgy texture.
Line your baking pan with parchment paper for easy removal.
Parchment prevents sticking and allows you to lift the entire brownie slab out for clean cutting. This avoids breaking or crumbling that often happens when cutting directly in the pan.
Check the brownies a few minutes before the minimum baking time.
Ovens vary in heat, and overbaking dries out the brownies. A toothpick with moist crumbs ensures they are perfectly fudgy and not overcooked.
Let the brownies cool completely before slicing.
Cooling allows the brownies to set and firm up, giving clean cuts and the right texture. Cutting warm brownies can cause crumbling and uneven slices.
Add chocolate chips for extra bursts of melted chocolate.
Chocolate chips melt during baking, creating pockets of gooey richness that enhance the overall flavor and texture, making the brownies more indulgent.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out enough zucchini moisture

Why it happens People often underestimate how much water zucchini holds and add it straight to the batter, thinking it will bake out.

Fix Use a clean towel or cheesecloth to press and squeeze the grated zucchini firmly until no water drips out. This prevents soggy, underbaked brownies.

Overbaking the brownies

Why it happens It’s tempting to bake longer to be sure they’re done, but this dries them out.

Fix Start checking with a toothpick 5 minutes before the timer ends. Remove when it comes out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

Overmixing the batter after adding dry ingredients

Why it happens Trying to get a perfectly smooth batter can lead to overmixing.

Fix Fold ingredients gently just until combined. This keeps the brownies tender and fudgy.

Using too much or too little fat

Why it happens Fat is essential for moisture and texture; some skip or reduce it to be healthier.

Fix Stick to the recipe’s fat amount. Using less will make brownies dry and crumbly; too much can make them greasy.

Cutting brownies before they fully cool

Why it happens Impatience or wanting to serve right away.

Fix Allow at least 30 minutes of cooling so the brownies set properly and slice cleanly without crumbling.

Variations

Nutty Zucchini Brownies

Add 1/2 cup chopped walnuts or pecans to the batter for a crunchy texture and nutty flavor that pairs beautifully with the chocolate and zucchini. The nuts add a satisfying contrast to the fudgy brownies and a bit of healthy fat.

Note: Fold nuts in gently with the zucchini and chocolate chips. Baking time remains the same, but check for doneness as nuts can affect heat distribution slightly.

Spiced Zucchini Brownies

Mix in 1 teaspoon cinnamon and a pinch of cayenne pepper for a warm, spicy twist. This adds depth of flavor and a subtle kick that complements the chocolate perfectly, making the brownies feel cozy and unique.

Note: Add spices to the dry ingredients before combining. No changes to baking time or temperature needed.

Vegan Zucchini Brownies

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use coconut oil or vegan butter. These brownies stay fudgy and moist but are entirely plant-based, perfect for vegan diets.

Note: Mix flax eggs and let them gel before adding. Baking time may need an extra 2-3 minutes; check doneness carefully.

Peanut Butter Swirl Zucchini Brownies

Drop spoonfuls of peanut butter on top of the batter and swirl gently with a knife before baking. This adds a creamy, salty contrast to the rich chocolate and moist zucchini base, elevating the flavor profile.

Note: Swirl peanut butter carefully to avoid overmixing. Bake as usual and check with a toothpick.

Chocolate Zucchini Brownie Muffins

Spoon the batter into a lined muffin tin for individual portions. Baking time reduces to about 18-20 minutes, perfect for grab-and-go snacks or lunchbox treats with the same fudgy texture.

Note: Fill muffin cups 3/4 full and check doneness earlier to avoid overbaking. Texture is slightly lighter due to smaller size.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store brownies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days. Keep them covered to prevent drying out. They maintain their fudgy texture best this way, making them perfect for quick snacks.

Refrigerator

Brownies can be refrigerated for up to 7 days in an airtight container. The cooler temperature firms them up, which some prefer. Let them come to room temperature or warm gently before serving for a softer texture.

Freezer

Freeze brownies tightly wrapped in plastic wrap and placed in a freezer bag for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave overnight in the fridge or at room temperature for a few hours. Reheat gently to restore softness.

Reheating

To reheat, warm brownies in the microwave for 15-20 seconds or in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes. Add a small splash of milk or water if they seem dry to restore moisture. Avoid overheating as it can dry them out.

Make Ahead

You can grate and squeeze the zucchini 1-2 days ahead and store it in the fridge. Mix the batter and bake on the day you want to serve for the freshest brownies. Baked brownies also freeze well if made ahead.

FAQ

Can I substitute zucchini with another vegetable?
Zucchini works best due to its mild flavor and high moisture. You can try grated carrots or beetroot, but these will change the taste and color. Always squeeze out excess moisture to avoid soggy brownies.
Why are my brownies too dry?
Dry brownies usually mean too much moisture was squeezed out or they were overbaked. Make sure to leave some zucchini moisture in the batter and check for doneness early to keep them fudgy.
Can I use gluten-free flour instead of no flour?
Yes, adding gluten-free flour will change the texture, making it less fudgy and more cakey. This recipe is designed for flourless brownies, so flour will alter the final result.
How do I store leftover brownies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 3 days, or refrigerate for up to a week. For longer storage, freeze tightly wrapped for up to 3 months.
Can I make this recipe vegan?
Yes, replace eggs with flax eggs and use vegan butter or coconut oil. The texture will be slightly different but still moist and delicious.
Why do my brownies crack on top?
Cracks are normal and often a sign of a fudgy interior with a slightly crisp crust. Avoid overmixing and overbaking to minimize cracking.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips?
Absolutely! Nuts add crunch and chocolate chips add melty bursts of chocolate. Fold them in gently before baking.
What’s the best way to cut brownies cleanly?
Let brownies cool completely and use a sharp knife wiped clean between cuts. You can warm the knife slightly for smoother edges.
