Cozy Creamy Cheesy Tomato Soup for Chilly Evenings

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 3, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This cheesy tomato soup is creamy and comforting without feeling too heavy. The melted cheese adds a cozy touch that’s perfect for cold nights. I love making it when I want something simple yet satisfying.

Introduction

I first made this cheesy tomato soup on a cold winter evening when I wanted something comforting but not too heavy. The idea came from my love of classic tomato soup but with a twist—melting in sharp cheddar cheese for a cozy richness that feels like a warm hug. What sets this version apart is how creamy it is without relying on tons of cream, thanks to a gentle simmer and the natural sweetness of the tomatoes balanced with a little sugar. I also love sautéing the onions and garlic just right to deepen the flavor base before adding the tomatoes. One trick I learned along the way is to add the cheese off the heat at the end, so it melts perfectly without becoming grainy. The final soup is velvety with a bright tomato flavor and just enough cheese to make it indulgent but still light. It’s become my go-to for chilly nights when I want something quick, simple, and deeply satisfying. Plus, it pairs beautifully with crusty bread or a grilled cheese sandwich for dipping. I hope you enjoy making it as much as I do!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 can (28 ounces) crushed tomatoes
  • 2 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried basil
  • 1/2 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1 cup shredded sharp cheddar cheese

How To Do It

Step 1
Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the finely chopped onion and cook gently for about 5-7 minutes until soft and translucent but not browned. This slow cooking releases the onion’s natural sweetness and builds a flavorful base for the soup. Stir frequently to avoid any burning, as burnt onions can make the soup bitter.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Onions should be soft, shiny, and slightly golden at the edges
Step 2
Add the minced garlic to the pot and cook for another 1-2 minutes until fragrant. Garlic cooks quickly and can burn easily, so keep stirring and watch for the aroma to fill your kitchen. This step adds a warm, savory layer that complements the tomatoes beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Garlic should be golden but not browned, releasing a fragrant smell
Step 3
Pour in the crushed tomatoes along with their juices, then add the vegetable broth. Stir gently to combine all the ingredients evenly. The broth helps thin the soup to the perfect consistency and balances the acidity of the tomatoes. Avoid rushing this step to ensure flavors meld well.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Mixture should be smooth and uniform with no dry tomato chunks
Step 4
Add the sugar, dried basil, and oregano. These herbs add an earthy note that complements the tomato’s brightness, while the sugar cuts through acidity to round out the flavor. Season with salt and pepper to taste but remember you can adjust later once the soup is finished.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Herbs and seasoning evenly distributed, soup beginning to smell aromatic
Step 5
Bring the soup to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat. Let it cook uncovered for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally. Simmering allows the flavors to deepen and the soup to thicken slightly. Keep the heat moderate to prevent the soup from burning on the bottom.
⏱ Time: 20
✓ Doneness: Soup should be slightly thickened with a rich, concentrated aroma
Step 6
Remove the pot from heat and stir in the heavy cream. Adding cream off the heat prevents curdling and keeps the soup silky smooth. Stir gently until the cream is fully incorporated, giving the soup a luscious texture without overpowering the tomato flavor.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Soup appears creamy and evenly blended without separation
Step 7
Add the shredded cheddar cheese gradually, stirring constantly until melted and fully blended. The cheese adds a rich, gooey element that makes the soup feel indulgent and comforting. Be patient—melting cheese slowly off the heat ensures it stays smooth rather than clumpy.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Cheese is melted, soup has a velvety texture and rich color
Step 8
Taste the soup and adjust seasoning with more salt, pepper, or herbs if needed. This final tweak is important to balance all the flavors perfectly. Remember that cheese adds saltiness, so add seasoning gradually.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Balanced flavor with no one taste overpowering the others
Step 9
Serve the soup hot, garnished with a sprinkle of extra cheese or fresh basil if desired. Pair it with crusty bread or a grilled cheese sandwich for a full comforting meal. Enjoy immediately for best flavor and texture.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Soup steaming hot and visually creamy with melted cheese topping

Pro Tips

Sauté onions slowly over medium heat until translucent and soft.
Slow cooking releases natural sugars in onions, creating a sweet, flavorful base. If you rush or use high heat, onions can brown or burn, adding bitterness that spoils the soup’s delicate balance.
Add the cream only after removing the pot from heat.
Adding cream off the heat prevents curdling and keeps the soup smooth and silky. Cream is sensitive to high temperatures, so this step preserves its texture and richness.
Use freshly shredded cheddar cheese instead of pre-shredded.
Pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking agents that can affect melting quality, causing clumps or graininess. Freshly shredded cheese melts evenly, creating a smooth, creamy soup.
Simmer the soup gently, avoiding a rolling boil.
A gentle simmer allows flavors to meld and the soup to thicken without breaking the cream or causing separation. Boiling can toughen proteins and lead to a grainy texture.
Taste and adjust seasoning at the end after adding cheese.
Cheese adds saltiness, so seasoning before adding cheese can lead to over-salted soup. Final adjustments ensure balanced flavor.
Use high-quality canned crushed tomatoes for best flavor.
Good tomatoes provide a fresh, vibrant base. Lower quality cans may be too acidic or bland, requiring more sugar or seasoning to fix.
Serve immediately for best texture and flavor.
Soup thickens and flavors intensify as it cools. Serving fresh preserves the creamy, velvety texture and bright tomato taste.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Burning the onions during sautéing

Why it happens Many cooks try to speed up the process by using high heat, which causes onions to brown or burn quickly, creating bitterness.

Fix Cook onions slowly over medium or medium-low heat, stirring often. Watch for translucency and softness without browning. Patience here is key for a sweet flavor base.

Adding cream while the soup is still boiling

Why it happens Some add cream directly to hot soup without removing from heat, causing it to curdle and separate.

Fix Remove the pot from heat before stirring in cream. Stir gently to combine and maintain a smooth texture.

Using pre-shredded cheese

Why it happens Pre-shredded cheese contains additives that prevent smooth melting, resulting in clumpy or grainy soup.

Fix Grate your own cheese fresh before adding. This ensures even melting and creamy texture.

Skipping seasoning adjustments after adding cheese

Why it happens Cheese adds salt, so seasoning before cheese can lead to oversalting.

Fix Always taste the soup after cheese is melted and then add salt or pepper as needed.

Boiling the soup vigorously during simmer

Why it happens High heat can break down the cream and cheese, causing separation and graininess.

Fix Maintain a gentle simmer and stir occasionally. Lower the heat if you see bubbling too vigorously.

Variations

Spicy Cheesy Tomato Soup

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes or a diced jalapeño while sautéing onions for a spicy kick. The heat complements the creamy cheese and bright tomato flavors, creating a cozy yet zesty soup. Perfect if you like a little fire!

Note: Adjust the amount of chili carefully; too much can overpower the soup’s balance. Simmer slightly longer to mellow spice.

Vegan Cheesy Tomato Soup

Swap heavy cream for coconut cream and use a plant-based shredded cheese alternative. This keeps the soup creamy and cheesy without dairy, great for vegans or lactose-intolerant folks. The coconut adds a subtle sweetness that pairs well with tomatoes.

Note: Choose a mild vegan cheese that melts well. Add cream substitute off heat to avoid separation.

Roasted Garlic Cheesy Tomato Soup

Roast whole garlic cloves until soft and sweet, then mash and add to the soup instead of raw garlic. This adds a mellow depth and richness, enhancing the soup’s cozy feel with a subtle caramelized flavor.

Note: Roast garlic for about 30-40 minutes at 400°F. Add after pureeing tomatoes for best infusion.

Herb-Infused Cheesy Tomato Soup

Add fresh thyme and rosemary sprigs during simmering, removing before serving. These herbs add an aromatic twist and deepen the flavor profile, making the soup feel more sophisticated and layered.

Note: Use fresh herbs sparingly as they are potent. Remove stems before serving to avoid bitterness.

Chunky Vegetable Cheesy Tomato Soup

Add diced carrots, celery, and bell peppers with onions for extra texture and nutrients. This variation turns the soup into a hearty meal with a nice crunch and fresh vegetable flavors alongside the creamy tomato base.

Note: Sauté vegetables longer to soften. Adjust broth quantity to maintain desired soup consistency.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This soup can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate promptly to keep it safe and fresh.

Refrigerator

Store the soup in an airtight container and refrigerate for up to 3 days. The soup may thicken as it cools; reheat gently and stir in a splash of broth or water to loosen if needed. Flavor may deepen but cheese texture remains creamy.

Freezer

Freeze the soup in freezer-safe containers for up to 2 months. Leave some headspace for expansion. To thaw, move to the fridge overnight and reheat gently on the stovetop, stirring frequently to maintain smooth texture.

Reheating

Reheat on low to medium heat on the stovetop, stirring often. Avoid boiling to prevent cheese separation. Add a little broth or cream if the soup is too thick. Microwave reheating works but stir halfway through to ensure even warming.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the soup base (onions, garlic, tomatoes, broth, and seasonings) 1-2 days ahead and refrigerate. Add cream and cheese just before serving to keep the texture fresh and creamy.

FAQ

Can I use fresh tomatoes instead of canned crushed tomatoes?
Yes, but fresh tomatoes require peeling and cooking down longer to achieve the right texture and flavor. Use ripe, flavorful tomatoes and simmer them until soft before blending or crushing. This adds time but gives a fresh taste.
What cheese can I substitute for sharp cheddar?
You can use Monterey Jack, Gruyère, or even mozzarella for a milder, creamier flavor. Keep in mind that sharper cheeses add more tang and depth, so mild cheeses may result in a less pronounced cheesy taste.
How can I make this soup thicker or thinner?
To thicken, simmer the soup longer uncovered to reduce liquid. To thin, add more broth or water gradually while stirring until desired consistency is reached.
Is it okay to blend the soup for a smoother texture?
Absolutely! For an ultra-smooth soup, use an immersion blender or regular blender after simmering. Just be careful when blending hot liquids—blend in batches and vent the lid slightly.
Can I prepare this soup in a slow cooker?
Yes, sauté onions and garlic first, then combine all ingredients except cream and cheese in the slow cooker. Cook on low for 4-6 hours. Add cream and cheese at the end off heat for best texture.
What if my soup is too acidic?
Add a small pinch of sugar or a splash of cream to balance acidity. Simmering longer can also mellow sharp flavors. Taste as you go to avoid over-sweetening.
Can I freeze the soup after adding cheese?
It’s better to freeze the soup before adding cheese, as freezing can change the cheese texture. Add cheese fresh when reheating for a creamier result.
How do I prevent the cheese from clumping when melted in the soup?
Add cheese slowly off the heat and stir gently to melt evenly. Avoid boiling the soup after cheese is added, as high heat causes separation and graininess.
