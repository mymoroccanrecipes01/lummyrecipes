Classic Creamy Cucumber and Onion Salad That Brightens Any Meal

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 4, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This salad is a refreshing mix of crisp cucumbers and sharp onions tossed in a tangy vinegar and creamy sour cream dressing. It's perfect for summer dinners or potlucks when you want something cool and light.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this creamy cucumber and onion salad years ago at a family barbecue, and it quickly became a staple in my summer recipe rotation. What I love about this version is how the crisp cucumbers soak up the tangy vinegar and smooth sour cream dressing perfectly, creating a refreshing balance of flavors. Unlike some heavy or overly sweet salads, this one feels light but still satisfying. The trick I learned is to slice the cucumbers and onions very thinly, so they absorb the dressing well and stay crisp without getting soggy. Also, letting it chill for at least 30 minutes before serving really helps the flavors meld beautifully. Every time I bring this salad to a potluck or family dinner, it disappears fast! It’s simple, cool, and a little creamy twist on a classic that feels like summer on a plate.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 2 large cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 2 tablespoons white vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Fresh dill (optional), chopped for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing the cucumbers using a sharp knife or mandoline. The thin slices allow the cucumbers to absorb the dressing better and keep the salad crisp. Be sure to slice evenly so the salad has a nice texture throughout. Avoid thick slices as they won’t soak up the flavors as well and can feel watery. If your cucumbers have thick skins or lots of seeds, you can peel or deseed them for a smoother texture.
⏱ Time: 10
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers are uniformly thin and flexible but still firm to the touch.
Step 2
Next, thinly slice the red onion. Thin slices prevent the onion’s sharpness from overpowering the salad. If you want a milder onion flavor, soak the slices in cold water for 5 minutes, then drain before adding to the salad. This step reduces pungency but keeps the crunch. Combine the cucumber and onion slices in a large mixing bowl for even distribution.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Onion slices are translucent and thin without being mushy.
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the sour cream, white vinegar, sugar, salt, and black pepper until smooth. The vinegar adds a bright tang that balances the creamy sour cream, while the sugar cuts through acidity for a well-rounded flavor. Make sure the sugar dissolves completely to avoid graininess in the dressing.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Dressing is smooth, slightly glossy, and evenly mixed.
Step 4
Pour the dressing over the cucumber and onion slices. Gently toss everything together to coat all the pieces evenly. Be careful not to mash the cucumbers—use a light hand to keep their texture intact. Proper coating ensures every bite is flavorful without being soggy.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: All cucumber and onion slices are evenly coated with dressing.
Step 5
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and refrigerate the salad for at least 30 minutes before serving. This resting time allows the cucumbers and onions to soak up the dressing, mellowing the onions’ bite and blending the flavors beautifully. Skipping this step results in a less flavorful and less cohesive salad.
⏱ Time: 30
✓ Doneness: Salad is chilled and flavors taste melded but cucumbers remain crisp.
Step 6
Before serving, give the salad a gentle stir to redistribute any dressing that may have settled. Taste and adjust seasoning with a pinch more salt or pepper if needed. If desired, sprinkle fresh chopped dill on top for a bright herbal note that complements the creamy tanginess.
⏱ Time: 2
✓ Doneness: Salad looks fresh with evenly distributed herbs and seasoning.
Step 7
Serve the salad chilled as a side dish alongside grilled meats, sandwiches, or as part of a potluck spread. It’s especially refreshing on warm days and pairs well with smoky or spicy flavors. Leftovers keep well and taste even better the next day.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Salad remains crisp and fresh when served cold.
Step 8
If you want to prepare ahead, slice and dress the cucumbers and onions but wait to add fresh herbs until just before serving to keep their flavor bright. You can also adjust the dressing thickness by adding a splash of milk or water if it thickens too much after chilling.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Dressing consistency is creamy but pourable after chilling.
Step 9
For a lighter version, substitute Greek yogurt for sour cream. This gives the salad a tangy, protein-rich boost without losing creaminess. Just whisk the yogurt with vinegar and sugar the same way. The flavor will be slightly different but equally delicious.
⏱ Time: 0
✓ Doneness: Dressing remains smooth and coats salad evenly.

Pro Tips

Use a mandoline slicer for perfectly thin cucumber and onion slices.
Even, thin slices absorb dressing better and ensure a consistent texture throughout the salad. Uneven slices can lead to some pieces being underdressed or soggy while others remain too crunchy.
Let the salad chill for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Resting time allows the flavors to meld and the cucumbers to soak up the dressing, softening the onions’ sharpness and creating a harmonious taste. Skipping this results in a less flavorful salad.
Add fresh herbs like dill just before serving.
Fresh herbs lose their vibrancy and flavor if mixed too early. Adding them last keeps their color bright and their aromatic oils intact, enhancing the salad’s freshness.
Soak onion slices in cold water to mellow their bite if desired.
Cold water reduces the sulfur compounds in onions that cause pungency without sacrificing crunch. This step is helpful if you prefer a milder onion flavor in your salad.
Use full-fat sour cream for the best creamy texture.
Full-fat versions provide richness and body to the dressing, preventing it from becoming watery or thin. Low-fat sour creams can separate or lack flavor.
Adjust sugar in the dressing to balance acidity to your taste.
Sugar cuts through vinegar’s sharpness, creating a balanced dressing. Too little sugar makes it too tart, too much makes it overly sweet. Taste and tweak accordingly.
Serve the salad chilled to keep cucumbers crisp and refreshing.
Warm temperatures cause cucumbers to soften and lose their refreshing crunch. Serving cold ensures the salad feels light and crisp, perfect for hot weather.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using thick cucumber slices that don’t absorb dressing well.

Why it happens Many assume thicker slices are easier to prepare, but they don’t soak up flavors and can make the salad watery.

Fix Always slice cucumbers thinly and evenly, ideally with a mandoline. This increases surface area for dressing absorption and keeps the texture crisp.

Skipping the chilling step and serving immediately.

Why it happens Impatience or time constraints lead cooks to serve right away, but the flavors haven’t melded.

Fix Plan ahead to refrigerate the salad for at least 30 minutes. This resting time is essential for flavor development and mellowing onions.

Using low-fat sour cream that makes dressing watery.

Why it happens Trying to make the dish lighter often leads to choosing low-fat products that lack the richness needed.

Fix Opt for full-fat sour cream or substitute with Greek yogurt for creaminess. Avoid low-fat versions for best texture.

Not balancing vinegar with sugar, resulting in overly tart salad.

Why it happens Some skip sugar thinking it’s unnecessary, but vinegar alone can be too sharp.

Fix Always add sugar to the dressing and adjust to taste, balancing acidity with sweetness for a harmonious flavor.

Adding fresh herbs too early causing them to wilt.

Why it happens Mixing herbs in before chilling exposes them to moisture and cold, making them limp.

Fix Add herbs like dill just before serving to keep them fresh and flavorful.

Variations

Greek Yogurt Version

Swap sour cream for Greek yogurt for a lighter, protein-packed dressing. The salad stays creamy but gains a slight tanginess unique to yogurt. This is perfect if you want a healthier twist without sacrificing flavor.

Note: Whisk yogurt with vinegar and sugar just like sour cream. Texture will be slightly thinner; chill longer if needed to thicken.

Add Fresh Herbs Mix

Mix fresh dill, parsley, and chives into the salad for an herbaceous boost. This adds layers of flavor and a vibrant green color, making the salad even more refreshing and fragrant.

Note: Add herbs just before serving to maintain their aroma and color.

Spicy Kick

Add a pinch of cayenne pepper or finely chopped jalapeño to the dressing for a subtle heat that contrasts nicely with the creamy tang. Great if you want to spice up a classic.

Note: Start with a small amount of spice and adjust after chilling to avoid overpowering.

Vinegar-Free Dressing

Replace vinegar with lemon juice for a fresher, citrusy tang. This variation brightens the salad and pairs beautifully with seafood or grilled chicken dishes.

Note: Use fresh lemon juice and balance with sugar to avoid harsh acidity.

Add Cherry Tomatoes

Toss in halved cherry tomatoes for a juicy, sweet contrast to the creamy cucumber salad. It adds color and a burst of freshness, making the salad more vibrant and summery.

Note: Add tomatoes just before serving to prevent them from becoming mushy.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours as the dairy-based dressing can spoil. Keep it covered on the table and refrigerate promptly after serving.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The cucumbers will soften slightly over time, but the flavors deepen. Serve chilled or at cool room temperature for best taste.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become watery and lose crunch when thawed. The creamy dressing also separates upon freezing.

Reheating

This salad is best served cold or at room temperature. Avoid reheating as it will ruin the texture and flavor.

Make Ahead

You can slice cucumbers and onions and prepare the dressing up to 1 day ahead, storing separately. Toss and chill just before serving for maximum freshness.

FAQ

Can I use regular yogurt instead of sour cream?
Yes, you can substitute regular yogurt, but Greek yogurt is preferred because it's thicker and creamier. Regular yogurt tends to be runnier, which can make the dressing less rich and may require straining to thicken.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
Store the salad in an airtight container for up to 3 days. Keep in mind cucumbers will soften over time and the texture changes, but the flavor often improves as it rests. Always serve chilled for best results.
What if I don’t have white vinegar?
You can substitute apple cider vinegar or lemon juice in equal amounts. Both provide acidity but with slightly different flavor profiles. Avoid balsamic or darker vinegars as they may overwhelm the salad's delicate taste.
Can I make this salad ahead of time?
Absolutely! Prepare the cucumbers and onions and mix the dressing separately a day ahead. Combine and chill the salad just before serving to keep cucumbers crisp and herbs fresh.
Why is my salad watery after chilling?
Cucumbers release water when salted or chilled. To reduce this, slice thinly and drain excess liquid before dressing. Avoid adding salt too early and toss gently to prevent breaking the cucumber cells.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Yes, cherry tomatoes, radishes, or fresh herbs can add color and flavor. Add these just before serving to maintain texture and freshness.
Is it okay to skip the sugar in the dressing?
Skipping sugar may result in a more acidic and sharper taste. The sugar balances the vinegar’s bite, so reduce it cautiously or substitute with a small amount of honey if preferred.
How can I make the onion less pungent?
Soak thinly sliced onions in cold water for 5-10 minutes before adding to the salad. This reduces their sulfur compounds, mellowing the sharpness while preserving crunch.
