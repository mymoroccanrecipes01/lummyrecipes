Easy Vegan Tofu Stir Fry with Colorful Veggies in 30 Minutes

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 7, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This vegan tofu stir fry is a quick, healthy dinner packed with vibrant veggies and flavorful sauce. The tofu soaks up the sauce perfectly, making every bite super satisfying. Great for busy weeknights or anytime you want a wholesome meal!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this vegan tofu stir fry when I was craving something quick and healthy on a hectic weeknight. I remember the first time I made it, the tofu was a bit soggy and didn’t quite soak up the sauce like I wanted. After a couple of tries, I learned that pressing the tofu well and tossing it in cornstarch before cooking makes all the difference—it crisps up beautifully and holds onto the sauce perfectly. What I love about this version is how the sauce is just the right balance of savory and slightly sweet, thanks to a touch of maple syrup. The colorful veggies brighten up the plate and add a fresh crunch that contrasts with the soft but crispy tofu. It’s really become a go-to meal for me when I want something nutritious, satisfying, and ready in under an hour. Plus, it’s super flexible—you can swap in whatever veggies you have on hand. The finished dish looks vibrant and smells amazing, making it a joy to eat and share. Honestly, once you nail the tofu texture and sauce, this stir fry feels like a little celebration in every bite.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 14 oz firm tofu, pressed and cubed
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon sesame oil
  • 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1 medium carrot, sliced thin
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, grated
  • 2 green onions, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon cornstarch mixed with 2 tablespoons water
  • 1 tablespoon maple syrup
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Optional: sesame seeds for garnish

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by pressing your firm tofu to remove excess water—this is key for getting a good crispy texture later. Wrap the tofu block in a clean kitchen towel and place something heavy on top for at least 15 minutes. This step prevents the tofu from steaming when cooked and helps it absorb the sauce better.
⏱ Time: 15
✓ Doneness: Tofu feels firm and much drier to the touch than before pressing
Step 2
Once pressed, cut the tofu into even cubes about 1-inch in size. Toss them gently in the cornstarch-water mixture. This coating forms a light crust when frying, giving the tofu that perfect golden-brown exterior that holds the sauce well.
⏱ Time: 5
✓ Doneness: Tofu cubes are evenly coated in a thin, slightly wet layer of cornstarch mixture
Step 3
Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Add the tofu cubes in a single layer, making sure not to overcrowd the pan. Fry them without moving for 3-4 minutes so they develop a crispy crust, then flip carefully to brown all sides evenly.
⏱ Time: 8
✓ Doneness: Tofu is golden brown and firm on all sides, with a slight crisp texture
Step 4
Remove the tofu from the pan and set aside. In the same pan, add the sesame oil along with minced garlic and grated ginger. Sauté for about 30 seconds until fragrant — this step infuses the oil with flavor and forms the aromatic base for the sauce.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Garlic and ginger smell fragrant but are not browned or burnt
Step 5
Add the sliced bell pepper, broccoli florets, and carrot slices to the pan. Stir-fry over medium-high heat for 5-7 minutes until veggies are tender-crisp. Stirring frequently ensures everything cooks evenly without getting soggy.
⏱ Time: 7
✓ Doneness: Vegetables are bright in color, tender but still have a slight crunch
Step 6
Return the tofu to the pan and drizzle the soy sauce and maple syrup over everything. Toss gently to coat all ingredients evenly. The sauce should bubble lightly and thicken slightly, clinging to tofu and veggies for maximum flavor.
⏱ Time: 3
✓ Doneness: Sauce is glossy and slightly thickened, coating tofu and vegetables well
Step 7
Add the sliced green onions, give everything one last gentle toss, and season with salt and pepper to taste. The green onions add a fresh bite and a pop of color right before serving.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Green onions are bright and fresh-looking, not wilted
Step 8
Transfer the stir fry to a serving dish and garnish with sesame seeds if you like. These add a nice nutty crunch and make the dish look even more inviting.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Dish looks colorful and well-coated with sauce, sesame seeds sprinkled on top
Step 9
Serve hot with steamed rice or your favorite grain. This dish is best enjoyed immediately while the tofu is still crispy and the vegetables retain their fresh crunch.
⏱ Time: 1
✓ Doneness: Steam rising from the plate and vibrant colors of the veggies

Pro Tips

Press the tofu well for at least 15 minutes before cooking.
Pressing removes excess moisture that would steam the tofu instead of frying it, allowing it to crisp up nicely. Without pressing, tofu tends to be soggy and won’t absorb the sauce as well.
Use a hot, well-oiled pan to fry tofu cubes without overcrowding.
A hot pan ensures immediate searing and browning, while overcrowding causes the tofu to steam. Proper spacing helps each piece develop a golden crust, improving texture and flavor.
Add garlic and ginger to oil first to release their flavors.
Sautéing aromatics in oil infuses the entire dish with their essence, creating a deep, fragrant base. Adding them too late or burning them results in bitter notes and lost flavor.
Cook vegetables just until tender-crisp over medium-high heat.
This retains their bright color, nutrients, and crunch, preventing mushiness. Stir-frying quickly at high heat helps lock in freshness and texture.
Toss tofu in cornstarch slurry before frying for extra crispiness.
Cornstarch forms a delicate crust that crisps when fried and helps the sauce cling better later. Skipping this step can lead to soggy tofu.
Add green onions at the end for a fresh pop of flavor and color.
Green onions lose their brightness if cooked too long. Adding them last maintains their mild sharpness and vibrant appearance.
Serve immediately for best texture and flavor.
Tofu loses its crispness and veggies soften over time. Serving right away ensures the dish is at its most appetizing with contrasting textures.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not pressing tofu enough.

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how much water tofu contains and skip pressing, assuming the block is firm enough as is.

Fix Press tofu thoroughly with a towel and weight for at least 15 minutes to remove excess moisture. This step is essential to get crispy tofu that holds sauce well.

Overcrowding the pan when frying tofu.

Why it happens In a rush, it’s tempting to dump all tofu in the pan at once, but this traps steam and prevents crisping.

Fix Fry tofu in batches if necessary, ensuring each piece has space to brown evenly. This prevents soggy tofu and promotes a golden crust.

Burning garlic or ginger.

Why it happens Cooking aromatics too long or at too high heat causes bitterness and ruins the flavor base.

Fix Add garlic and ginger to hot oil and cook briefly, about 30 seconds, until fragrant but not browned.

Overcooking vegetables until mushy.

Why it happens Leaving veggies in the pan too long or cooking at low heat results in loss of crunch and vibrant color.

Fix Stir-fry vegetables quickly over medium-high heat just until tender-crisp to keep texture and nutrients intact.

Adding sauce too early causing it to burn or become bitter.

Why it happens Some add soy sauce or sweeteners too soon or cook at too high heat, leading to burning.

Fix Add sauce after frying tofu and cooking veggies, then toss quickly over moderate heat until sauce thickens and coats ingredients.

Variations

Spicy Sriracha Tofu Stir Fry

Add 1-2 teaspoons of sriracha or your favorite chili sauce to the soy-maple sauce mix for a spicy kick. This variation adds heat and complexity, great if you like a little fire in your meals.

Note: Adjust the amount of chili sauce to control spice level. Adding it to the sauce allows even coating and flavor balance.

Peanut Sauce Tofu Stir Fry

Swap the soy-maple sauce for a creamy peanut sauce made with peanut butter, soy sauce, lime juice, and a bit of maple syrup. This gives a rich, nutty flavor that’s perfect for peanut lovers.

Note: Use smooth peanut butter and thin the sauce with a splash of water to get the right consistency for coating.

Tofu Stir Fry with Cashews and Snow Peas

Add toasted cashews and fresh snow peas for extra crunch and texture. These additions bring a delightful contrast and make the dish feel more substantial.

Note: Toast cashews separately to avoid burning and add at the end to retain crunch.

Low-Sodium Tofu Stir Fry

Use low-sodium soy sauce and omit the added salt to make this dish heart-healthier. Add extra garlic and fresh herbs to boost flavor without salt.

Note: Taste before serving and adjust with lemon juice or vinegar for brightness instead of salt.

Gluten-Free Tofu Stir Fry

Use tamari or coconut aminos instead of regular soy sauce for a gluten-free version. This swap keeps the umami flavor intact without gluten.

Note: Tamari has a slightly different flavor profile; balance with maple syrup as needed.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This tofu stir fry can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate leftovers promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The tofu may soften as it sits, so reheat gently to restore some texture. The flavors will deepen but veggies might lose some crunch.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat in a skillet with a splash of water or oil to refresh texture.

Reheating

Reheat on the stovetop over medium heat, stirring occasionally. Add a splash of water or soy sauce if the stir fry seems dry. Avoid microwaving too long or tofu will become rubbery.

Make Ahead

Press and cube tofu up to 2 days ahead and keep refrigerated. Chop veggies the day before for quick assembly. You can also prepare the sauce in advance and combine everything just before cooking.

FAQ

Can I use soft tofu instead of firm tofu?
Soft tofu isn’t recommended for stir frying as it breaks apart easily and won’t crisp up. Firm or extra-firm tofu holds its shape and texture better for this recipe.
How do I prevent tofu from sticking to the pan?
Make sure your pan is well-heated and use enough oil. Also, avoid moving tofu too soon—let it brown on one side before flipping to prevent sticking.
Can I make this stir fry gluten-free?
Yes! Substitute regular soy sauce with tamari or coconut aminos, which are gluten-free and still flavorful.
What if I don’t have maple syrup?
You can use agave nectar, honey (if not vegan), or a small amount of brown sugar as a substitute to add sweetness and help thicken the sauce.
How can I make this stir fry spicier?
Add chili flakes, sriracha, or a dash of hot sauce to the sauce mix. Adjust to taste and add gradually so it doesn’t overpower.
Can I add other vegetables?
Absolutely! Snap peas, mushrooms, zucchini, or baby corn all work well. Just adjust cooking times so veggies stay tender-crisp.
How do I store leftovers to keep tofu crispy?
Tofu tends to soften in the fridge. Store separately if possible and reheat in a hot pan with a little oil to help regain crispiness.
Is it okay to skip the cornstarch coating?
You can skip it, but the tofu won’t be as crispy or hold the sauce as well. Cornstarch creates a nice crust that enhances texture and flavor absorption.
