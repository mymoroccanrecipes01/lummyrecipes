Crispy Air Fryer Zucchini Fries with a Herby Twist

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 27, 2026
15 min Prep Time
10 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These zucchini fries are my quick fix when I crave something crunchy but healthy. The herby coating crisps up beautifully in the air fryer, making it a perfect snack or side any day of the week.

Introduction

I first discovered zucchini fries when I was craving something crunchy but wanted to keep things light and healthy. After a few trial runs, this version became my favorite because the coating crisps up perfectly in the air fryer without needing a ton of oil. What really sets this recipe apart is the herby mix in the breadcrumb coating — it adds just the right amount of flavor without being overpowering. I’ve tried baking these before, but the air fryer gives them that golden crunch that makes them feel like a treat, not just a vegetable. Plus, they come together in about 25 minutes, which is great for busy afternoons or when I want a quick side dish. The key is to cut the zucchini evenly and not overcrowd the basket, so each fry crisps up nicely. When they're done, the fries are crispy on the outside, tender on the inside, and totally addictive. I love dipping them in a simple garlic aioli or marinara sauce. Honestly, they’ve become a staple in my house, and I think you’ll love them too!

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 3 medium zucchinis, cut into fry-sized sticks
  • 1/2 cup panko breadcrumbs
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 1 teaspoon dried Italian herbs
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • Cooking spray or olive oil spray

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by cutting the zucchinis into evenly sized sticks, about 3 inches long and 1/2 inch thick. This uniform size ensures they cook evenly and get that perfect crispy texture. If they're cut too thin, they might get soggy; too thick, and they won't crisp up properly. Pat the zucchini sticks dry with paper towels to remove excess moisture — this step is crucial because moisture can prevent the coating from sticking and cause sogginess.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini sticks feel dry to the touch and are evenly sized
Step 2
In a shallow bowl, whisk the eggs until smooth. In another bowl, combine panko breadcrumbs, grated Parmesan, dried Italian herbs, garlic powder, salt, and pepper. The Parmesan adds a salty richness and helps the coating brown beautifully. Mix these ingredients thoroughly to distribute the herbs evenly, which will infuse every bite with flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Breadcrumb mixture looks evenly blended with no clumps
Step 3
Dip each zucchini stick into the beaten eggs, letting excess drip off, then roll it in the breadcrumb mixture to coat thoroughly. Press gently to make sure the coating sticks well. This double step creates a crispy crust that stays put while cooking. If you skip the egg wash or don't press enough, the coating can fall off during cooking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Each zucchini fry is evenly coated with breadcrumb mixture
Step 4
Preheat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the coated zucchini fries in a single layer in the air fryer basket, making sure they don’t touch. Overcrowding traps steam and results in soggy fries instead of crispy ones. You may need to cook in batches depending on your air fryer size.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer is fully preheated and fries are spaced apart
Step 5
Lightly spray the fries with cooking spray or olive oil spray. This step helps the coating crisp up and develop a golden color without adding too much oil. Avoid pouring oil directly as it can make the coating soggy or uneven.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fries have a light, even mist of oil
Step 6
Cook the zucchini fries in the air fryer at 400°F for 8-10 minutes. Halfway through, gently shake or flip the fries to ensure even cooking on all sides. This flipping is important because the air fryer’s heat circulates, and turning them prevents burning on one side and undercooking on the other.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fries are golden brown and crispy on all sides
Step 7
Once cooked, remove the fries carefully and let them cool for a couple of minutes on a wire rack or paper towels. This helps maintain their crispiness by allowing excess steam to escape instead of making them soggy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fries feel firm and crispy, not soft or soggy
Step 8
Serve immediately with your favorite dip like garlic aioli, marinara, or ranch. These fries are best enjoyed fresh but can be reheated for a short time in the air fryer if needed. The contrast of the crispy crust and tender zucchini inside makes for a delightful bite every time.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fries are warm with a crunchy exterior and soft interior
Step 9
For leftovers, store fries in an airtight container in the fridge and reheat in the air fryer for a few minutes to restore crispiness. Avoid microwaving as it tends to make them soggy. When reheating, keep an eye to prevent burning and enjoy almost-as-good-as-fresh fries.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fries are reheated evenly and remain crispy

Pro Tips

Pat zucchini sticks dry before coating.
Removing excess moisture from zucchini is crucial because water prevents the coating from sticking and causes sogginess. By drying the sticks well with paper towels, you ensure a crispier final fry since the breading adheres better and fries cook evenly.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Overcrowding traps steam between fries, making them soggy instead of crisp. Leaving space allows hot air to circulate freely, crisping every side. Cooking in batches if needed results in perfectly crunchy fries every time.
Use panko breadcrumbs instead of regular breadcrumbs.
Panko’s coarse texture creates more air pockets, leading to a lighter and crunchier crust. Regular breadcrumbs tend to be finer and produce a denser, less crispy coating. This choice is key for that satisfying crunch.
Flip fries halfway through cooking.
Turning fries ensures even exposure to hot air, promoting uniform browning and crispiness. Without flipping, one side may burn while the other remains undercooked, ruining the texture and flavor balance.
Lightly spray fries with oil before cooking.
A light coating of oil helps conduct heat and promotes browning without soaking the coating. Directly pouring oil can weigh down the crust and cause sogginess, so a spray is the best way to achieve a golden, crispy exterior.
Serve fries immediately for best texture.
Zucchini fries are crispiest right after cooking. As they sit, steam softens the coating, making them less crunchy. If you need to hold them, place on a wire rack to allow airflow and keep them crisp longer.
Use freshly grated Parmesan cheese.
Freshly grated Parmesan melts and browns better than pre-grated versions, which often contain anti-caking agents that can affect texture and flavor. This enhances both taste and appearance of the fries.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the drying step for zucchini.

Why it happens Many assume that washing is enough and don’t realize moisture affects coating adhesion and crispiness.

Fix After cutting, pat zucchini sticks dry thoroughly with paper towels to remove surface moisture. This ensures the egg wash and breadcrumb coating stick properly and fries crisp up instead of steaming.

Overcrowding the air fryer basket.

Why it happens Trying to cook all fries at once to save time leads to steaming rather than frying.

Fix Arrange fries in a single layer with space between each piece. Cook in batches if necessary to allow hot air circulation for even crisping.

Not flipping fries during cooking.

Why it happens Forgetting or rushing the cooking process prevents even browning.

Fix Pause halfway through cooking to shake or flip fries gently. This ensures all sides crisp evenly and prevents burning.

Using regular breadcrumbs instead of panko.

Why it happens Sometimes panko isn’t on hand, so cooks substitute with regular breadcrumbs.

Fix Use panko for that light, airy crunch. If you only have regular breadcrumbs, toast them lightly to add crispness before coating.

Applying too much oil before cooking.

Why it happens Thinking more oil equals more crispiness, some over-spray or drizzle oil.

Fix Use a light spray of oil to avoid soggy coating and uneven cooking. Excess oil weighs down the crust and prevents crisp texture.

Variations

Spicy Zucchini Fries

Add 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper or smoked paprika to the breadcrumb mixture for a kick of heat. These fries will have a smoky, spicy edge that pairs well with cool ranch or yogurt-based dips, perfect if you like a bit of bold flavor.

Note: Keep an eye on cooking time as the spices can darken the coating faster; flip fries more frequently to avoid burning.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Fries

Swap panko breadcrumbs for gluten-free breadcrumbs or crushed gluten-free cornflakes. This keeps the fries crispy and makes the recipe safe for gluten-sensitive eaters without sacrificing texture.

Note: Gluten-free coatings may brown quicker, so monitor closely during air frying to prevent overcooking.

Cheesy Herb Zucchini Fries

Mix in some finely chopped fresh herbs like parsley or chives with the breadcrumb mixture and increase Parmesan to 1/3 cup. These fries have a fresher, more aromatic flavor and a richer cheesy bite.

Note: Fresh herbs add moisture, so ensure zucchini is well dried to maintain crispiness.

Vegan Zucchini Fries

Replace eggs with a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water) and use nutritional yeast instead of Parmesan for cheesy flavor. The fries stay crunchy and flavorful, perfect for plant-based diets.

Note: Flax egg is less sticky than real egg, so press coating firmly and handle fries gently to prevent crumb loss.

Baked Zucchini Fries

If you don’t have an air fryer, bake fries on a parchment-lined baking sheet at 425°F for 20-25 minutes, flipping halfway. They won’t be quite as crispy but still delicious and healthier than deep-fried versions.

Note: Use a wire rack on the baking sheet if possible to allow air circulation underneath for better crisping.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Zucchini fries can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours when covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond this time, moisture can cause them to become soggy or unsafe to eat.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. The fries will lose some crispness but can be reheated in the air fryer to restore texture. Avoid storing with wet dips to prevent sogginess.

Freezer

Freeze cooked zucchini fries in a single layer on a baking sheet before transferring to a freezer-safe container or bag. Freeze for up to 1 month. To thaw, reheat directly from frozen in the air fryer for best results; avoid microwaving to maintain crispiness.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 375°F for 3-5 minutes until crispy and heated through. Avoid microwave reheating as it will make fries soggy. A quick spray of oil before reheating helps restore crunch.

Make Ahead

You can prepare zucchini sticks and breadcrumb mixture up to 1 day ahead and keep refrigerated separately. Dip and coat just before cooking to keep coating fresh and fries crispy. This saves time on busy days.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for these fries?
Frozen zucchini releases a lot of water when thawed, making it difficult to get a crispy coating. If you want to try, thaw thoroughly and squeeze out as much moisture as possible, but fresh zucchini is best for optimal crunch.
What if I don’t have an air fryer?
You can bake the fries in a conventional oven at 425°F for 20-25 minutes, flipping halfway. They won’t be quite as crispy but still tasty. Using a wire rack on the baking sheet helps air circulate for better texture.
How do I make these fries vegan?
Replace the eggs with a flax egg (1 tbsp ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tbsp water) and substitute Parmesan with nutritional yeast for a cheesy flavor. The coating may be less sticky, so press the crumbs firmly onto the zucchini.
Why aren’t my fries crispy?
Common reasons include not drying the zucchini well, overcrowding the air fryer basket, or skipping the oil spray. Make sure to pat dry zucchini, cook in a single layer, and lightly spray oil before air frying.
Can I add other spices to the coating?
Absolutely! Feel free to add paprika, cayenne, or your favorite herbs to customize the flavor. Just be mindful of strong spices that could overpower the zucchini’s delicate taste.
How long can I store leftovers?
Store cooked fries in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in the air fryer to restore crispiness. Avoid leaving them at room temperature for more than 2 hours to prevent spoilage.
What dipping sauces do you recommend?
Garlic aioli, marinara, ranch, or even a simple squeeze of lemon work beautifully. The fries have a mild flavor so they pair well with a wide range of dips.
Can I make these gluten-free?
Yes! Use gluten-free breadcrumbs or crushed gluten-free cereals instead of panko. Keep an eye on cooking time as gluten-free coatings can brown faster.
