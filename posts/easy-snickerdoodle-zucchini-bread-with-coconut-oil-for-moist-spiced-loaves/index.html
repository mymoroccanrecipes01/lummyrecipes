Cozy Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread with a Tender Coconut Oil Twist

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 10, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This zucchini bread brings a fun snickerdoodle spin with warm cinnamon sugar and moist coconut oil. It’s perfect for breakfast or a comforting snack. You'll love how tender and flavorful it is!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this snickerdoodle zucchini bread recipe a few summers ago when I had way too much zucchini from my garden. I wanted to do something a little different than the usual zucchini bread, and the idea to mix in that classic cinnamon sugar snickerdoodle flavor just clicked. The coconut oil addition is a game changer — it keeps the bread incredibly moist and adds a subtle richness that butter just doesn't deliver. I’ve tried this recipe a few times now, and each loaf turns out tender, with a perfect crumb and that warm cinnamon sugar crust on top. One trick I learned early on is to make sure you squeeze the zucchini really well — if it’s too wet, the bread can get dense or gummy. Also, mixing the cinnamon sugar topping right before baking creates that lovely crackly, sweet finish that makes this loaf so irresistible. When you slice into it, you’ll get a soft, fragrant bread that’s great for breakfast with coffee or just a cozy afternoon snack. Honestly, it’s become a favorite in my house, and I think you’ll love it too!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 2 cups grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1/2 cup coconut oil, melted
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 teaspoons cinnamon sugar (for topping)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating your zucchini finely and then place it in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth. Squeeze out as much moisture as possible—this step is crucial because too much water will make your bread dense and soggy. The goal is to keep the bread moist but not wet. Set the zucchini aside while you prepare the batter.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels damp but no dripping water when squeezed
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the melted coconut oil, granulated sugar, and brown sugar until well combined. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing thoroughly after each addition. Stir in the vanilla extract. This mixture should be smooth and slightly glossy, which helps create a tender crumb in the bread.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks smooth and homogenous without sugar granules
Step 3
In a separate bowl, sift together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt. Sifting helps remove lumps and evenly distributes the leavening agents and spices, which is important for an even rise and consistent flavor. Once sifted, slowly add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, folding gently to avoid overmixing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is combined with no visible streaks of flour but not overworked
Step 4
Fold the squeezed zucchini into the batter just until evenly incorporated. Overmixing here can toughen the bread, so be gentle. The zucchini adds moisture and a subtle texture that makes the loaf so special.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly distributed without clumps or dry spots
Step 5
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with a bit of coconut oil or line it with parchment paper to prevent sticking. Pour the batter into the pan and smooth the top with a spatula for even baking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread and smooth on top
Step 6
Mix together the cinnamon sugar topping (2 teaspoons cinnamon and sugar combined). Sprinkle it evenly over the top of the batter. This step adds a sweet, crackly crust that mimics the classic snickerdoodle cookie finish, giving the bread its signature flavor and texture contrast.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping is evenly sprinkled with no heavy clumps
Step 7
Bake the bread for 50-55 minutes. Check doneness by inserting a toothpick in the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Avoid opening the oven door too often, as this can cause the bread to sink or bake unevenly.
⏱ Time: 50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick comes out mostly clean; top is golden brown with crackly sugar
Step 8
Once baked, remove the loaf from the oven and let it cool in the pan for about 15 minutes. This resting time allows the bread to set and makes it easier to remove from the pan without breaking.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf feels firm to the touch and slightly pulls away from pan edges
Step 9
Transfer the bread to a wire rack to cool completely before slicing. Cooling fully ensures the crumb finishes setting and prevents it from turning gummy when cut. Slice with a serrated knife for clean cuts, and enjoy warm or at room temperature.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is fully cooled and slices cleanly without crumbling

Pro Tips

Always squeeze zucchini thoroughly before adding it to the batter.
Removing excess moisture prevents the bread from becoming gummy or dense. If the zucchini is too wet, it dilutes the flour’s ability to form the gluten network, resulting in a heavy texture.
Use room temperature eggs for better batter consistency.
Cold eggs can cause the melted coconut oil to solidify, leading to uneven mixing. Room temperature eggs blend smoothly with fats and sugars, ensuring a uniform batter and consistent rise.
Don’t overmix the batter once you add the flour.
Overmixing develops gluten excessively, making the bread tough. Gently folding the dry ingredients in just until combined keeps the crumb tender and soft.
Sprinkle cinnamon sugar topping right before baking.
Adding the topping too early can cause it to dissolve into the batter. Sprinkling just before baking creates a crisp, caramelized crust that adds texture and flavor contrast.
Use a serrated knife to slice the cooled bread.
A serrated knife cuts through the tender crumb without squashing it, preserving the loaf’s shape and texture.
Let the bread cool completely before storing.
Cooling prevents condensation inside storage containers, which can make the bread soggy or mold faster.
Store leftovers wrapped tightly or in an airtight container.
This keeps the bread moist and fresh for several days by preventing air exposure, which dries it out.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many people underestimate how much water zucchini holds, assuming grating alone is enough.

Fix After grating, wrap zucchini in a clean towel and press firmly to remove excess water. You can even use your hands to twist the towel for better results.

Overmixing the batter after adding flour

Why it happens It’s tempting to mix thoroughly to avoid lumps, but overmixing develops too much gluten.

Fix Fold dry ingredients gently until just combined. Stop mixing as soon as you see no more flour streaks.

Using cold eggs or coconut oil

Why it happens Cold ingredients don’t blend well, causing uneven texture.

Fix Bring eggs and coconut oil to room temperature before mixing for a smooth batter.

Skipping the cinnamon sugar topping

Why it happens Some skip it to save time, but it’s key for flavor and texture.

Fix Sprinkle the cinnamon sugar mix just before baking to get that perfect snickerdoodle crust.

Cutting the bread too soon

Why it happens Impatience leads to slicing while still warm, causing crumbling or gummy texture.

Fix Let the bread cool completely on a wire rack before slicing to ensure a clean cut.

Variations

Gluten-Free Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the bread tender and moist while accommodating gluten sensitivities. Expect a slightly different crumb texture but the same great flavor.

Note: Gluten-free flours absorb moisture differently; you may need to adjust the liquid slightly or add an extra egg for structure.

Vegan Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg) and use coconut yogurt or applesauce instead of eggs for moisture. Coconut oil keeps it rich and tender. The bread will be slightly denser but still delicious.

Note: Vegan substitutes can alter rise and texture; make sure flax eggs gel well before mixing.

Add Walnuts for Crunch

Fold in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts for a crunchy texture contrast. Nuts complement the cinnamon flavor and add a nice bite to the soft zucchini bread. Great for a more substantial snack.

Note: Add nuts at the last mixing step to avoid overmixing the batter.

Pumpkin Snickerdoodle Bread

Replace half the zucchini with canned pumpkin puree and add a pinch of nutmeg and cloves along with cinnamon. This variation brings a deeper fall flavor that pairs perfectly with the snickerdoodle spice.

Note: Pumpkin adds extra moisture; you might reduce coconut oil slightly to balance.

Mini Muffin Snickerdoodle Zucchini Bites

Pour batter into a mini muffin tin for bite-sized treats. Bake for about 18-20 minutes. These are perfect for lunchboxes or parties and have the same tender, flavorful crumb.

Note: Adjust baking time carefully to avoid overbaking small portions.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This bread keeps well at room temperature for up to 2 days if wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. Make sure it’s completely cooled before wrapping to avoid condensation.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up and lose some moisture when chilled, so bring it to room temperature or warm slightly before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and then in aluminum foil or place in a freezer bag. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat gently to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm slices in a toaster oven or microwave for 20-30 seconds with a damp paper towel to prevent drying out. Avoid overheating to keep the bread moist and tender.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Just give it a gentle stir before pouring into the pan and baking. This helps develop flavor and saves time on baking day.

FAQ

Can I use regular vegetable oil instead of coconut oil?
Yes, you can substitute vegetable oil for coconut oil in equal amounts. However, coconut oil adds a subtle richness and helps create a tender crumb that vegetable oil might not replicate exactly. The flavor will be slightly different but still delicious.
What if I don’t have cinnamon sugar for the topping?
You can mix regular sugar with ground cinnamon in the right proportions (about 1 tsp cinnamon to 2 tbsp sugar) to sprinkle on top. This combination caramelizes nicely in the oven, giving that signature snickerdoodle crust.
How do I prevent the bread from being too dense?
Make sure to squeeze out excess moisture from the zucchini and avoid overmixing the batter after adding the flour. Overmixing develops gluten which can make the bread tough. Also, don’t skip the baking powder and soda—they help the bread rise properly.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free all-purpose flour blend that contains xanthan gum to mimic the structure gluten provides. You might need to adjust liquid amounts slightly, but the bread will still be moist and flavorful.
Is it possible to freeze this bread?
Yes, this bread freezes very well. Wrap it tightly in plastic wrap and then foil or a freezer bag. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature, then warm gently before serving to restore moisture.
Can I add nuts or chocolate chips to the recipe?
Definitely! Adding 1/2 cup of chopped nuts or chocolate chips can add texture and flavor. Fold them in gently at the end to avoid overmixing.
Why is my bread dry after baking?
Dry bread often means overbaking or not enough fat/moisture. Make sure to follow baking times closely and use the correct amount of coconut oil. Also, squeezing out too much moisture from zucchini can sometimes remove too much liquid; balance is key.
Can I use frozen grated zucchini?
You can, but thaw and squeeze out all excess water very well before using. Frozen zucchini tends to release more moisture, so thorough draining is critical to avoid soggy bread.
