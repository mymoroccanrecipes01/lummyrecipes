Cozy Fall Snack Mix with Spiced Pumpkin Seeds and Sweet Apple Chips

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This fall snack mix is my quick fix for cozy vibes — crunchy spiced pumpkin seeds paired with sweet apple chips and a rich caramel dip. It’s perfect for afternoon cravings or sharing at casual gatherings!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this cozy fall snack mix a couple of years ago when I wanted something warm and comforting but without the hassle of baking or cooking. The combination of spiced pumpkin seeds and sweet apple chips quickly became a favorite in my family — it’s crunchy, lightly sweet, and has just the right hint of spice. What makes this version special is the touch of cayenne pepper mixed with cinnamon and nutmeg on the pumpkin seeds, which adds a subtle kick that balances the sweetness of the apple chips and caramel dip perfectly. I usually prepare this mix when I want a quick afternoon pick-me-up or when friends drop by unexpectedly. It’s so simple yet feels indulgent. The trick I learned is roasting the pumpkin seeds just right so they’re crispy but not burnt, which brings out their nutty flavor without bitterness. When you put it all together, the mix looks beautiful and tastes like fall in a bowl — warm, cozy, and satisfying. I think you’ll love how easy it is to whip up and how it keeps well if you want to prepare it ahead for gatherings.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 1 cup raw pumpkin seeds
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional)
  • 1 tablespoon maple syrup
  • 2 cups dried apple chips
  • 1/2 cup caramel dip (store-bought or homemade)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 325°F (165°C). Spread the raw pumpkin seeds evenly on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Roasting at this moderate temperature allows the seeds to toast evenly without burning, which enhances their nutty flavor and gives a satisfying crunch. Keep an eye on them during roasting, stirring once halfway through, so they brown uniformly. Avoid increasing the heat too much, or the seeds will scorch and taste bitter.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Seeds are golden brown and smell toasty, not burnt.
Step 2
While the seeds roast, mix the ground cinnamon, nutmeg, and cayenne pepper in a small bowl. This blend adds warmth and a subtle spicy kick to the seeds, balancing the natural sweetness of the apple chips later. The cayenne is optional but highly recommended if you like a little heat. If you skip it, your snack will be sweeter and milder.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Spice blend is fragrant and evenly mixed.
Step 3
Once the pumpkin seeds are roasted, immediately transfer them to a mixing bowl. While still warm, drizzle the maple syrup over the seeds and sprinkle the spice mix on top. Toss everything thoroughly so each seed is evenly coated. The warmth helps the syrup and spices stick better, creating a delicious glaze. If you wait too long, the seeds cool and the coating won’t adhere as well.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Seeds appear glossy and evenly coated with spices.
Step 4
Allow the coated seeds to cool completely on a plate or tray. This lets the glaze set and prevents clumping. If you try to mix or store them while warm, they’ll stick together and lose their crunchiness. Cooling also intensifies the flavor as the spices meld with the seeds.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Seeds are dry to the touch and crisp.
Step 5
Prepare your serving bowl by placing the dried apple chips on one side and the spiced pumpkin seeds on the other. This keeps textures distinct and visually appealing. The apple chips provide a sweet, crisp contrast to the nutty, spiced seeds. If you mix them too early, the seeds’ glaze might transfer onto the apple chips, making them soggy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack mix looks colorful and inviting.
Step 6
Pour the caramel dip into a small serving dish and place it alongside your snack mix. Use a good-quality caramel dip or make your own by gently heating caramel sauce until pourable but not too runny. The dip adds a rich, buttery sweetness that ties the whole snack together. Avoid overly thick or grainy caramel sauces as they won’t complement the crisp textures well.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Caramel dip is smooth and easily dippable.
Step 7
Serve immediately or cover the spiced seeds and apple chips separately in airtight containers if preparing ahead. This keeps textures fresh and flavors vibrant. The caramel dip is best served fresh but can be stored in the fridge for a day or two. Rewarm gently before serving if it hardens.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack mix retains crunch and caramel is smooth.
Step 8
For an extra touch, sprinkle a pinch of flaky sea salt over the pumpkin seeds before serving. The salt enhances the sweet and spicy flavors and adds a gourmet feel. Be careful not to overdo it; a light sprinkle is enough to make a difference without overpowering the other flavors.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Seeds have a subtle sparkle of salt and balanced flavor.
Step 9
Enjoy your cozy fall snack mix with a warm cup of tea or coffee. The combination of crunchy, sweet, and spicy flavors is perfect for chilly afternoons or casual gatherings. Don’t hesitate to experiment by adding nuts or dried cranberries if you want to customize the mix.
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Snack mix is enjoyed fresh and crisp.

Pro Tips

Use parchment paper on your baking sheet to prevent sticking and easy cleanup.
Parchment paper creates a non-stick surface that ensures pumpkin seeds roast evenly without sticking to the pan. This also simplifies cleanup and helps maintain the seeds’ shape and texture, preventing them from breaking or burning where they contact the metal directly.
Stir pumpkin seeds halfway through roasting for uniform browning.
Turning the seeds during roasting exposes all sides to heat, encouraging even color and flavor development. Without stirring, some seeds can burn while others remain pale and undercooked, resulting in uneven texture and taste.
Add spices to warm seeds immediately after roasting while still hot.
Warm seeds are more receptive to the sticky maple syrup and spices, which helps the seasoning adhere better. Waiting until the seeds cool leads to patchy coverage and less flavorful results.
Cool the coated seeds completely before storing to keep them crunchy.
If stored while warm, moisture trapped inside containers can make seeds soggy. Cooling allows the glaze to harden and moisture to dissipate, preserving the crisp texture essential for an enjoyable snack.
Keep apple chips separate from the seeds until serving to maintain crunch.
Combining the moist-coated seeds with apple chips too early causes the chips to absorb moisture and lose their crispness. Serving them separately preserves texture contrast.
Use a light sprinkle of flaky sea salt on seeds before serving for flavor lift.
Salt enhances sweetness and spices, providing a more complex flavor profile. Flaky sea salt adds a delicate crunch and bursts of flavor without overwhelming the snack.
If you want to make this snack spicier, increase cayenne slowly and taste as you go.
Cayenne’s heat can quickly overpower the other flavors. Gradual addition allows control over spice level, ensuring a balanced result that complements the sweetness and warmth of cinnamon and nutmeg.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Roasting pumpkin seeds at too high a temperature

Why it happens Many assume higher heat means faster roasting, but this often leads to burnt seeds with a bitter taste.

Fix Stick to 325°F and watch the seeds closely. Stir halfway through roasting for even browning. Remove from oven as soon as they turn golden and smell nutty.

Adding spices when seeds are cold

Why it happens It’s tempting to wait until seeds cool to handle them, but cold seeds don’t hold seasoning well.

Fix Coat seeds immediately after roasting while warm. This ensures the syrup and spice mix sticks evenly, creating a flavorful glaze.

Mixing apple chips with coated seeds too early

Why it happens People often combine everything at once for convenience, not realizing moisture transfer ruins crispness.

Fix Keep apple chips separate until serving to maintain their crunch and prevent sogginess.

Using pre-roasted or salted pumpkin seeds

Why it happens Pre-seasoned seeds can overpower the delicate spice blend and make the snack too salty.

Fix Start with raw, unsalted pumpkin seeds for full control over flavor and seasoning.

Storing coated seeds before they cool

Why it happens Warm seeds in airtight containers trap moisture, causing sogginess.

Fix Cool seeds completely on a tray before storing in airtight containers to keep them crisp.

Variations

Nutty Cranberry Fall Mix

Add 1/2 cup chopped toasted pecans and 1/2 cup dried cranberries to the mix for a nutty, tangy twist. The pecans add buttery richness while cranberries bring a tart pop that pairs beautifully with the spices.

Note: Toast pecans lightly before mixing to enhance flavor. Add dried cranberries just before serving to keep them chewy.

Savory Herb Pumpkin Seeds

Skip the sweet spices and maple syrup, and instead toss roasted pumpkin seeds with olive oil, rosemary, thyme, and sea salt for a savory snack option that pairs well with cheese plates.

Note: Roast seeds plain, then toss with finely chopped herbs and warm olive oil for even coating.

Chocolate-Dipped Apple Chips

Dip some dried apple chips halfway into melted dark chocolate and let them set for a decadent treat alongside the spiced seeds. This adds a rich, bittersweet element to the snack mix.

Note: Use tempered chocolate for shine and snap. Allow chocolate to fully harden before serving.

Spiced Pumpkin Seed Granola

Mix the spiced pumpkin seeds with oats, honey, and nuts, then bake into a crunchy granola. It’s perfect for breakfast or topping yogurt, offering the same cozy flavors in a new form.

Note: Bake granola at lower temperature (300°F) to avoid burning seeds and achieve even toasting.

Vegan Caramel Dip

Make a dairy-free caramel dip using coconut milk and coconut sugar for a vegan-friendly option that’s just as creamy and sweet, pairing perfectly with the snack mix.

Note: Cook coconut milk and sugar slowly until thickened; chill to set before serving.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This snack mix can be kept at room temperature for up to 6 hours when covered loosely to maintain crispness. Avoid tightly sealing at room temp as moisture can cause sogginess.

Refrigerator

Store spiced pumpkin seeds and apple chips separately in airtight containers for up to 5 days. The caramel dip should be refrigerated in a sealed container for up to 3 days. Bring dip to room temperature before serving for best flavor.

Freezer

You can freeze the spiced pumpkin seeds in an airtight container for up to 2 months. Apple chips don’t freeze well as they become soggy. Thaw seeds at room temperature and re-crisp briefly in a warm oven if needed.

Reheating

If pumpkin seeds lose crunch after storage, reheat them in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes to restore crispness. Avoid microwaving as it can make seeds chewy. Caramel dip can be gently warmed in a double boiler or microwave in short bursts.

Make Ahead

Prepare and roast pumpkin seeds 1-2 days ahead, store cooled seeds separately from apple chips. Make caramel dip up to 2 days in advance and refrigerate. Assemble snack mix just before serving for best texture.

FAQ

Can I use salted pumpkin seeds instead of raw ones?
I don’t recommend salted seeds because they can make the snack too salty and interfere with the delicate spice balance. Using raw seeds lets you control seasoning and ensures the spices and maple syrup adhere properly.
How do I keep the pumpkin seeds crunchy after storing?
Make sure the seeds are completely cooled before storing in an airtight container. If they soften, a quick reheat in a 300°F oven for a few minutes will bring back their crunch without burning.
Can I substitute maple syrup with honey?
Yes, honey works as a substitute but it has a stronger flavor and different viscosity, which might affect how the spices stick. Use a mild honey and adjust amounts to taste.
What if I don’t have dried apple chips?
You can use thinly sliced dried apples or even crispy dried pears. Just make sure they’re crunchy and not chewy to maintain texture contrast in the snack mix.
Is the cayenne pepper necessary?
No, cayenne is optional. It adds a nice warm heat that balances the sweetness but if you prefer milder snacks, just omit it or reduce the amount.
How long does the caramel dip keep after opening?
Store-bought caramel dip usually keeps 3-5 days refrigerated once opened. Homemade caramel dip lasts about 3 days in the fridge. Always keep it sealed to prevent drying out or absorbing fridge odors.
Can I make the snack mix gluten-free?
Absolutely! This recipe is naturally gluten-free since it uses pumpkin seeds and apple chips. Just double-check any store-bought caramel dip to ensure it’s gluten-free.
What’s the best way to serve this snack mix at a party?
Serve the spiced seeds and apple chips separately in bowls with the caramel dip in a small dish for dipping. This keeps textures fresh and lets guests customize bites.
