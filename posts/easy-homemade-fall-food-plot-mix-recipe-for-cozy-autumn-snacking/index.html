Cozy Fall Food Plot Mix with Nuts, Seeds, and Cranberries

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 12, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crunchy and chewy snack mix blends roasted nuts, pumpkin seeds, and tart cranberries for a perfect autumn treat. I whip up a big batch to snack on during chilly afternoons or share with friends at casual get-togethers.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this Fall Food Plot Mix a few years ago when I wanted a snack that felt seasonal but was still wholesome and easy to grab on the go. What makes this mix really stand out for me is how it balances that toasty crunch from the nuts and seeds with the sweet chewiness of dried cranberries. Plus, adding a touch of maple syrup and cinnamon gives it this warm autumn vibe that's just irresistible. I’ve tried other versions before, but what I love about this recipe is the simple roasting technique that really brings out the depth of flavor without making it overly sweet or greasy. One trick I learned is to toss the nuts and seeds in melted coconut oil and spices before roasting; it helps everything crisp up perfectly and keeps the flavors well-coated. The result is a snack that’s not just tasty but feels like a little cozy hug in every bite. Whether I’m packing it for a hike, munching while reading a book, or sharing it at a casual get-together, this mix never disappoints. It’s pretty forgiving too — you can swap nuts or seeds based on what you have, and it still turns out great. Honestly, it’s become a fall staple in my kitchen and I think you’ll love it as much as I do.

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 cups, 6 servings

  • 1 cup raw almonds
  • 1 cup raw cashews
  • 1/2 cup pumpkin seeds (pepitas)
  • 1/2 cup sunflower seeds
  • 1/2 cup dried cranberries
  • 1 tablespoon maple syrup
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon sea salt
  • 1 tablespoon coconut oil

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 325°F (160°C). This moderate temperature ensures the nuts and seeds roast evenly without burning, allowing their natural oils to release and deepen their flavor. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper or a silicone baking mat to prevent sticking and make cleanup easier. Spread the raw almonds, cashews, pumpkin seeds, and sunflower seeds in a single layer on the sheet. Roasting at this temperature gives the mix a toasty aroma and crunch, which is the foundation of the snack’s texture.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts should turn a light golden brown and smell fragrant but not burnt.
Step 2
While the nuts and seeds are roasting, melt the coconut oil in a small bowl. Stir in the maple syrup, ground cinnamon, and sea salt until fully combined. This mixture will coat the nuts and seeds, helping the spices stick and adding a subtle sweetness that complements the natural flavors. The coconut oil also aids in crisping the nuts during roasting and adds a slight tropical note that balances the fall spices.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Smooth, well-blended syrup mixture with no separation.
Step 3
Once the nuts and seeds are lightly toasted, remove the baking sheet from the oven. Immediately pour the syrup and oil mixture over the warm nuts and seeds. Use a spatula or wooden spoon to gently toss everything together, making sure each piece is evenly coated. The warmth helps the coating stick better, so don’t wait too long. If you skip this step or add the syrup when the nuts are cold, the flavor won’t cling as well and the mix can turn soggy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: All nuts and seeds glisten with a light, even coating.
Step 4
Return the coated mixture to the baking sheet and spread it out again in a single layer. Place it back in the oven for another 10-15 minutes to bake the coating onto the nuts and seeds. This second roast helps the flavors meld and creates a slight caramelization on the maple syrup, enhancing sweetness without adding stickiness. Keep a close eye during this step to avoid burning, especially near the end.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts are golden brown with a shiny glaze and smell sweet and spiced.
Step 5
Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the nut and seed mixture cool completely on the sheet. Cooling is crucial because the coating will harden as it cools, giving the mix a satisfying crunch. If you try to eat it warm, it might feel sticky or soft. Spread it out so it cools evenly and doesn’t clump together. This step also lets the flavors settle and intensify.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mix is dry to the touch and crunchy.
Step 6
Once cooled, transfer the nut and seed mixture to a large bowl. Stir in the dried cranberries gently. Adding them last keeps the cranberries chewy and fresh-tasting rather than dried out or overheated. The tartness of the cranberries contrasts beautifully with the sweet and spiced nuts, making each bite interesting and well-balanced.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cranberries are evenly distributed without bruising.
Step 7
Taste your mix and adjust seasoning if needed. If you prefer it a bit sweeter, add a small drizzle of maple syrup and toss gently. For a saltier kick, sprinkle a pinch more sea salt and mix. This step lets you customize the flavor to your liking, which is great because some people love it sweeter, others more savory.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Flavor is balanced to your preference.
Step 8
Store the finished mix in an airtight container at room temperature. It keeps beautifully for up to two weeks, making it perfect for batch prep. When stored properly, the crunch stays intact and the flavors remain vibrant. Avoid storing in humid areas to prevent sogginess.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Mix stays fresh and crunchy over time.
Step 9
Enjoy this mix as a quick snack, sprinkle it over yogurt or oatmeal for extra texture, or pack it in your lunchbox for a healthy energy boost. It’s versatile and convenient, perfect for busy days or relaxing moments. The combination of textures and flavors makes it a satisfying treat that feels indulgent but is wholesome.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Snack is enjoyed with a smile!

Pro Tips

Always roast nuts and seeds at a moderate temperature (325°F) to avoid bitterness.
Roasting at high heat can cause nuts to scorch and develop a bitter flavor. A moderate temperature allows the Maillard reaction to occur slowly, enhancing nuttiness and crunch without burning. This careful control is key to a balanced flavor profile.
Toss nuts with oil and spices while still warm from the oven.
Warm nuts absorb the coating better because the heat opens their pores slightly, allowing flavors to penetrate. If you wait until they cool, the coating may not adhere evenly, causing uneven flavor and texture.
Spread the mix out in a single layer on the baking sheet for even roasting.
Crowding the pan traps steam and prevents proper browning, leading to soggy or unevenly roasted nuts. A single layer ensures dry heat exposure, crisp texture, and consistent flavor development.
Add dried cranberries after roasting to preserve their chewiness.
Heating cranberries during roasting can dry them out and make them tough or brittle. Adding them last keeps their natural moisture and tart flavor intact, creating a delightful contrast to the crunchy nuts.
Cool the mix completely before storing to maintain crunch.
Warm nuts release steam that can cause condensation in storage, leading to sogginess and spoilage. Allowing the mix to cool fully prevents moisture buildup and extends shelf life.
Use parchment paper or a silicone mat to prevent sticking and ease cleanup.
Coated nuts can stick to bare baking sheets, risking broken pieces and uneven roasting. A non-stick surface helps maintain the integrity of the mix and saves time washing up.
Customize the spice blend with additions like nutmeg or ginger for variety.
Adding complementary spices can enhance the autumnal flavor profile. These spices work well with cinnamon and maple syrup, offering depth and warmth without overpowering the nuts.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Roasting nuts at too high a temperature

Why it happens Many cooks rush the roasting process to save time, assuming higher heat will speed things up without consequence.

Fix Stick to a moderate temperature around 325°F and roast slowly. This ensures even browning and prevents bitterness. Use a timer and check frequently toward the end to avoid burning.

Adding syrup coating to cold nuts

Why it happens Some think the coating will stick regardless of nut temperature and try to save time by skipping the warm toss step.

Fix Always coat nuts while warm from roasting. The heat helps the syrup and oil adhere better, resulting in an even, glossy finish and better flavor absorption.

Overcrowding the baking sheet

Why it happens Trying to roast everything at once to save time or reduce dishes leads to uneven cooking.

Fix Use a large enough baking sheet so nuts are spread out in a single layer. Roast in batches if needed to ensure proper heat circulation and crispness.

Storing mix before it cools completely

Why it happens Impatience or lack of planning causes storing warm mix, leading to condensation inside the container.

Fix Let the mix cool fully on the baking sheet before transferring to airtight containers. This keeps it crunchy and prevents mold or spoilage.

Heating dried cranberries during roasting

Why it happens Some assume all ingredients can be roasted together for simplicity.

Fix Add dried cranberries only after the roasting and cooling steps to maintain their chewy texture and avoid drying them out.

Variations

Spicy Fall Mix

Add 1/2 teaspoon cayenne pepper and a pinch of smoked paprika to the syrup mixture for a warm, spicy kick. This variation pairs beautifully with the sweetness of the maple syrup and tart cranberries, adding complexity and a little heat to the snack.

Note: Be cautious with cayenne; start small and adjust to taste to avoid overpowering the mix.

Chocolate Drizzle Mix

After the mix cools, drizzle melted dark chocolate over it and let it set in the fridge. The chocolate adds richness and indulgence, making this mix perfect for dessert or holiday gifting.

Note: Ensure the mix is fully cooled before drizzling chocolate to prevent melting and clumping.

Nut-Free Version

Replace almonds and cashews with extra pumpkin and sunflower seeds to make this mix safe for nut allergies. The seeds still provide great crunch and nutrition, while the cranberries add sweetness.

Note: Roast seeds slightly less time than nuts to avoid burning due to smaller size.

Tropical Twist

Add dried pineapple chunks and toasted coconut flakes to the mix for a bright, tropical flavor. This brings a refreshing contrast to the warm spices and makes the snack feel summery even in fall.

Note: Add coconut flakes after roasting to prevent burning and keep them crisp.

Savory Herb Mix

Swap maple syrup for olive oil and toss nuts with rosemary, thyme, and garlic powder before roasting. This savory version is perfect for cheese boards or cocktail parties where a salty snack is preferred.

Note: Roast at the same temperature but reduce time slightly to prevent herbs from burning.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the mix in an airtight container at room temperature for up to two weeks. Keep it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to maintain crunch and freshness. Avoid humid areas to prevent clumping or mold.

Refrigerator

Refrigerate in a sealed container for up to one month. The mix may lose some crunchiness in the fridge but will still taste great. Let it come to room temperature before serving for best texture.

Freezer

Freeze the mix in a freezer-safe container or bag for up to three months. To prevent freezer burn, remove as much air as possible before sealing. Thaw at room temperature and toss gently to restore texture.

Reheating

No reheating is needed for this snack mix. If you prefer a warm snack, spread a portion on a baking sheet and heat in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes to refresh crunch. Avoid microwaving as it can make nuts chewy or soggy.

Make Ahead

Prepare the nut and seed roasting and coating steps 1-2 days ahead and store in an airtight container. Add dried cranberries just before serving to keep their texture fresh. This saves time and keeps the mix tasting vibrant.

FAQ

Can I use roasted nuts instead of raw nuts?
Using pre-roasted nuts is possible but not ideal because they can overcook or burn during the second roast with the syrup coating. Raw nuts allow you to control roasting time and flavor development better, resulting in a fresher, crunchier mix.
What if I don’t have coconut oil? Can I substitute it?
Yes, you can substitute coconut oil with other neutral oils like avocado or light olive oil. Butter works too but adds a different flavor and can brown faster. Coconut oil’s high smoke point and subtle flavor make it a great choice, but alternatives will still work.
How do I make this mix gluten-free?
This recipe is naturally gluten-free as it contains only nuts, seeds, dried fruit, and natural sweeteners. Just ensure your spices and dried cranberries are certified gluten-free to avoid cross-contamination.
Can I add other dried fruits?
Absolutely! Feel free to add raisins, dried cherries, or chopped dried apricots. Add dried fruits after roasting to preserve their texture. Keep in mind that sweeter fruits will alter the overall flavor balance, so adjust maple syrup accordingly.
How crunchy will the mix stay after storage?
Stored properly in an airtight container at room temperature, the mix stays crunchy for up to two weeks. Refrigeration or freezing may soften the nuts slightly but won’t ruin the snack. Refresh crunch by briefly warming in the oven if desired.
Can I make this mix spicy?
Yes! Try adding cayenne pepper or chili powder to the syrup mixture before coating the nuts. Start with a small amount to avoid overpowering the sweetness. This adds a nice warm heat that complements the cinnamon and maple syrup.
Is it okay to skip the maple syrup?
Skipping the maple syrup removes the sweet glaze and caramelization, resulting in a more neutral, nutty flavor. You can substitute with honey or agave if desired, but the roasting step and coating help bind the spices either way.
Why is it important to cool the mix before storing?
Cooling prevents condensation inside the storage container, which can make the nuts soggy and promote mold growth. Letting the mix cool completely ensures it stays crisp and fresh longer.
