Cozy Cheesy Tomato Pie with Buttery Crust and Fresh Herbs

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 2, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Medium Difficulty

This tomato pie is pure comfort with its buttery crust and layers of melty cheese and fresh tomatoes. I love adding a sprinkle of fresh herbs to brighten it up. Perfect for a laid-back dinner or weekend brunch!

Introduction

I stumbled upon this cheesy tomato pie recipe a few summers ago when my garden was overflowing with ripe tomatoes, and I wanted something more exciting than a simple salad. This pie quickly became a family favorite because it’s just so comforting yet fresh. The buttery, flaky crust paired with layers of juicy tomatoes and melty cheese is honestly one of the best combos I’ve made. What sets my version apart is the touch of fresh herbs—basil and thyme—that add a subtle brightness to balance the richness. Plus, I use a mix of cheddar and mozzarella for that perfect gooey texture and sharp flavor. I've tried making it without mayo in the filling before, but that extra creaminess really helps bind everything and keeps the pie from feeling dry. When it comes out of the oven, it’s golden and bubbling, with the scent of fresh herbs filling the kitchen. It’s great for casual dinners, potlucks, or even brunch, and leftovers are just as good reheated. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I make anytime I want something cozy but not heavy. I think you’ll love how easy it is to put together and how impressive it looks on the table.

What You'll Need

One 9-inch pie, 6 servings

  • 1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1/2 cup cold unsalted butter, cubed
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 3-4 tablespoons ice water
  • 4 large ripe tomatoes, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt, divided
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 cup shredded sharp cheddar cheese
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons chopped fresh basil
  • 1 tablespoon chopped fresh thyme

How To Do It

Step 1
To make the crust, combine the sifted flour and salt in a large bowl. Add the cold, cubed butter and use a pastry cutter or your fingers to work the butter into the flour until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces. This step is crucial because those butter bits create steam during baking, resulting in a flaky crust. Be careful not to overwork the dough, or it will become tough. Once the butter is incorporated, slowly add ice water, one tablespoon at a time, mixing gently until the dough just comes together. You want it moist enough to hold but not sticky.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough holds together when pressed but isn’t sticky or wet
Step 2
Form the dough into a disk, wrap it in plastic, and chill it in the fridge for at least 30 minutes. Chilling firms up the butter and relaxes the gluten in the flour, which helps prevent shrinkage during baking and keeps the crust tender. Skipping this step often leads to a tough or shrunken crust, so don’t rush it! When chilled, roll the dough out on a lightly floured surface into a 12-inch circle to fit your 9-inch pie pan. Transfer it carefully, trimming any excess dough and crimping the edges for a rustic look.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough is firm and easy to roll without cracking
Step 3
Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). While the oven warms, slice your tomatoes about 1/4-inch thick. Lay them out on paper towels and sprinkle with 1 teaspoon salt to draw out excess moisture. This step prevents the pie from becoming soggy. Let them sit for 10-15 minutes, then gently pat dry with more paper towels. Removing the tomato juice concentrates their flavor and helps keep the crust crisp.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tomato slices feel less wet and have released some liquid
Step 4
In a small bowl, mix together the mayonnaise, black pepper, dried oregano, chopped fresh basil, and thyme. This mixture will add creaminess and herbaceous notes to the pie. Spread a thin layer of this mayo-herb mixture over the bottom of the pie crust. It acts as a moisture barrier and flavor base, enhancing every bite without overpowering the fresh tomatoes.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even, thin coating of herb mayo spread on crust
Step 5
Layer half of the tomato slices over the mayo spread, slightly overlapping them. Sprinkle half of the shredded cheddar and mozzarella evenly over the tomatoes. Then repeat with the remaining tomato slices and cheeses. The layering ensures every slice has a perfect balance of juicy tomato and melty cheese, creating that signature gooey texture. Don’t overcrowd the pie; it needs room to bake evenly.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese is evenly distributed and tomatoes are layered neatly
Step 6
Bake the pie in the preheated oven for 30 to 35 minutes. During baking, the butter in the crust melts and creates steam, giving the crust a flaky texture. The cheese melts and bubbles, and the tomatoes soften but still hold their shape. Keep an eye on the crust edges; if they brown too quickly, cover them with foil to prevent burning. The pie is ready when the crust is golden and the cheese is bubbly and lightly browned.
⏱ Time: 30-35 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden crust edges and bubbly, slightly browned cheese on top
Step 7
Once baked, let the tomato pie cool for about 10 minutes before slicing. This resting time allows the filling to set, so it won’t spill out when cut. The flavors meld beautifully during this time, making each bite savory and fresh. Use a sharp knife and cut into wedges for serving.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling is set but still warm and cheese is slightly firm
Step 8
For an extra touch, garnish the pie with more fresh basil or thyme right before serving. This adds a fresh, bright contrast to the rich cheese and buttery crust. It’s these small touches that elevate the dish from simple to special. Plus, the aroma of fresh herbs just makes it irresistible.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fresh herbs visibly vibrant and fragrant on top
Step 9
Serve the pie warm alongside a crisp green salad or a light soup for a complete meal. This pie also makes great leftovers, perfect for lunch the next day. If you want to reheat, do so gently in the oven to keep the crust crisp. Avoid microwaving as it can make the crust soggy. Enjoy the comforting flavors and the flaky texture with every bite!
⏱ Time: 0 minutes
✓ Doneness: Warm pie with crisp crust and melty cheese

Pro Tips

Always chill your pie dough before rolling it out.
Chilling solidifies the butter and relaxes the gluten, which prevents the crust from shrinking and becoming tough. If you skip this step, the dough can become elastic and difficult to roll, resulting in a dense crust.
Salt and drain your tomato slices before assembling the pie.
Tomatoes carry a lot of water that can make your pie soggy. Salting draws out excess moisture through osmosis. Draining and patting them dry ensures a crisp crust and concentrated tomato flavor.
Use a mixture of cheddar and mozzarella cheese.
Cheddar provides sharp, bold flavor while mozzarella melts beautifully for that gooey texture. Using only one cheese can make the pie either too mild or lacking in meltiness.
Don’t skip the mayo layer under the tomatoes.
Mayonnaise acts as a moisture barrier and adds creaminess, keeping the crust crisp and the filling luscious. It’s a simple ingredient that makes a big difference.
Bake at 375°F and watch the crust edges closely.
This temperature balances even cooking of the crust and bubbling of the cheese. If edges brown too fast, cover them with foil to prevent burning while the rest bakes through.
Let the pie rest before slicing.
Resting allows the filling to set, preventing it from spilling out when cut. It also lets flavors meld, giving a better taste experience.
Use fresh herbs for garnish and in the filling.
Fresh basil and thyme add brightness and aroma that lift the richness of the cheese and butter, creating a more complex and fresh flavor profile.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not chilling the dough before baking

Why it happens Many rush through the process wanting to bake immediately, assuming it doesn’t affect the crust much.

Fix Make sure to chill the dough for at least 30 minutes. This step solidifies the butter and relaxes gluten, preventing shrinkage and tough crust. If you’re short on time, pop it in the freezer for 15 minutes but watch carefully to avoid freezing solid.

Skipping the tomato salting and draining

Why it happens It seems like an extra step that can be skipped when in a hurry or when tomatoes look firm.

Fix Don’t skip this. Salt the tomatoes and let them drain on paper towels for 10-15 minutes. This reduces moisture and prevents a soggy pie crust, ensuring a better texture overall.

Using pre-shredded cheese

Why it happens Pre-shredded cheese is convenient and widely available.

Fix Grate your own cheese whenever possible. Pre-shredded cheese contains anti-caking agents that can prevent proper melting, resulting in a less gooey texture.

Overfilling the pie with tomatoes and cheese

Why it happens It’s tempting to pile on ingredients for more flavor.

Fix Stick to the recommended quantities. Overfilling can cause uneven cooking and a soggy crust. Proper layering ensures balanced flavor and texture.

Not covering crust edges if browning too fast

Why it happens Many overlook the crust edges while focusing on the filling.

Fix If edges brown quickly, cover them loosely with foil halfway through baking. This prevents burning while allowing the rest of the pie to cook fully.

Variations

Spicy Tomato Pie

Add a pinch of red pepper flakes to the mayo-herb mixture and sprinkle sliced jalapeños over the tomatoes for a spicy kick. This variation adds warmth and depth without overpowering the fresh tomato flavor.

Note: Monitor baking time closely as the jalapeños can soften quickly; the crust and cheese melt the same as the original.

Vegan Tomato Pie

Use a vegan butter substitute for the crust and replace the cheeses with your favorite plant-based cheese alternatives. Swap mayonnaise for vegan mayo to keep the creamy texture. The pie will be lighter but still flavorful and satisfying.

Note: Vegan cheeses may melt differently, so adjust baking time to prevent overbrowning.

Herbed Goat Cheese Tomato Pie

Replace the cheddar and mozzarella with creamy goat cheese mixed with fresh herbs. This gives the pie a tangy, rich flavor that pairs beautifully with the sweet tomatoes and buttery crust.

Note: Goat cheese doesn’t melt like mozzarella, so expect a creamier, less stretchy filling.

Gluten-Free Tomato Pie

Use a gluten-free pie crust mix or make your own with almond flour and tapioca starch. The filling remains the same. This is perfect for those avoiding gluten but wanting all the comfort of the original pie.

Note: Gluten-free crusts can be more fragile; chill thoroughly and handle gently when rolling.

Tomato and Caramelized Onion Pie

Add a layer of caramelized onions beneath the tomatoes for a sweet, savory depth. The onions complement the cheese and herbs, adding complexity to the flavor profile.

Note: Caramelize onions slowly over low heat to avoid bitterness, and cool before layering.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This pie can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Use a pie dome or loosely cover with foil to prevent drying out. Beyond this, refrigerate to keep it fresh.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container or wrap tightly with plastic wrap for up to 3 days. The crust may lose some crispness but reheating in the oven restores texture. The flavors often deepen overnight.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled pie tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating to maintain texture and flavor.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes until warmed through and crust is crisp again. Avoid microwaving as it can make the crust soggy. Adding a splash of water before reheating is unnecessary if wrapped well.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the pie dough and herb mayo mixture 1-2 days ahead and keep chilled. Slice and salt the tomatoes the day of baking for best freshness. Assemble and bake just before serving for optimal texture.

FAQ

Can I use store-bought pie crust instead of making my own?
Absolutely! Store-bought crust saves time and works fine here. Just choose a high-quality crust and keep it chilled before baking. You might miss some of the buttery flakiness of homemade dough, but it’s a great shortcut.
What if I don’t have mayonnaise? Can I skip it?
You can substitute with sour cream or Greek yogurt to add creaminess and moisture control. Skipping it entirely may make the pie wetter and less cohesive, so some kind of creamy layer is recommended.
How do I prevent the crust from getting soggy?
Salting and draining the tomatoes is key to removing excess moisture. Also, spreading the mayo layer acts as a moisture barrier. Baking at the right temperature and chilling the dough help maintain a crisp crust.
Can I use other cheeses besides cheddar and mozzarella?
Yes, cheeses like Gruyère, fontina, or Monterey Jack can work well. Just make sure to use a combination that melts nicely and complements the tomatoes without overpowering them.
Is it okay to use canned tomatoes?
Fresh tomatoes are best for texture and flavor in this pie. Canned tomatoes tend to be too watery and can make the crust soggy. If you must use canned, drain them thoroughly and pat dry.
How long can I store leftover pie?
Store leftovers covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Reheat in the oven for best texture. The flavors improve after resting but avoid keeping it beyond 3 days for safety.
Can I prepare this pie ahead of time?
Yes! Prepare the crust and herb mayo up to 2 days in advance and keep refrigerated. Salt the tomatoes the day of baking for freshness. Assemble and bake just before serving.
What’s the best way to reheat the pie?
Reheat in a 350°F oven for 10-15 minutes until warmed through and crust is crisp. Avoid microwaving as it can make the crust soggy and cheese rubbery.
