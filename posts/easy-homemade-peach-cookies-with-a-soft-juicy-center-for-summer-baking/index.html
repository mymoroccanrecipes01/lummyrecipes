Soft and Juicy Homemade Peach Cookies That Taste Like Summer Bliss

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 16, 2026
15 min Prep Time
20 min Time
12 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These peach cookies are a summer dream—soft, tender, and bursting with fresh peach flavor. The trick? Using ripe peaches that keep the center moist and juicy. Perfect for a sunny afternoon snack or sharing with friends!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this peach cookie recipe on a sweltering summer afternoon when I had an abundance of ripe peaches that needed using fast. I was skeptical at first—cookies with fresh fruit can sometimes turn out soggy or flat—but this recipe blew me away. The secret is folding in the diced peaches gently so they stay juicy without making the dough too wet. I also like to use a mix of granulated and brown sugar to balance sweetness and add a subtle caramel note. The finished cookies come out soft, tender, and bursting with peach flavor, not to mention they smell amazing as they bake. My family was hooked after the first batch, and it quickly became our go-to summer treat. If you’re after a cookie that’s a little different but still super comforting, this one’s for you. Plus, it’s pretty forgiving, so even if you’re not a baking pro, you’ll get great results. Expect warm, golden cookies with a moist center and just enough sweetness to satisfy without overpowering the fresh peaches. I think you’ll love how they capture the essence of summer in every bite.

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 cookies

  • 1 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup fresh peaches, peeled and diced
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by preheating your oven to 350°F (175°C) and lining a baking sheet with parchment paper. This ensures your cookies bake evenly and don’t stick. Proper oven temperature is key here—too hot and the cookies brown too fast on the outside while staying raw inside; too low and they spread too much. Parchment paper also helps achieve a nice bottom crust without burning.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oven fully preheated to 350°F and baking sheet ready with parchment
Step 2
In a large bowl, cream together the softened butter, granulated sugar, and brown sugar until light and fluffy. Use a hand mixer or stand mixer on medium speed for about 3-4 minutes. This step incorporates air, which helps your cookies rise and get that tender crumb. If you rush this, your cookies could turn out dense.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks pale, smooth, and creamy
Step 3
Beat in the eggs one at a time, followed by the vanilla extract. Make sure each egg is fully incorporated before adding the next. This helps create a smooth batter and avoids curdling. The vanilla adds a lovely aroma that complements the peaches beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth and glossy without streaks of egg
Step 4
In a separate bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, and cinnamon if using. This ensures even distribution of the leavening agents, which helps your cookies rise evenly. Sifting or whisking also helps prevent clumps in the dough.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are well combined and fluffy
Step 5
Gradually add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture, mixing on low speed just until combined. Overmixing develops gluten, which can make cookies tough instead of soft and tender. Stop mixing as soon as you see no more flour streaks.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough comes together smoothly but still looks soft
Step 6
Fold in the diced fresh peaches gently with a spatula. Be careful not to overwork the dough here, or the peaches will break down too much and make the dough soggy. The goal is to keep chunks of peach intact so the cookies stay juicy and flavorful.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peach pieces are evenly distributed without too much juice in the dough
Step 7
Scoop tablespoon-sized balls of dough onto the prepared baking sheet, spacing them about 2 inches apart to allow spreading. If you want uniform cookies, use a cookie scoop. Slightly flatten each ball with your fingers for an even bake and pretty shape.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dough balls evenly spaced and slightly flattened
Step 8
Bake the cookies for 18-20 minutes or until the edges are golden but the centers still look soft. The cookies will continue to set as they cool, so don’t overbake or they’ll dry out. You’ll know they’re done when you see a light golden color and a gentle spring back when touched.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Edges are golden, centers look set but soft
Step 9
Remove the baking sheet from the oven and let the cookies cool on it for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. This rest allows the cookies to firm up and prevents them from breaking apart. If you move them too early, they might crumble because of the juicy peaches inside.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cookies hold their shape when lifted and feel slightly firm

Pro Tips

Don’t skip creaming the butter and sugars thoroughly.
Creaming incorporates air that helps the cookies rise and develop a tender crumb. If you rush this step or don’t cream enough, the cookies can turn dense and flat.
Fold in the peaches gently at the end.
Overmixing after adding peaches can break down the fruit too much, releasing excess juice and making the dough soggy. Gentle folding keeps peach chunks intact and juicy.
Use room temperature ingredients for even mixing.
Cold eggs or butter don’t blend well and can cause uneven texture. Room temp ingredients create a smooth batter that bakes uniformly.
Don’t overbake the cookies; watch the edges closely.
Cookies continue cooking after removal, so pulling them out when edges are golden but centers still soft ensures a tender, moist bite.
Peel peaches before dicing to avoid tough skin in cookies.
Peach skin can be chewy and detract from the soft texture. Peeling ensures each bite is tender and consistent.
Chill the dough briefly if peaches feel very juicy.
Chilling firms the dough, making it easier to handle and helping prevent spreading during baking.
Use parchment paper or silicone mats to prevent sticking.
This protects the delicate cookies and promotes even baking without burning the bottoms.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding peaches too early or mixing too aggressively.

Why it happens Some cooks try to mix peaches in with the dry ingredients or overwork the dough, thinking it will distribute flavor better.

Fix Always fold in diced peaches gently at the very end of mixing to keep fruit intact and prevent excess moisture from leaking into the dough.

Overbaking the cookies until fully firm.

Why it happens It’s tempting to wait for cookies to look completely set, but peaches keep centers moist, so they’ll look softer even when done.

Fix Remove cookies when edges are golden but centers still look slightly soft; they will firm up as they cool.

Using underripe or overly juicy peaches.

Why it happens Sometimes only imperfect peaches are on hand, or people try to shortcut by using canned fruit.

Fix Choose ripe but firm peaches, peel and pat dry if very juicy to maintain dough texture and flavor.

Skipping creaming step or not softening butter.

Why it happens In a rush, some skip proper butter preparation.

Fix Always use softened butter and cream with sugars properly to get light, airy cookie dough.

Not spacing cookies properly on the baking sheet.

Why it happens Crowding cookies can cause them to merge and bake unevenly.

Fix Leave at least 2 inches between dough balls to allow spreading and proper heat circulation.

Variations

Peach and White Chocolate Chip Cookies

Add 1/2 cup white chocolate chips to the dough along with the peaches for a sweet, creamy contrast. The white chocolate melts into pockets, complementing the juicy fruit perfectly.

Note: White chocolate chips soften during baking but keep their shape, so fold them in gently with the peaches to avoid breaking.

Spiced Peach Oat Cookies

Swap 1 cup of flour for rolled oats and add 1/2 teaspoon ground nutmeg and cloves for a warm, rustic flavor. The oats add chewiness, and the spices deepen the peach’s sweetness.

Note: Oats absorb moisture differently, so you may want to chill dough a bit longer before baking to prevent spreading.

Gluten-Free Peach Cookies

Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend instead of all-purpose. These cookies keep their soft texture and juicy centers, perfect for gluten-sensitive friends.

Note: Gluten-free doughs can be more fragile; handle gently and consider adding an extra egg yolk for binding.

Peach and Almond Cookies

Add 1/2 teaspoon almond extract along with vanilla and sprinkle sliced almonds on top before baking for a nutty twist that pairs wonderfully with peaches.

Note: Almond extract is potent—start with a small amount to avoid overpowering the peach flavor.

Vegan Peach Cookies

Replace butter with vegan margarine and eggs with flax eggs (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg). The cookies remain soft and juicy, perfect for plant-based diets.

Note: Flax eggs add moisture and binding but can slightly change texture; chill dough before baking for best results.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Cookies can be stored at room temperature in an airtight container for up to 2 days without losing freshness. Keep them away from direct sunlight or heat sources to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store cookies in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. They may firm up a bit; bring to room temperature before serving or warm slightly in the microwave for 10 seconds.

Freezer

Freeze cookies in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer-safe container or bag for up to 3 months. Thaw at room temperature or warm gently in the oven to restore softness.

Reheating

Warm cookies in a preheated oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 10-15 seconds. Adding a small splash of water on a paper towel nearby during microwaving helps retain moisture.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the dough 1 day ahead, wrap tightly, and refrigerate. Shape and bake the next day for fresh cookies. This resting time enhances flavor and texture.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches for this recipe?
Frozen peaches tend to release extra water when thawed, which can make the dough soggy. If you must use frozen, thaw completely, drain excess liquid, and pat dry before folding into the dough to minimize moisture.
Why are my cookies spreading too much?
Overly soft butter, warm dough, or too little flour can cause spreading. Make sure to use softened (not melted) butter and chill dough briefly if your kitchen is warm. Also, measure flour accurately and avoid overmixing.
Can I substitute the butter with oil?
Butter provides flavor and structure that oil can’t fully replicate. Using oil may result in greasier, flatter cookies. If needed, use a mild-flavored oil and reduce the quantity slightly, but results will differ.
How ripe should the peaches be?
Peaches should be ripe enough to be sweet and soft but still firm enough to hold their shape when diced. Overripe peaches can make the dough too wet and affect cookie texture.
Can I make these cookies dairy-free?
Yes! Use a dairy-free butter substitute like vegan margarine or coconut oil. Coconut oil will add a slight coconut flavor, so choose based on your preference.
How do I store leftover cookies?
Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days or refrigerate for up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze as described in storage tips.
Should I peel the peaches?
Peeling is recommended because peach skin can be tough and chewy. You can blanch peaches in hot water briefly to loosen skins or use a vegetable peeler.
Can I add spices like cinnamon or nutmeg?
Absolutely! Adding 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon or a pinch of nutmeg complements the peaches nicely and adds warmth to the flavor.
