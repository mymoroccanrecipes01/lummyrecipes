One-Pot Garlic Herb Chicken and Rice for Busy Weeknights

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 10, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This one-pot garlic herb chicken and rice is a lifesaver on hectic evenings. The secret is layering garlic and fresh herbs for deep flavor without fuss. It’s wholesome, comforting, and ready in under an hour.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this one-pot chicken and rice recipe when I was juggling work and family dinners, desperate for something quick yet satisfying. What hooked me was the simplicity: everything cooks together, which means less time washing dishes and more time enjoying the meal. Unlike other versions, I layer minced garlic and fresh herbs right at the start, allowing their flavors to bloom as the rice cooks. One trick I learned was to sear the chicken thighs until golden before adding the rice and broth — it locks in juices and adds a lovely caramelized note. The final dish is tender, fragrant, and comforting, with fluffy rice soaking up all those garlicky, herby juices. It’s become a staple in my weeknight rotation, especially when I want something nourishing but don’t have much time to fuss. Honestly, the first time I tried it, I thought it might be too simple, but the flavor is surprisingly rich and layered. Plus, it’s so forgiving—if you forget a step or the timing’s off, it still turns out great. I’m sure you’ll love how this one-pot wonder feels like a warm hug after a busy day.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 1.5 pounds boneless skinless chicken thighs, trimmed
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 cup long grain white rice, rinsed
  • 2 cups low sodium chicken broth
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh parsley, chopped (for garnish)

How To Do It

Step 1
Heat the olive oil in a large deep skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the chicken thighs in a single layer, making sure not to crowd the pan. Sear them for about 4-5 minutes on each side until they develop a golden-brown crust. This step is crucial because it locks in moisture and builds flavor through caramelization. Avoid moving the chicken too much during searing to get that nice crust. Once browned, remove the chicken and set aside. The fond (browned bits) left in the pan will add depth to the dish.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken is golden brown on both sides and releases easily from the pan
Step 2
Lower the heat to medium and add the diced onion and minced garlic to the same pan. Stir frequently and cook until the onions are translucent and the garlic is fragrant, about 3-4 minutes. This step softens the aromatics and releases their flavor into the oil, which will infuse the rice later. Be careful not to burn the garlic, as it can turn bitter and ruin the dish’s balance.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions are soft and translucent, garlic is golden and aromatic
Step 3
Add the rinsed rice to the pan and stir well to coat each grain with the garlicky onion mixture. Toasting the rice slightly for about 2 minutes enhances its nutty flavor and helps prevent it from becoming mushy. Stir constantly during this step to keep the rice from sticking and burning.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice grains are slightly translucent at the edges but mostly opaque
Step 4
Pour in the chicken broth and sprinkle in the dried thyme, oregano, salt, and pepper. Stir everything to combine and scrape the bottom of the pan to loosen any stuck bits. This deglazing step incorporates that rich fond into the liquid, boosting the flavor of the entire dish. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer before moving on.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Broth is simmering gently with small bubbles around the edges
Step 5
Nestle the seared chicken thighs back into the pan on top of the rice and broth. Cover the pan with a tight-fitting lid and reduce the heat to low. Let it cook undisturbed for 20-25 minutes. This slow simmer allows the rice to absorb the broth fully and the chicken to finish cooking through without drying out. Avoid lifting the lid during cooking, as steam is essential for perfectly tender rice.
⏱ Time: 25 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice is tender and liquid is absorbed; chicken registers 165°F internally
Step 6
Once cooking is complete, remove the pan from heat and let it sit covered for 5 minutes. This resting period lets the steam redistribute, making the rice fluffier and the chicken juicier. Resist the urge to dig in immediately; patience pays off here.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Steam rises gently when lid is lifted; rice is fluffy and separated
Step 7
Fluff the rice gently with a fork to separate grains without breaking them. Carefully lift the chicken pieces and slice or serve whole. Spoon the rice onto plates and top with chicken. Garnish with freshly chopped parsley to add a pop of color and fresh flavor that cuts through the richness.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice is light and fluffy; parsley adds vibrant green contrast
Step 8
Taste the dish and adjust seasoning if needed, adding a pinch more salt or pepper. The flavors should be balanced with the garlic and herbs shining through. If you want a little extra brightness, a squeeze of fresh lemon juice at the end works beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced savory flavor with fresh herbal notes
Step 9
Serve the one-pot garlic herb chicken and rice hot alongside a simple green salad or steamed veggies for a complete meal. The leftovers reheat well, making it perfect for meal prep or busy days when you want homemade comfort without the hassle.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Dish is steaming hot and inviting on the plate

Pro Tips

Don’t skip searing the chicken before simmering.
Searing creates a golden crust through the Maillard reaction, locking in juices and adding deep, savory flavor. Skipping this step results in bland, pale chicken and less flavorful broth.
Rinse your rice thoroughly before cooking.
Rinsing removes excess surface starch, preventing the rice from becoming sticky or gummy. This helps achieve fluffy, separate grains that soak up the broth beautifully.
Use a tight-fitting lid during simmering.
A tight lid traps steam, enabling even cooking and proper absorption of liquid by the rice. Lifting the lid too often lets steam escape, causing uneven texture and longer cooking time.
Let the dish rest off heat before serving.
Resting allows moisture to redistribute, making the rice fluffier and the chicken juicier. Serving immediately can result in wetter rice and less tender meat.
Add fresh herbs at the end for brightness.
Fresh parsley or similar herbs add color and a fresh flavor contrast that balances the rich, savory base. Adding herbs too early can dull their freshness.
Use low sodium broth to control saltiness.
Broth can vary in salt content, so starting with low sodium lets you season the dish precisely. Over-salted broth can overpower the delicate flavors.
Avoid overcooking the rice by monitoring simmer time.
Overcooked rice becomes mushy and loses texture. Follow timing carefully and check rice tenderness close to the end to ensure perfect doneness.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the sear on the chicken

Why it happens Many assume simmering alone is enough to cook chicken fully and add flavor, so they skip searing to save time.

Fix Always sear chicken first to develop flavor and texture. It only takes a few minutes but makes a big difference in taste and juiciness.

Not rinsing the rice before cooking

Why it happens People often overlook this step, assuming rice is clean and ready to use straight from the package.

Fix Rinse rice under cold water until the water runs clear to remove excess starch. This prevents clumping and results in fluffier rice.

Lifting the lid frequently during simmering

Why it happens Curiosity or impatience makes cooks check the dish repeatedly, disrupting the cooking process.

Fix Resist opening the lid while cooking; trapped steam is essential for even cooking and rice absorption. Only check near the end of the cooking time.

Using chicken breasts without adjusting cooking time

Why it happens Breasts cook faster but some use the same timing as thighs, causing dryness.

Fix If using breasts, reduce simmer time and monitor internal temperature to avoid overcooking and dryness.

Burning the garlic during sauté

Why it happens Garlic cooks quickly and can burn if the heat is too high or it’s left unattended.

Fix Cook garlic on medium heat and stir frequently. Remove from heat if it starts turning dark brown to prevent bitterness.

Variations

Spicy One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Add a teaspoon of smoked paprika and a pinch of cayenne pepper to the garlic and onion step for a smoky, spicy kick. You can also toss in chopped jalapeños with the onions. The dish gains warmth and complexity, perfect if you love a bit of heat.

Note: Adjust broth quantity slightly if adding fresh peppers to maintain proper rice texture.

Creamy One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Stir in 1/4 cup of cream or coconut milk after removing from heat for a luscious, creamy texture. This variation adds richness and smoothness without changing the core flavors.

Note: Add cream off heat to prevent curdling and maintain smooth consistency.

Vegetable-Packed One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Include diced carrots, peas, and bell peppers with the onions to boost nutrition and color. This makes the dish heartier and adds a fresh crunch, making it a great all-in-one meal.

Note: Add firmer vegetables earlier and softer ones closer to the end of cooking to avoid overcooking.

Lemon Herb One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Add zest and juice of one lemon along with the herbs for a bright, zesty flavor that cuts through the richness. This twist feels fresh and summery, perfect for lighter meals.

Note: Add lemon juice after cooking to preserve its fresh acidity and brightness.

Brown Rice One-Pot Chicken and Rice

Swap white rice for brown rice for a nuttier flavor and more fiber. Since brown rice takes longer to cook, increase the broth and simmer time accordingly, about 40-45 minutes total.

Note: Monitor liquid levels carefully as brown rice absorbs more water and takes longer to soften.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This dish can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so it’s best to refrigerate promptly.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 4 days. The rice may firm up slightly when cold, so reheat gently with a splash of broth or water to restore moisture and fluffiness.

Freezer

Freeze in airtight containers for up to 3 months. To prevent freezer burn, wrap containers tightly or use vacuum seal bags. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat on the stovetop over low heat with a splash of broth, stirring occasionally until warmed through. Alternatively, microwave covered with a damp paper towel to retain moisture. Avoid overheating to prevent dry chicken and mushy rice.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the chicken and sauté the aromatics a day ahead, then refrigerate separately. Assemble the rice and broth just before cooking to keep rice texture optimal. This saves time on busy nights.

FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?
Yes, you can substitute chicken breasts, but they cook faster and can dry out if overcooked. Reduce the simmering time by about 5-7 minutes and monitor internal temperature to keep them juicy.
What if I don’t have chicken broth?
You can use water in a pinch, but the dish will be less flavorful. To compensate, add extra herbs and a pinch of salt. Homemade or store-bought broth adds richness and depth that water lacks.
Can I make this recipe vegan or vegetarian?
To make it vegan, substitute the chicken with hearty vegetables or plant-based protein like chickpeas, and use vegetable broth instead of chicken broth. Adjust cooking times accordingly for the protein chosen.
How do I prevent the rice from burning on the bottom?
Use a heavy-bottomed pan or Dutch oven to distribute heat evenly. Keep the heat low during simmering and avoid lifting the lid too often, which can cause uneven cooking and burning.
Can I add other vegetables to this dish?
Absolutely! Diced carrots, peas, bell peppers, or spinach work well. Add firmer veggies earlier in the cooking process and delicate ones near the end to avoid overcooking.
How do I store leftovers properly?
Cool leftovers quickly and store in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat gently with a splash of broth to restore moisture and prevent dryness.
What’s the best way to reheat this dish?
Reheat on the stovetop over low heat with a bit of broth, stirring occasionally. Microwaving covered with a damp paper towel also works well to keep the rice moist.
Can I prepare this recipe ahead of time?
Yes, you can prep the chicken and aromatics a day ahead. Refrigerate separately and combine with rice and broth just before cooking for the best texture.
