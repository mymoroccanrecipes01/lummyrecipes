Crispy Air Fryer Chicken Thighs with Garlic Herb Butter

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These air fryer chicken thighs come out perfectly crispy on the outside and juicy inside, all thanks to a simple garlic herb butter finish. Great for quick dinners or when you want something fuss-free but impressive.

Introduction

I first tried air fryer chicken thighs a couple of years ago when I wanted a fuss-free dinner that still felt special. The skin comes out so crispy, and the meat stays juicy — it’s honestly a game changer for weeknight meals. What makes this version stand out is the garlic herb butter I smear on right after cooking. It melts into the hot chicken, giving it this amazing rich flavor and a subtle herbal punch. I’ve tweaked the seasoning over time, adding smoked paprika and thyme to give it a slightly smoky depth that pairs perfectly with the butter. One thing I learned the hard way is to pat the chicken dry before seasoning — it really helps the skin crisp up in the air fryer. When it’s done, you get this golden, crackly skin with tender meat underneath, and the garlic butter just brings everything together beautifully. It’s my go-to when I want something quick but comforting, and my family always asks for seconds!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs, trimmed
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
  • 3 tablespoons unsalted butter, softened
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh parsley, chopped

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by patting the chicken thighs completely dry with paper towels. This removes excess moisture that can prevent the skin from crisping properly in the air fryer. Then, rub the olive oil over each piece to help the seasoning stick and promote browning. Be sure to get the oil under the skin if possible — that layer of fat will render and crisp beautifully. Avoid skipping the drying step; moist skin will steam instead of crisping, leaving you with soggy chicken.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken skin feels dry to the touch before seasoning
Step 2
Mix the smoked paprika, garlic powder, dried thyme, salt, and pepper in a small bowl. Sprinkle this seasoning blend evenly over all sides of the chicken thighs, pressing gently so it adheres well. The paprika adds a smoky warmth, while the thyme brings a subtle earthiness that complements the garlic butter later. Don’t rush this step — even seasoning makes for a balanced flavor in every bite.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken thighs are evenly coated with a reddish herb-spiced layer
Step 3
Preheat your air fryer to 400°F (200°C) for about 3-5 minutes. Preheating ensures the chicken starts cooking immediately and helps develop that crispy skin. If you skip this, the chicken might cook unevenly or take longer to crisp up. A hot air fryer basket also prevents sticking and promotes even browning.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Air fryer display shows 400°F and preheat cycle is complete
Step 4
Place the chicken thighs skin-side down in a single layer inside the air fryer basket. Make sure they don’t overlap or touch too much, or the hot air won’t circulate properly, causing uneven cooking. Cooking skin-side down first helps render the fat and start crisping the skin from the bottom up.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken is arranged in a single layer with space around each piece
Step 5
Cook the chicken at 400°F for 12 minutes skin-side down. This initial phase crisps the skin nicely. Keep an eye out for a golden-brown color. Avoid opening the fryer too often as it releases heat and extends cooking time. If the skin isn’t browning, your air fryer might need a higher temperature or longer cook time.
⏱ Time: 12 minutes
✓ Doneness: Skin is golden and slightly crisp on the bottom side
Step 6
Flip the chicken thighs carefully using tongs, so the skin side is up. Continue cooking for another 12-15 minutes until the internal temperature reaches 165°F (74°C). Cooking skin-side up now helps the top crisp and the meat finish cooking evenly. Use a meat thermometer for accuracy — undercooked chicken is unsafe, and overcooking dries it out.
⏱ Time: 12-15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Juices run clear and thermometer reads 165°F in the thickest part
Step 7
While the chicken cooks, combine the softened butter, minced garlic, and chopped parsley in a small bowl. Mix until well blended. This garlic herb butter will melt over the hot chicken for an irresistible finish. Fresh garlic gives it a bright punch, while parsley adds freshness and color.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Butter mixture is smooth and well combined with visible parsley bits
Step 8
Once the chicken is done, transfer it to a plate and immediately spread a tablespoon of the garlic herb butter on each piece while it’s still hot. The heat will melt the butter, soaking into the crispy skin and meat for extra flavor and moisture. Don’t skip this step — it transforms the dish from good to unforgettable.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Butter visibly melts and glistens on the chicken surface
Step 9
Let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before serving. Resting allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat, keeping it tender and juicy. Cutting into it right away can cause the juices to run out, drying the chicken. Serve with your favorite sides and enjoy the crispy, flavorful results!
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Chicken feels warm but rests without excessive juice loss when cut

Pro Tips

Always pat the chicken dry before seasoning.
Removing surface moisture is critical to getting a crispy skin. Moisture creates steam during cooking, which prevents the skin from browning and crisping properly. Patting dry ensures the Maillard reaction can occur efficiently, giving you that golden, crunchy exterior.
Preheat your air fryer before adding the chicken.
Preheating ensures the cooking environment is hot right from the start, which helps the skin start crisping immediately. This prevents the chicken from cooking slowly and steaming, which would result in soggy skin and uneven cooking.
Use a meat thermometer to check doneness.
Chicken thighs are safe to eat at 165°F internal temperature. Using a thermometer prevents overcooking, which dries out the meat, or undercooking, which is unsafe. It’s the most reliable way to get juicy, perfectly cooked chicken every time.
Don’t overcrowd the air fryer basket.
Proper air circulation is key in an air fryer. Crowding causes uneven cooking and less crispy skin because the hot air can’t circulate around each piece. Arrange pieces in a single layer with space between them for best results.
Apply the garlic herb butter immediately after cooking.
The residual heat melts the butter right into the skin and meat, enhancing flavor and adding moisture. Waiting too long means the butter won’t melt properly, and you’ll miss out on that luscious finish.
Let the chicken rest before serving.
Resting allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat, making it more tender and juicy. Cutting it immediately after cooking causes the juices to escape, resulting in drier chicken.
Trim excess fat from the chicken thighs.
Removing large fat pieces prevents flare-ups or uneven cooking in the air fryer. It also helps the seasoning stick better and reduces greasy spots, resulting in a cleaner, crispier finish.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the drying step before seasoning.

Why it happens Many assume seasoning right away is fine, but moisture on the skin creates steam during cooking, which prevents crisping.

Fix Always use paper towels to thoroughly pat the chicken dry before applying oil and seasoning. This ensures the skin crisps up properly in the air fryer.

Overcrowding the air fryer basket.

Why it happens It’s tempting to cook everything at once, but too many pieces block air flow.

Fix Cook in batches if necessary, leaving space between pieces to allow hot air circulation for even cooking and crispiness.

Not preheating the air fryer.

Why it happens Some skip preheating to save time, but this causes slower cooking and less crispy skin.

Fix Always preheat your air fryer for 3-5 minutes to ensure the cooking environment is hot and ready.

Not using a thermometer to check doneness.

Why it happens Guessing doneness often leads to undercooked or dry chicken.

Fix Use an instant-read thermometer to confirm the internal temperature reaches 165°F for safe, juicy results.

Applying garlic herb butter too late.

Why it happens Waiting until the chicken cools means the butter won’t melt and infuse properly.

Fix Spread the butter immediately after cooking while the chicken is hot to get the best flavor and moisture.

Variations

Spicy Cajun Air Fryer Chicken

Swap the smoked paprika and thyme for a Cajun seasoning blend with cayenne and oregano for a spicy kick. This variation adds bold heat and a bit of smokiness, perfect if you like your chicken with a punch. The air fryer still crisps it beautifully, and the garlic butter can be replaced with a spicy compound butter for extra flavor.

Note: Spicy seasoning can burn quicker; watch the chicken closely during cooking to avoid charring.

Lemon Herb Butter Chicken

Add fresh lemon zest and a squeeze of lemon juice to the garlic herb butter for a bright, citrusy twist. This lightens the richness and pairs well with fresh sides like green beans or salad. The lemon also helps tenderize the meat slightly and adds a refreshing aroma.

Note: Apply lemon butter right after cooking to preserve the fresh citrus flavor without cooking off the zest.

Honey Mustard Glazed Chicken Thighs

Brush a honey mustard glaze on the chicken during the last 5 minutes of air frying to create a sticky, sweet coating. This variation adds a delicious tangy sweetness that contrasts nicely with the crispy skin. Use equal parts honey and Dijon mustard with a pinch of smoked paprika for a balanced glaze.

Note: Glaze at the end of cooking to prevent burning sugars and to keep the glaze shiny and fresh.

Herb-Crusted Parmesan Chicken

Mix grated Parmesan cheese, Italian seasoning, and breadcrumbs into the seasoning mix for a crispy, cheesy crust. This variation adds texture and a savory depth that pairs well with pasta or roasted veggies. The Parmesan crisps up nicely in the air fryer, creating a delightful crust.

Note: Add the Parmesan and breadcrumb mixture after the initial oil rub to help it stick and crisp.

Asian-Inspired Soy Garlic Chicken

Marinate the chicken thighs in a mixture of soy sauce, minced garlic, ginger, and a touch of honey before air frying. Finish with a drizzle of sesame oil and chopped scallions. This variation delivers a sweet-savory umami flavor with a tender juicy bite, great for serving with rice or noodles.

Note: Pat chicken dry before air frying to maintain crispiness, but allow the marinade to soak in for at least 30 minutes.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Cooked chicken can be safely left out at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely. Make sure to avoid exposure to direct heat or sunlight to prevent bacterial growth.

Refrigerator

Store leftover chicken in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3-4 days. The skin may lose some crispness but reheating in the air fryer or oven restores some texture. Cold leftovers can also be shredded for salads or sandwiches.

Freezer

Freeze cooked chicken thighs in a freezer-safe container or wrapped tightly in foil and plastic wrap for up to 3 months. Prevent freezer burn by removing as much air as possible. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Reheating

Reheat in the air fryer at 350°F for 5-7 minutes to crisp the skin again. Alternatively, use an oven set to 375°F, covered loosely with foil for 10-15 minutes. Avoid microwaving as it will make the skin soggy.

Make Ahead

You can season and prep the chicken thighs 1 day ahead, storing them covered in the fridge. Cook fresh on the day for best crispiness. Alternatively, cook fully and reheat as needed, applying fresh garlic herb butter after reheating.

FAQ

Can I use boneless chicken thighs instead of bone-in?
Yes, you can use boneless thighs, but they will cook faster—usually in about 15-20 minutes at 400°F. Keep in mind boneless thighs may not be as juicy or flavorful as bone-in, and the skin might crisp differently if it’s removed.
What if I don’t have an air fryer? Can I bake this?
Absolutely! You can bake the chicken thighs in a preheated oven at 425°F for about 30-35 minutes, flipping halfway through. You won’t get quite the same crispiness as an air fryer but baking still yields great results.
How do I know when the chicken is done without a thermometer?
If you don’t have a thermometer, check that the juices run clear when you pierce the thickest part and the meat is no longer pink inside. However, a thermometer is the safest and most reliable method to avoid under- or overcooking.
Can I make the garlic herb butter ahead of time?
Yes, you can prepare the garlic herb butter a day or two ahead and store it covered in the fridge. Let it come to room temperature before spreading on hot chicken for best melting and flavor release.
My chicken skin isn’t crispy. What did I do wrong?
Most likely, the chicken wasn’t dried well before cooking or the air fryer basket was overcrowded, which blocks airflow. Also, make sure you preheat the air fryer and cook at a high enough temperature (400°F).
Can I use frozen chicken thighs?
It’s best to thaw chicken thighs completely before air frying for even cooking and safety. Cooking from frozen can result in uneven cooking and less crispy skin.
What sides go well with this chicken?
Roasted veggies, mashed potatoes, steamed green beans, or a fresh salad all pair wonderfully. The garlic herb butter chicken is rich, so lighter sides balance the meal nicely.
Is this recipe gluten-free?
Yes, this recipe uses only naturally gluten-free ingredients. Just make sure your paprika and any other spices are certified gluten-free if you have a sensitivity.
