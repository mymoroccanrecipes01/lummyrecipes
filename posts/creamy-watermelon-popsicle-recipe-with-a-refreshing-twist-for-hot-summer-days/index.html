Creamy Watermelon Coconut Popsicles for Hot Summer Days

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 25, 2026
15 min Prep Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These popsicles combine fresh watermelon with coconut milk for a refreshing and creamy treat. Super easy to make and perfect for cooling off on sunny afternoons or serving at summer parties!

Introduction

I first stumbled on this creamy watermelon popsicle recipe during a particularly hot summer when I wanted something cooling but a little more indulgent than just frozen fruit chunks. What makes this version stand out is the addition of coconut milk, which lends a silky texture and subtle richness that balances the bright watermelon flavor perfectly. I’ve tried just blending watermelon and freezing it, but it often ends up icy and less satisfying. Adding coconut milk and a splash of lime juice creates a dreamy, smooth popsicle that melts slowly and tastes fresh. Plus, it’s super simple to make—just blend and freeze. These popsicles have become a staple for my family’s summer afternoons, perfect for sharing at backyard barbecues or a quick, refreshing snack after a day in the sun. They’re naturally sweet but you can tweak the sweetness with honey or maple syrup if you like. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes that feels fancy but takes no effort—my kind of summer treat!

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 popsicles

  • 4 cups fresh watermelon, cubed and seeds removed
  • 1 cup coconut milk, full fat for creaminess
  • 2 tablespoons honey or maple syrup, optional for sweetness
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lime juice
  • A pinch of salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by cutting the watermelon into cubes, making sure to remove all seeds. This is important because seeds can affect the texture and make blending less smooth. Place the watermelon cubes in a blender. The goal here is to get a smooth puree that will form the base of your popsicles, so blend thoroughly until no chunks remain. If your watermelon isn’t very sweet, you might want to add a little natural sweetener later. Avoid over-blending as it can heat up the fruit and slightly change the flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: The watermelon should be a smooth, bright red liquid with no visible chunks
Step 2
Add the coconut milk, lime juice, honey or maple syrup (if using), and a pinch of salt to the blender. The coconut milk adds creaminess and richness, while the lime juice brightens the flavor and balances sweetness. The salt enhances all the flavors, making the popsicles taste more vibrant. Blend again until everything is well combined and smooth. Don’t rush this step because you want the flavors to meld perfectly for that creamy yet refreshing taste.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should be smooth, creamy, and slightly thickened with a balanced color
Step 3
Pour the mixture into popsicle molds, leaving a little space at the top for expansion as they freeze. Insert popsicle sticks and place the molds in the freezer. Freezing slowly at a consistent temperature helps develop a creamy texture rather than icy crystals. Avoid opening the freezer frequently during this time to prevent melting and refreezing, which can ruin the texture.
⏱ Time: 6 hours or overnight
✓ Doneness: Popsicles should be completely solid and easy to remove from the molds
Step 4
To remove the popsicles, run warm water briefly over the outside of the molds. This loosens the edges without melting the popsicle itself, making it easier to pull out intact. Be careful not to leave the molds in water too long or the popsicles will start melting.
⏱ Time: 1-2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Popsicles should slide out easily without breaking
Step 5
Serve immediately for the best texture. These popsicles are perfect for hot days because they’re both hydrating and satisfying with their creamy mouthfeel. If you’re serving them at a party, keep them in the freezer until just before serving to maintain firmness.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Popsicles are cold, creamy, and refreshing
Step 6
If you want to get creative, try adding fresh mint leaves or a splash of fresh orange juice into the blender for a twist. These additions complement the watermelon and coconut flavors nicely and add a little extra zing. Just pulse them briefly so the texture stays smooth.
⏱ Time: Optional, 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Flavors should be balanced and fresh without overpowering the base
Step 7
For a chunkier popsicle, reserve some small watermelon cubes and add them into the molds before pouring the blended mixture. This adds a fun texture contrast and bursts of juicy watermelon in every bite. Make sure the cubes are small enough to hold on the stick.
⏱ Time: Optional, 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Popsicles have visible fruit pieces suspended inside
Step 8
If your popsicles freeze too hard, let them sit at room temperature for a few minutes before eating. This softens them slightly and brings out the creamy texture. Avoid microwaving as it melts unevenly and changes the texture.
⏱ Time: 2-3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Popsicles are soft enough to bite but still cold
Step 9
Store any leftover popsicles in an airtight container or freezer bag to prevent freezer burn. If they develop ice crystals, it’s best to eat them soon or repurpose by blending into a smoothie. Proper storage keeps the flavors fresh and texture creamy.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Popsicles remain flavorful and smooth after freezing

Pro Tips

Use ripe, seedless watermelon for best flavor and texture.
Ripe watermelon ensures natural sweetness and juiciness, which reduces the need for added sugars. Seedless varieties make blending and eating easier without gritty seeds. Underripe fruit leads to bland popsicles and can affect freezing texture.
Don’t skip the coconut milk — it’s what makes these popsicles creamy.
Watermelon alone freezes into icy shards, but coconut milk’s fat content smooths out the texture by preventing large ice crystals. Using full-fat coconut milk is crucial for that silky mouthfeel.
Add lime juice right before freezing to keep flavors bright.
Acidity from lime juice balances sweetness and prevents flavor dulling during freezing. Adding it too early can cause color changes and flavor loss, so blend it in just before pouring into molds.
Freeze popsicles in silicone molds for easy removal.
Silicone molds flex and release frozen popsicles more easily than rigid plastic, reducing breakage. They also freeze evenly, which helps maintain consistent texture throughout.
Run warm water over molds briefly to loosen popsicles before removing.
This prevents the popsicles from cracking or breaking when you try to pull them out. Warm water melts the edges slightly without affecting the frozen core, making removal smooth.
Avoid microwaving popsicles to soften—let them thaw at room temperature instead.
Microwaving melts popsicles unevenly and ruins the creamy texture, turning them icy or watery. Thawing at room temp softens them gently while keeping the flavor intact.
Adjust sweetness after blending and before freezing by tasting the mixture.
Watermelon sweetness varies, so tasting lets you customize. Adding too much sweetener upfront can make popsicles overly sweet and mask the fresh fruit flavor.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using underripe or watery watermelon

Why it happens People often use whatever watermelon is on hand without checking ripeness, assuming all watermelon is equally sweet and juicy.

Fix Choose watermelon that smells sweet and has a deep red color. If it tastes bland, add a bit more honey or maple syrup to compensate before freezing.

Skipping the coconut milk

Why it happens Some assume watermelon alone is enough, not realizing it freezes too icy and lacks creaminess.

Fix Always add full-fat coconut milk to create a smooth texture. If allergic, try full-fat dairy or a creamy plant milk but expect different flavor.

Not removing watermelon seeds

Why it happens Seeds can be overlooked during prep, especially with small or numerous seeds.

Fix Take time to remove all seeds before blending to prevent gritty texture and unpleasant bites.

Pouring mixture into molds too full

Why it happens Many don’t leave enough headspace for expansion during freezing, causing overflow or cracked popsicles.

Fix Fill molds about 90% full to allow for expansion. This prevents mess and keeps popsicles intact.

Opening freezer repeatedly while popsicles freeze

Why it happens Impatience or multitasking leads to frequent freezer door opening, disrupting freezing temperature.

Fix Set a timer and avoid opening the freezer until popsicles are fully frozen to maintain consistent texture.

Variations

Minty Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

Add a handful of fresh mint leaves to the blender for a cool, herbal twist. Mint brightens the flavor and adds a refreshing aroma that complements the sweetness. This version feels extra summery and works great for garden parties or relaxing afternoons.

Note: Pulse the mint leaves briefly so they blend well without becoming bitter. Too much blending can release chlorophyll and cause a grassy taste.

Spicy Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

For a fun kick, add a pinch of cayenne pepper or chili powder to the blend. The heat contrasts beautifully with the sweet watermelon and creamy coconut, making these popsicles exciting and unique. Great if you like a little spice in your desserts!

Note: Start with a very small amount of spice and adjust to taste. Spice can intensify when frozen, so balance carefully.

Orange Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

Swap lime juice for fresh orange juice to add a sweeter, citrusy note. This variation is less tart and has a bright, sunny flavor that kids especially love. It’s a great way to add vitamin C and a slightly different fruit profile.

Note: Use freshly squeezed orange juice and add it just before freezing to preserve brightness.

Vegan Sweetened Watermelon Popsicles

Use maple syrup instead of honey to keep the recipe vegan. Maple syrup adds a subtle caramel note that enhances the watermelon’s natural sweetness. Perfect for plant-based diets without sacrificing flavor or texture.

Note: Maple syrup blends evenly and freezes well, but avoid using too much to prevent overly sweet popsicles.

Chunky Watermelon Coconut Popsicles

Leave some watermelon cubes whole and add them into the molds before pouring the blended mixture. This adds fun texture and bursts of juicy fruit in every bite, making the popsicles more interesting to eat.

Note: Make sure cubes are small enough to hold on the popsicle stick and distribute evenly in molds.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

These popsicles should not be kept at room temperature for more than 10-15 minutes as they will melt quickly. Keep them covered in the freezer until ready to serve to preserve texture and flavor.

Refrigerator

Not suitable for refrigerator storage as they will melt. Popsicles should be stored in the freezer and only thawed briefly at room temperature before eating.

Freezer

Store popsicles in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap to prevent freezer burn. They keep well for up to 2 months. Avoid freezer odors by sealing properly. Thaw slightly at room temperature before serving.

Reheating

Do not reheat popsicles. To soften, let them sit at room temperature for 2-3 minutes. Avoid microwaving, which ruins texture and flavor.

Make Ahead

Prepare the blended mixture up to 1 day ahead and keep it refrigerated in an airtight container. Stir well before pouring into molds and freezing. This saves time on a busy day while keeping flavors fresh.

FAQ

Can I use canned watermelon juice instead of fresh watermelon?
Fresh watermelon puree is best because it has natural sweetness, flavor, and texture that canned juice lacks. Canned juice may be diluted or contain preservatives that affect taste and freezing. If you must use canned juice, add more coconut milk and sweetener to balance flavor.
What if I don’t have coconut milk?
You can substitute full-fat dairy milk or almond milk but expect a less creamy texture and different flavor. Coconut milk’s fat content is key to the creamy mouthfeel, so other milks may produce icier popsicles.
How long do the popsicles take to freeze completely?
It usually takes about 6 hours or overnight for the popsicles to freeze solid. Freezing times vary by freezer temperature and mold size. Avoid opening the freezer during this time to ensure even freezing.
Can I make these popsicles without added sweetener?
Yes, if your watermelon is very ripe and sweet, you can skip the honey or maple syrup. Taste the blended mixture before freezing to decide if additional sweetener is needed.
Why do my popsicles turn out icy instead of creamy?
This usually happens if there isn’t enough fat in the mixture or if freezing is uneven. Make sure to use full-fat coconut milk and freeze in a stable, cold freezer without frequent door openings.
How do I prevent popsicles from sticking to the molds?
Use silicone molds or lightly oil the molds before pouring. Also, run warm water on the outside of the molds for a few seconds before removing the popsicles to loosen them.
Can I add alcohol to these popsicles?
Small amounts of alcohol can be added but will prevent the mixture from freezing solid. Avoid adding more than 1-2 tablespoons total per batch to maintain freeze quality.
Is it possible to make these popsicles without a blender?
A blender is recommended to achieve a smooth, creamy texture. Without one, you can mash watermelon by hand but the popsicles will be chunkier and less smooth.
