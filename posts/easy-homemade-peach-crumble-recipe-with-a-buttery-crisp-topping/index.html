Cozy Peach Crumble with Buttery Crisp Topping for Summer Nights

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 24, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This peach crumble is my ultimate comfort dessert — juicy peaches under a buttery, crispy topping. I love how the sweet and tart flavors mingle, making it perfect for warm summer evenings or casual get-togethers.

Introduction

I’ve been making this peach crumble for years, and it’s honestly one of my favorite ways to enjoy fresh peaches. The first time I tried baking it, I was nervous about the topping—it took a couple of tries to get that perfect balance between crisp and buttery. What makes my version different is the addition of rolled oats in the topping, which gives it a lovely texture and a bit more depth than just flour and sugar. Plus, I always use fresh peaches when they’re in season because their natural juiciness makes the filling so vibrant and flavorful. When it comes out of the oven, the crumble is golden brown and smells heavenly, with bubbling peach juices peeking through. It’s the kind of dessert that feels like a warm hug after a long day. I usually serve it warm, sometimes with a scoop of vanilla ice cream, which takes it to the next level. Trust me, you’ll want to make this over and over.

What You'll Need

Approximately 6 servings

  • 6 cups fresh peaches, peeled and sliced
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 1/2 cup packed light brown sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, cold and cut into cubes

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). In a large bowl, gently toss the sliced peaches with half of the granulated sugar, lemon juice, vanilla extract, and cinnamon. This step is crucial because the sugar helps draw out the natural juices from the peaches, creating that luscious syrupy filling. The lemon juice brightens the flavor and balances the sweetness. Be careful not to overmix or the peaches may become mushy. Let this sit while you prepare the topping.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches should be well coated and starting to release some juice but still hold their shape.
Step 2
In a separate bowl, combine the flour, oats, brown sugar, salt, and the remaining granulated sugar. Add the cold cubed butter and use your fingertips or a pastry cutter to work it into the dry ingredients until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs. This technique is key for a crumbly, flaky topping. The cold butter melts during baking, creating pockets of crispness and richness. Avoid warming the butter with your hands or overmixing to keep the texture light.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture should look chunky and sandy, with some pea-sized butter bits visible.
Step 3
Transfer the peach mixture to a greased 9x13-inch baking dish, spreading it evenly. Then, sprinkle the crumble topping evenly over the peaches. Be sure to cover the fruit fully but don’t press down, so the topping stays light and crisp. This layering ensures the peaches bubble up beneath a golden crust, giving you that perfect contrast of textures.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Even layer of topping covering all the peaches without compacting it.
Step 4
Bake uncovered in the preheated oven for about 30 minutes. You want to bake until the topping turns golden brown and the peach filling is bubbling around the edges. The bubbling is a sign that the juices have thickened and the filling is cooked through. Keep an eye on the color of the topping; if it browns too quickly, tent loosely with foil to prevent burning.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping should be golden and crisp, and peach juices visibly bubbling.
Step 5
Once baked, remove from the oven and let the crumble cool for at least 10 minutes before serving. This resting time allows the juices to thicken further and the topping to set, making it easier to serve. If you dig in too hot, the filling might be too runny and could spill over. I love serving mine warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or whipped cream for that creamy contrast.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling thickened, topping firm but still crisp to the touch.
Step 6
If you want to prepare ahead, you can assemble the crumble up to this point and cover it tightly with plastic wrap or foil. Store it in the fridge for up to 24 hours, then bake when ready. This makes it perfect for entertaining or busy days when you want a quick dessert. Just add a few extra minutes to the baking time if baking cold.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: N/A
Step 7
For a deeper flavor, you can add a splash of bourbon or a tablespoon of almond extract to the peach mixture before baking. These add-ons complement the peaches beautifully and give the crumble a subtle, grown-up twist. It’s optional but highly recommended if you want to impress guests or switch things up.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: N/A
Step 8
To get the best texture on your crumble topping, make sure your oats are old-fashioned rolled oats, not instant or quick-cooking. The thicker flakes provide a hearty chew and hold up well during baking. Using the right oats is a simple trick that makes a noticeable difference in the final dish.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: N/A
Step 9
If you want to reduce sweetness or adjust flavors, taste your peach filling before adding the topping. You can always add a bit more cinnamon, nutmeg, or adjust sugar to your liking. Because peaches vary in sweetness, this step lets you customize the dessert perfectly. Keep in mind the topping will add sweetness too, so balance accordingly.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Filling tastes balanced—sweet but not overpowering, with a hint of spice.

Pro Tips

Use cold butter straight from the fridge when making the topping.
Cold butter melts slowly during baking, creating flaky pockets that give the crumble its signature crisp texture. If the butter is too warm, it blends too thoroughly with the flour and sugar, resulting in a dense, cakey topping instead of a crumbly one.
Let the peach mixture sit for 10 minutes before baking.
Allowing the peaches to macerate with sugar and lemon juice draws out their juices, creating a syrupy filling. This step ensures the fruit cooks evenly and the flavors meld, preventing a dry or bland filling.
Don’t press down the crumble topping on the peaches.
Keeping the topping loose allows for air pockets to form, which bake into a light, crisp crust. Pressing it down compacts the mixture, leading to a dense and less crunchy texture.
Use a mix of granulated and brown sugar in the topping.
Brown sugar adds moisture and a caramel-like depth, while granulated sugar helps the topping crisp up. The balance between the two sugars creates a topping that's both flavorful and texturally perfect.
Serve the crumble warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream.
The contrast between the hot, juicy fruit and the cold, creamy ice cream enhances the dessert experience, balancing sweetness and temperature for maximum comfort food appeal.
Use a 9x13-inch baking dish for even cooking.
A shallow, wide dish spreads the peach filling and topping evenly, ensuring consistent baking and a perfect balance between fruit and crumble in every bite.
If topping browns too fast, tent with foil midway.
Aluminum foil shields the topping from direct heat, preventing burning while allowing the filling to continue cooking and bubbling underneath.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overripe or watery peaches

Why it happens People often grab the ripest peaches thinking they’ll be sweetest, but overripe fruit can turn mushy and release too much liquid during baking.

Fix Choose peaches that are ripe but still firm. If using very juicy peaches, toss them with a tablespoon of cornstarch to help thicken the filling and prevent it from becoming soupy.

Pressing the crumble topping too firmly

Why it happens Some think pressing the topping will help it stick better, but this compacts the mixture and stops it from crisping properly.

Fix Simply sprinkle the topping evenly over the peaches without pressing. This keeps it light and crumbly, yielding the best texture.

Skipping the resting time after baking

Why it happens Impatience or hunger might lead cooks to serve the crumble immediately, but the filling can be too runny at this stage.

Fix Let the crumble cool for at least 10 minutes to allow the juices to thicken and the topping to set, making it easier to serve and more enjoyable to eat.

Using instant oats instead of rolled oats

Why it happens Instant oats are convenient, but many don’t realize they don’t hold up well in baking.

Fix Always use old-fashioned rolled oats for the topping. They provide the ideal chewy texture and crispness that instant oats can’t.

Not adjusting sugar based on peach sweetness

Why it happens Peaches vary widely in sweetness, so following sugar amounts rigidly can lead to overly sweet or bland results.

Fix Taste your peach mixture before baking and adjust sugar accordingly. Less sweet peaches might need the full amount, while very sweet ones might need less.

Variations

Berry Peach Crumble

Add a cup of fresh blueberries or raspberries to the peach filling for a colorful twist. The tart berries complement the sweet peaches and add a pop of flavor. Expect a slightly juicier filling and a more vibrant look.

Note: Since berries release extra juice, consider adding an extra tablespoon of flour or cornstarch to the fruit mixture to prevent a soggy crumble.

Gluten-Free Peach Crumble

Swap the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free flour blend and use gluten-free oats. This version works just as well and is perfect for those with gluten sensitivities. The texture might be a bit different but still delicious.

Note: Make sure your gluten-free flour blend contains xanthan gum or add a teaspoon for structure in the topping.

Peach Crumble with Almond Topping

Add 1/2 cup sliced almonds to the topping mixture for extra crunch and a nutty flavor. Almonds pair beautifully with peaches and make the crumble feel a bit more special.

Note: Toast the almonds lightly before mixing them in to enhance their flavor and crunch.

Spiced Peach Crumble

Add a pinch of nutmeg and ground ginger to the peach filling along with the cinnamon. These warm spices give the crumble a cozy, autumnal vibe even in summer.

Note: Be careful not to add too much spice; start with 1/4 teaspoon each to avoid overpowering the delicate peach flavor.

Vegan Peach Crumble

Replace butter with a vegan margarine or coconut oil and use maple syrup instead of sugar if preferred. This version keeps all the deliciousness but fits vegan diets perfectly.

Note: Cold coconut oil can be used like butter for the topping but may yield a slightly different texture, so chill the topping mixture before baking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Peach crumble can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely to protect from dust and insects. Beyond that, refrigeration is recommended to maintain freshness and food safety.

Refrigerator

Store leftover crumble in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The topping may soften slightly when chilled. Reheat in the oven at 325°F for 10-15 minutes to restore crispness, or microwave for a quicker option though the topping won’t be as crisp.

Freezer

Freeze the crumble in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly with plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating. Reheat in the oven to regain crisp topping texture.

Reheating

For best results, reheat in a 325°F oven for 10-15 minutes until warmed through and topping is crisp again. Avoid microwaving if you want to keep the crumble crunchy. Add a splash of water or fruit juice if the filling seems dry during reheating.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the peach filling and topping separately up to 24 hours ahead. Store filling in the fridge and topping in an airtight container. Assemble just before baking for maximum freshness and texture.

FAQ

Can I use frozen peaches instead of fresh?
Yes, frozen peaches work well when fresh aren’t available. Thaw them completely and drain any excess liquid before using to prevent a soggy filling. You might want to add a tablespoon of cornstarch to help thicken the juices during baking.
How do I prevent the topping from burning while baking?
If the topping browns too quickly, cover the crumble loosely with aluminum foil halfway through baking. This protects the topping from direct heat while allowing the filling to cook fully.
Can I make this crumble gluten-free?
Absolutely! Use a gluten-free flour blend and certified gluten-free rolled oats. Make sure the blend includes xanthan gum or add some separately to help bind the topping properly.
What’s the best way to store leftovers?
Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in the oven to restore the topping’s crispness. Avoid leaving it out at room temperature for long periods to maintain freshness and safety.
Can I add other fruits to this recipe?
Yes, berries like blueberries or raspberries are great additions. Just be mindful that berries release extra juice, so add a bit more flour or cornstarch to the filling to keep it from getting too watery.
Why does my crumble topping sometimes turn out dense instead of crumbly?
This usually happens if the butter is too warm when mixed or if the topping is overworked. Use cold butter and mix gently until pea-sized pieces form to keep the topping light and crisp.
Can I prepare this dessert ahead of time?
Yes! Assemble the crumble and refrigerate it covered for up to 24 hours before baking. Add a few extra minutes to the baking time if baking from cold to ensure it cooks through.
Is it better to peel the peaches?
Peeling peaches is a personal preference. Peels add texture and nutrients but can be a bit tougher after baking. If you prefer a smoother filling, peel them; otherwise, leaving the skins on is fine.
