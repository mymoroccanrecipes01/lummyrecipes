Cozy Peach Crisp with a Buttery Oat Topping Made from Frozen Peaches

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 17, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
6 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This peach crisp uses frozen peaches for year-round sweetness and a perfectly crisp oat topping. It's super easy and tastes like a warm hug on a chilly evening. Serve it with vanilla ice cream for the ultimate cozy dessert!

Introduction

I’ve been making this peach crisp for years, especially when fresh peaches aren’t in season. The trick I learned early on is to use frozen peaches—they bring out a lovely, juicy sweetness that’s just perfect. I used to worry the crisp topping would get soggy, but letting the peaches thaw properly and mixing in a bit of cornstarch helps keep everything just right. My family loves it warm, straight from the oven, with a big scoop of vanilla ice cream melting on top. It’s the kind of dessert that feels both comforting and effortless, ideal after a busy day when you want something sweet but don’t want to fuss. The oat topping is buttery and crisp, contrasting perfectly with the tender peaches beneath. I also like that it’s so adaptable — you can add nuts or spices if you want to switch things up. Honestly, this peach crisp has become a go-to in our house for a cozy treat any time of the year.

What You'll Need

6 servings

  • 5 cups frozen peach slices, thawed
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup rolled oats
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup light brown sugar, packed
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, cold and cubed

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thawing your frozen peaches completely. This is key because it allows excess juice to release, which you’ll then mix with cornstarch to thicken the filling. If you skip thawing or don’t drain the juices, your crisp might turn out watery. Once thawed, gently toss the peaches with granulated sugar, cornstarch, and vanilla extract in a large bowl. This mix helps balance sweetness and ensures the filling sets nicely during baking.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Peaches are fully thawed with liquid separated, mixture looks glossy and slightly thickened
Step 2
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). While it warms, prepare the oat topping by combining rolled oats, flour, brown sugar, cinnamon, and salt in a medium bowl. Next, add cold, cubed butter and use your fingers or a pastry cutter to work it into the dry ingredients until the mixture looks crumbly and sandy with pea-sized lumps. The cold butter is crucial here — it melts during baking and creates that wonderfully crisp, golden topping everyone loves.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping mixture is crumbly with butter well incorporated, no large chunks of butter remain
Step 3
Transfer the peach filling to a greased 8x8-inch baking dish, spreading it out evenly. Then sprinkle the oat topping evenly over the peaches, covering the entire surface. Don’t press the topping down; leaving it loose helps it bake into a crispy, crunchy layer rather than a dense crust. This layering is what makes the texture so appealing.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling evenly spread, topping fully covers peaches without compacting
Step 4
Bake your peach crisp in the preheated oven for about 30 minutes. The goal is to get the topping golden brown and crispy while the peach filling bubbles around the edges. If your oven runs hot, check at 25 minutes to avoid burning the topping. The bubbling juices are a great sign the filling has thickened properly.
⏱ Time: 30 minutes
✓ Doneness: Topping is golden brown, peach juices bubbling around edges
Step 5
Once baked, let the crisp cool for at least 10 minutes before serving. This resting time helps the filling set so it’s not too runny when you scoop it out. Plus, it’s easier to serve and you avoid burning your mouth! It’s best enjoyed warm, especially with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or whipped cream on top.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Filling has thickened slightly, crisp topping remains crunchy
Step 6
For serving, use a large spoon to scoop generous portions onto plates or bowls. Adding vanilla ice cream right away creates a delicious contrast between hot and cold, creamy and crunchy. This combo is what makes this peach crisp a total crowd-pleaser in my house.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Ice cream slightly melting over warm crisp, creating a luscious texture
Step 7
If you want to prepare ahead, you can assemble the crisp in the baking dish, cover tightly with plastic wrap, and refrigerate for up to 24 hours before baking. Just add a few extra minutes to the baking time if baking straight from the fridge to ensure the topping crisps up nicely.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Crisp bakes evenly with golden topping after refrigerated storage
Step 8
To get a more complex flavor, try stirring in a pinch of ground ginger or nutmeg with the cinnamon in the topping. These warm spices complement the peaches beautifully and add a little extra cozy vibe to the dessert. Just be careful not to overpower the fresh fruit flavor.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Spices subtly enhance aroma without overwhelming peach flavor
Step 9
For a crunchy twist, sprinkle chopped pecans or walnuts over the oat topping before baking. Nuts add texture and a toasty flavor that pairs perfectly with the buttery oats and sweet peaches. Just watch the nuts so they don’t burn during baking — you might want to reduce oven temperature slightly if adding nuts.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Nuts are toasted golden but not dark brown or burnt

Pro Tips

Always thaw frozen peaches fully and drain excess juice before baking.
Thawing allows the peaches to release their juices, which when combined with cornstarch, thickens the filling properly. If you skip thawing or don’t drain, the dessert can become watery and the topping soggy. This step ensures a perfect balance of juicy filling and crisp topping.
Use cold butter and cut it into the topping mixture until crumbly.
Cold butter creates small pockets of fat that melt slowly during baking, producing a flaky, crisp texture. If the butter is too warm or melted, the topping will be dense and greasy. Cutting in the butter properly is essential for that perfect oat crispiness.
Don’t press the oat topping down; keep it loose and crumbly.
Pressing the topping compacts it, which can lead to a dense, cakey texture rather than a crisp one. A loose topping allows heat to circulate and steam to escape during baking, resulting in a crunchy, golden crust.
Let the crisp rest after baking before serving.
Resting helps the filling thicken as it cools, making it easier to serve without it running everywhere. It also allows flavors to meld and the topping to maintain its texture. Serving immediately out of the oven can be too hot and runny.
Add a pinch of cinnamon or nutmeg to the topping for extra warmth.
Warm spices complement the peaches and add depth to the flavor profile. They enhance the comfort-food vibe and make the dessert smell irresistible. Be careful not to add too much, or you’ll overpower the fresh fruit taste.
Use a baking dish with straight sides for even cooking.
Straight-sided dishes ensure the topping cooks evenly and the filling bubbles properly. Shallow or wide pans can cause the filling to dry out or the topping to brown too fast. A square 8x8-inch dish is ideal for balanced results.
Serve with vanilla ice cream or whipped cream for best results.
The cold creaminess contrasts beautifully with the warm, crunchy crisp, creating a delightful texture and flavor combination that elevates the dessert. It’s a simple addition that makes the experience feel extra special.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the thawing and draining of frozen peaches.

Why it happens Many assume you can use frozen fruit straight from the freezer, but this traps excess moisture in the dish, making it watery.

Fix Always thaw peaches fully in a bowl and drain off excess liquid before mixing with sugar and cornstarch. This prevents a soggy filling and keeps the topping crisp.

Using melted or room temperature butter in the topping.

Why it happens It’s tempting to melt butter for easier mixing, but this changes the texture of the topping drastically.

Fix Use cold butter and cut it into the dry ingredients until crumbly. This technique traps fat pockets that create a crisp, flaky texture when baked.

Pressing down the oat topping firmly before baking.

Why it happens Some think compacting the topping will make it hold together better, but it actually ruins the texture.

Fix Keep the topping loose and crumbly on top of the peaches. This allows it to crisp up nicely and creates the perfect contrast with the soft fruit.

Baking at too high a temperature causing burnt topping.

Why it happens Rushing the cooking process by increasing oven heat can burn the topping before the filling cooks through.

Fix Bake at 350°F for about 30 minutes and watch for golden brown topping and bubbling juices. Adjust time slightly if needed.

Serving immediately without letting it rest after baking.

Why it happens Excited to eat, many serve right out of the oven, but the filling is too runny and hot.

Fix Let the crisp rest 10 minutes to cool slightly and thicken. This improves serving ease and flavor melding.

Variations

Nutty Peach Crisp

Add 1/2 cup chopped pecans or walnuts to the oat topping for extra crunch and a toasty flavor. The nuts complement the peaches beautifully and add a nice contrast in texture.

Note: Add nuts before baking and keep oven temperature consistent to avoid burning. Watch topping color closely.

Spiced Peach Crisp

Incorporate 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg or ginger into the topping mixture. These warm spices deepen the flavor and make the crisp extra cozy and aromatic.

Note: Mix spices evenly with dry ingredients for balanced flavor without overpowering the fruit.

Gluten-Free Peach Crisp

Use gluten-free rolled oats and substitute all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend. This keeps the texture similar while accommodating dietary needs.

Note: Ensure your gluten-free flour blend includes xanthan gum for structure; otherwise, the topping may be crumbly.

Vegan Peach Crisp

Replace butter with coconut oil or vegan butter substitute. The oat topping stays crisp and flavorful, and the peaches remain juicy and sweet.

Note: Use solid coconut oil chilled and cut into the topping like butter for best texture.

Berry Peach Crisp

Mix 1 cup fresh or frozen berries (blueberries or raspberries) with the peaches before baking. This adds color, tartness, and complexity to the filling.

Note: Adjust sugar slightly if berries are tart and ensure berries are thawed and drained similar to peaches.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The peach crisp can be left at room temperature for up to 2 hours covered loosely with foil or a clean towel. Beyond that, it’s best to refrigerate to keep it safe and fresh.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The crisp topping may soften but reheating can restore some crunch. Serve warm or cold depending on preference.

Freezer

Freeze the baked crisp in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in plastic wrap and foil for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat in the oven to refresh the topping’s texture.

Reheating

Reheat individual portions in a 350°F oven for about 10-15 minutes until warmed through and topping crisps up again. Avoid microwaving as it can make the topping soggy. Adding a splash of water or juice before reheating helps keep filling moist.

Make Ahead

You can assemble the crisp ahead of time and keep it covered in the fridge for up to 24 hours before baking. This makes weeknight dessert stress-free. Just add a few extra minutes to baking time when cooking from chilled.

FAQ

Can I use fresh peaches instead of frozen?
Absolutely! Fresh peaches work great in season. Just peel, slice, and toss with sugar and cornstarch like the frozen ones. The texture will be slightly different—fresh peaches tend to be firmer and less watery—but the flavor is fantastic. You might want to reduce the cornstarch a bit if your peaches are very firm.
How do I prevent the topping from burning?
Bake at a moderate 350°F and keep an eye on the topping during the last 5-10 minutes. If it’s browning too fast, loosely cover with foil to prevent burning while the filling finishes cooking. Using cold butter and not pressing the topping down also helps it brown evenly without burning.
Can I make this peach crisp vegan?
Yes! Swap the butter for chilled coconut oil or a vegan butter substitute. The texture will be slightly different but still delicious and crisp. Be sure to use solid fats chilled and cut into the topping like butter for best results.
What’s the best way to store leftovers?
Store leftover peach crisp in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. The topping may soften but reheating in the oven can bring back some crunch. You can also freeze baked crisp for longer storage—just thaw and reheat before serving.
Can I add nuts to the topping?
Definitely! Chopped pecans or walnuts add a wonderful crunch and flavor. Mix them into the oat topping before baking. Just watch the baking time and temperature so nuts don’t burn—reduce oven heat slightly if needed.
How do I make the filling less sweet?
You can reduce the sugar in the filling by a few tablespoons if you prefer less sweetness. Taste your peaches first—frozen peaches can be sweetened already. Adjust sugar accordingly to keep a nice balance between tartness and sweetness.
Why do I need to let the crisp rest before serving?
Resting allows the filling to thicken as it cools, preventing it from being too runny and making it easier to serve. The topping also stays crispier instead of becoming soggy from steam. A 10-minute rest is usually perfect.
Can I double this recipe?
Yes, you can double the ingredients and bake in a larger dish, like a 9x13-inch pan. Baking time may increase slightly—check for bubbling filling and golden topping to know it’s done. You might want to cover with foil if the topping browns too quickly.
