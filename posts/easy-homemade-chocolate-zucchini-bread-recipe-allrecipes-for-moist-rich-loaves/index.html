Moist and Chocolatey Zucchini Bread with a Healthy Twist

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 6, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This chocolate zucchini bread is my favorite when I crave something rich yet sneaky healthy. The zucchini adds moisture without masking the deep cocoa flavor. Great for breakfast or an afternoon snack!

Introduction

I first made this chocolate zucchini bread a few years ago when I wanted to sneak some veggies into my family's snacks without them noticing. Honestly, I was a bit skeptical at first—would the zucchini overpower the chocolate? Turns out, it doesn’t at all! Instead, the zucchini keeps the bread incredibly moist and tender, making it almost impossible to resist. What makes this version different is how finely shredded the zucchini is and the extra step of squeezing out the moisture to avoid a soggy loaf. I also love adding a touch of vanilla to complement the cocoa. The end result is a rich, chocolatey bread that feels indulgent but is a little healthier thanks to the zucchini. It’s perfect for breakfast, a midday pick-me-up, or even dessert. Plus, it’s so simple to whip up on a weekend afternoon. I’ve had plenty of failed zucchini breads before—too wet, too dense—but this one nails the texture every time. I think you’ll love how soft and flavorful it turns out!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 1 ½ cups all-purpose flour
  • ½ cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • ¼ teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • ½ cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 ½ cups finely shredded zucchini, squeezed dry
  • ½ cup chopped walnuts (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan well. This ensures the bread doesn’t stick and helps it bake evenly. Preparing your pan first prevents any last-minute scrambling and sets you up for success. The greasing also promotes a golden crust while keeping the loaf moist inside.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is evenly greased with no dry spots and oven is fully preheated
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, cocoa powder, baking soda, baking powder, and salt. Sifting or whisking these dry ingredients helps distribute the leavening agents evenly, which is key for a uniform rise and avoiding dense spots in the bread.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are fully combined with no clumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat together the sugar and vegetable oil until well combined. Then add the eggs one at a time, mixing well after each addition. This creates a smooth, emulsified base that traps air and helps the bread rise light and fluffy.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is creamy and slightly thickened with no visible oil separation
Step 4
Stir in the vanilla extract and shredded zucchini. Make sure your zucchini is finely shredded and squeezed dry to avoid excess moisture, which can make the bread soggy. The zucchini adds moisture and subtle texture without overpowering the chocolate flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly distributed throughout the wet mixture
Step 5
Gradually fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture, mixing just until combined. Overmixing can develop gluten and make the bread tough. Folding gently keeps the crumb tender and soft as the flour is absorbed.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth with no visible flour pockets but still slightly lumpy
Step 6
If using, fold in the chopped walnuts for a nice crunch contrast. This step is optional but adds a lovely texture and nutty flavor that complements the chocolate and zucchini beautifully.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Nuts are evenly distributed without overmixing the batter
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and rise uniformly, avoiding peaks or cracks on top.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is level and fills the pan about 3/4 full
Step 8
Bake for 30-35 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Baking time can vary based on your oven, so start checking at 30 minutes to avoid overbaking, which can dry the bread out.
⏱ Time: 30-35 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick test shows moist crumbs but no wet batter
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. This resting time lets the bread set and finish baking gently, making slicing easier and cleaner.
⏱ Time: 15 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread is firm enough to slice without crumbling

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from the shredded zucchini before adding it to the batter.
Removing extra water prevents a soggy texture and ensures the bread holds its shape. Excess moisture can interfere with gluten development and leavening, resulting in a dense or gummy loaf.
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently and just until combined.
Overmixing activates gluten too much, making the bread tough instead of soft. Folding preserves tenderness and a light crumb by limiting gluten formation.
Don’t skip the step of preheating your oven fully before baking.
An oven at the right temperature ensures the bread rises properly and cooks evenly. Putting the bread into a cold or warming oven can cause uneven baking and poor texture.
Use a toothpick to check doneness starting at the minimum baking time.
Baking too long dries out the bread, while underbaking leaves it gummy inside. The toothpick test helps you catch the perfect moment when the bread is moist but fully cooked.
Add chopped nuts like walnuts for texture contrast, but fold them in gently.
Nuts add a nice crunch and flavor depth without overwhelming the bread. Gentle folding prevents breaking the nuts or overworking the batter.
Let the bread cool in the pan briefly before transferring to a wire rack.
This resting time allows the bread to set and finish baking gently, making it easier to slice without crumbling or sticking.
Measure flour by spooning it into your cup and leveling off.
Accurate flour measurement prevents a dry or dense bread. Scooping directly can pack too much flour, throwing off the moisture balance.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing out zucchini moisture

Why it happens Many assume shredded zucchini won’t add much water, but it contains a surprising amount of liquid that can disrupt the batter.

Fix After shredding, wrap zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess water. This keeps the bread from becoming soggy or gummy.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Eager to combine ingredients quickly, some mix vigorously, developing too much gluten.

Fix Use a folding motion and mix just until no dry flour remains. This keeps the texture tender and crumb soft.

Baking at too high a temperature

Why it happens Rushing baking time or miscalibrated ovens cause bread to brown too fast outside but remain undercooked inside.

Fix Set oven to 350°F and avoid opening the door too often. Use an oven thermometer to verify temperature.

Using Dutch-processed cocoa instead of natural

Why it happens Some recipes don’t specify, leading to confusion since Dutch-processed has different acidity.

Fix Stick with natural cocoa powder for this recipe to ensure proper rise and flavor balance.

Not letting bread cool before slicing

Why it happens Impatience leads to slicing hot bread, which can crumble and fall apart.

Fix Allow the loaf to cool at least 15 minutes in the pan, then fully on a wire rack for clean slices.

Variations

Gluten-Free Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Swap the all-purpose flour for a gluten-free blend designed for baking. Expect a slightly different crumb—less chewy but still moist—thanks to the zucchini. Add a teaspoon of xanthan gum if your blend doesn’t include it to improve structure.

Note: Gluten-free flours absorb liquids differently, so you may need to adjust moisture slightly by reducing zucchini or adding an extra egg.

Vegan Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use coconut oil instead of vegetable oil. The bread will be a bit denser but still moist and flavorful.

Note: Flax eggs help bind the batter, but the rise may be less pronounced. Bake a few minutes longer and test doneness carefully.

Spiced Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Add 1 teaspoon cinnamon and a pinch of nutmeg to the dry ingredients for a warm, cozy twist. These spices complement the chocolate and zucchini beautifully, perfect for fall baking.

Note: Adding spices won’t affect baking time but enhances aroma and flavor complexity.

Chocolate Zucchini Muffins

Divide the batter into a muffin tin lined with paper cups and bake for 18-20 minutes. Muffins are great for portion control and quick snacks on the go.

Note: Watch closely near the end of baking to prevent overcooking; muffins bake faster than a loaf.

Nut-Free Chocolate Zucchini Bread

Simply omit walnuts for a nut-free version. You can add sunflower seeds or pumpkin seeds for crunch if desired. The texture remains moist and rich without nuts.

Note: Removing nuts doesn’t affect baking time or texture significantly.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the cooled bread covered loosely with foil or in a bread box at room temperature for up to 2 days. Avoid airtight containers at room temp to prevent sogginess from trapped moisture.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 5 days. Refrigeration can firm up the texture slightly; warm slices gently in the microwave or toaster oven before serving to restore softness.

Freezer

Wrap the loaf tightly in plastic wrap and foil, then freeze for up to 3 months. To thaw, leave at room temperature for a few hours or overnight in the fridge. Reheat slices gently to regain softness.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster oven at 300°F for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 20-30 seconds with a damp paper towel to keep moist. Avoid overheating, which can dry out the bread.

Make Ahead

You can shred and squeeze the zucchini a day ahead, storing it wrapped in the fridge. Mix the batter and bake on the day you plan to serve for maximum freshness and moisture.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini for this recipe?
You can use frozen zucchini, but be sure to thaw it completely and squeeze out as much moisture as possible before adding it to the batter. Frozen zucchini often contains more water, so extra draining is crucial to avoid a soggy bread.
Why does my zucchini bread come out dry sometimes?
Dry zucchini bread usually means too much flour or not enough moisture. Make sure to measure flour properly and don’t overmix the batter, as that develops gluten and toughens the crumb. Also, check that your zucchini is finely shredded and well-drained.
Can I substitute the vegetable oil with butter?
Butter can be used but will change the texture slightly, making the bread less moist and more crumbly. If using butter, melt it first and let it cool before mixing to avoid cooking the eggs prematurely.
How do I store leftover zucchini bread?
Store leftovers in an airtight container at room temperature for a couple days or in the fridge up to 5 days. For longer storage, freeze wrapped tightly. Reheat gently to restore softness before serving.
Can I make this bread gluten-free?
Yes! Use a gluten-free baking blend and add xanthan gum if your mix doesn’t have it to help with structure. The texture will be slightly different but still moist and delicious.
What’s the best way to shred zucchini?
Use the medium holes on a box grater or a food processor with a shredding disc. Shredding finely helps the zucchini blend seamlessly into the batter without large chunks.
Why do I need both baking soda and baking powder?
Baking soda neutralizes the acidity from the cocoa and zucchini, helping the bread rise and brown properly. Baking powder adds extra lift and balances the rise for a light crumb. Using both ensures the bread isn’t dense or flat.
Can I add chocolate chips to this recipe?
Absolutely! Stir in about ½ cup of chocolate chips for extra bursts of melty chocolate throughout the bread. Fold them in gently at the end to avoid overmixing the batter.
