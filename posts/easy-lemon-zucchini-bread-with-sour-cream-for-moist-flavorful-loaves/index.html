Bright and Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread with Sour Cream for Spring

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is a moist, tender quick bread that’s bursting with fresh lemon zest and a subtle tang from sour cream. It's my go-to for easy breakfasts or a sunny afternoon pick-me-up.

Introduction

I first tried making lemon zucchini bread a few years ago when I wanted a quick, fresh-tasting bread for spring mornings. I remember my first attempt was a bit dry, so I started experimenting with adding sour cream to the batter. That little tweak made all the difference, keeping the bread incredibly moist and tender without weighing it down. I also learned that using fresh lemon zest, not just juice, gives the bread a bright, vibrant flavor that feels like sunshine in every bite. What I love about this recipe is how straightforward it is — no fancy equipment or complicated steps, just simple ingredients that come together beautifully. The grated zucchini adds moisture and subtle texture, making it a great way to sneak in some veggies, too. When it’s done, the crust has a gentle golden color with a soft crumb inside that’s perfect for breakfast, an afternoon snack, or even a light dessert with tea or coffee. I usually slice it thin and toast it lightly to bring out the lemon aroma even more. It’s become a staple in my kitchen, especially when zucchinis are in season and I want something fresh but comforting. Honestly, it’s a recipe I feel good sharing with friends because it’s both delicious and approachable. If you’ve struggled with dry quick breads before, give this one a try — the sour cream is a total game changer!

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, about 8 servings

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour, sifted
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest (from 2 lemons)
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup sour cream, full fat
  • 1 1/2 cups grated zucchini (about 1 medium zucchini), squeezed dry

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan with butter or non-stick spray. This step is crucial because a well-greased pan ensures your bread releases easily without sticking or tearing, which can ruin the loaf’s appearance. Using a metal or glass pan works fine, but avoid dark non-stick pans as they can brown the bread too quickly. While the oven heats, gather your ingredients so you’re ready to mix without delay.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf pan is evenly greased with no bare spots; oven reaches stable 350°F
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the sifted flour, baking soda, baking powder, salt, and lemon zest. Sifting helps aerate the flour and evenly distributes the leavening, which creates a tender crumb and prevents dense spots. The lemon zest infuses the batter with citrus oils that give a fresh aroma and flavor. Mix just until combined to avoid overworking the gluten, which can make the bread tough.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry ingredients are evenly combined with visible lemon zest flecks, no clumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, beat the sugar and oil together until well combined but don’t overbeat. This step helps dissolve the sugar slightly and ensures even distribution of fat for moistness. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition to incorporate air and create a smooth batter. Then stir in vanilla extract for depth of flavor. Mixing these wet ingredients thoroughly is key to a consistent texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is smooth, glossy, and slightly thickened; no visible egg streaks
Step 4
Fold the sour cream into the wet ingredients until just combined. Sour cream adds moisture and a subtle tang that balances the sweetness, plus it helps tenderize the crumb by adding fat and acidity. Be gentle here to keep the batter light. If you add sour cream cold, it can cause the batter to curdle slightly but that’s okay; it will smooth out once mixed with the dry ingredients.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is creamy and slightly thickened, no lumps of sour cream remain
Step 5
Add the dry ingredients to the wet mixture in two additions, folding gently with a spatula after each. Avoid overmixing to prevent gluten development, which would make the bread tough. The batter should be thick but smooth. The lemon zest and baking soda will react with the sour cream’s acidity during baking to give a nice rise and tender crumb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is uniform with no flour pockets but still thick and soft
Step 6
Fold in the grated zucchini, making sure it’s well drained to avoid a soggy loaf. The zucchini adds moisture and subtle texture without overpowering the lemon flavor. It’s important to squeeze out excess water using a clean kitchen towel or paper towels, as too much liquid can prevent proper rising and cause a gummy crumb.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is evenly distributed; batter consistency remains thick but not runny
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. This helps the bread bake evenly and creates a nice crust. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles that could cause holes or uneven texture.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter fills pan evenly with a smooth surface
Step 8
Bake in the preheated oven for 50-60 minutes. Start checking around 50 minutes by inserting a toothpick in the center—it should come out with a few moist crumbs but no raw batter. Baking too short leaves the center gooey; too long dries it out. The lemon aroma will fill your kitchen when it’s nearly done, and the crust should be golden brown.
⏱ Time: 50-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Toothpick inserted near center comes out clean or with moist crumbs; crust golden
Step 9
Let the bread cool in the pan for 15 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely. This resting period allows the bread to set and prevents it from breaking apart. Cooling on a rack keeps the crust from getting soggy. Once cooled, slice with a serrated knife for clean cuts. The bread is best enjoyed within a couple of days but can be wrapped and frozen for longer storage.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Bread feels firm but moist when touched; slices cleanly without crumbling

Pro Tips

Always squeeze the grated zucchini to remove excess water before adding it to the batter.
Excess moisture from zucchini can lead to a soggy, dense bread that won’t rise properly. Removing water helps maintain the right batter consistency and ensures a tender crumb with good structure.
Use fresh lemon zest rather than bottled for the best citrus flavor.
Fresh lemon zest contains volatile oils that give a bright, fresh aroma and flavor. Bottled zest loses these oils and tastes dull, which can make the bread less vibrant.
Mix dry and wet ingredients gently and avoid overmixing.
Overmixing develops gluten, which toughens the bread and makes it dense. Gentle folding keeps the crumb tender and light while still incorporating ingredients evenly.
Bring eggs and sour cream to room temperature before mixing.
Room temperature ingredients combine more smoothly and reduce the risk of curdling when mixing wet and dry ingredients, resulting in a uniform batter and better texture.
Use a serrated knife to slice the cooled bread for clean cuts.
A serrated knife cuts through the crust without compressing or tearing the soft crumb, preserving the bread’s texture and appearance.
Let the bread cool completely on a wire rack before storing.
Cooling prevents condensation inside storage containers, which can make the crust soggy and encourage mold growth.
Check for doneness with a toothpick starting at 50 minutes.
Ovens vary, so checking early prevents overbaking, which dries out the bread. The toothpick should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Adding too much liquid zucchini without draining

Why it happens Many assume fresh zucchini can be added as-is, but its high water content can overwhelm the batter.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean towel and squeeze firmly to remove excess water. This keeps the batter balanced and prevents sogginess.

Overmixing the batter

Why it happens Some think thorough mixing improves texture but it actually develops gluten making bread tough.

Fix Fold ingredients gently just until flour disappears. Stop mixing as soon as combined to maintain tenderness.

Using bottled lemon zest or lemon flavor

Why it happens Convenience leads cooks to use bottled zest which lacks fresh oils and aroma.

Fix Always zest fresh lemons right before baking to maximize flavor and aroma.

Skipping sour cream or substituting with milk

Why it happens Some try to reduce fat or use what’s on hand, not realizing sour cream’s acidity and fat are key.

Fix Use full-fat sour cream for moisture and rise. If unavailable, plain Greek yogurt is a better substitute than milk.

Not greasing the pan properly

Why it happens Rushing or forgetting this step causes bread to stick and break when removing.

Fix Grease the pan thoroughly with butter or spray, covering all edges to ensure easy release.

Variations

Lemon Poppy Seed Zucchini Bread

Add 2 tablespoons of poppy seeds to the batter for a pleasant crunch and nutty flavor that complements the lemon zest beautifully. This variation adds texture and a subtle nuttiness that pairs well with the moist crumb.

Note: Fold poppy seeds in with the dry ingredients, ensuring even distribution without overmixing.

Gluten-Free Lemon Zucchini Bread

Swap all-purpose flour with a gluten-free blend that includes xanthan gum. The bread remains moist and tender thanks to the sour cream, but expect a slightly denser crumb and adjust baking time as needed.

Note: Use a 1:1 gluten-free baking flour blend and monitor doneness closely as gluten-free flours can brown faster.

Vegan Lemon Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use a plant-based yogurt instead of sour cream. The bread will be slightly denser but still flavorful and moist with a subtle lemon zing.

Note: Allow flax eggs to gel for at least 10 minutes before mixing; baking time may increase by 5-10 minutes.

Lemon Walnut Zucchini Bread

Fold in 1/2 cup chopped toasted walnuts for added crunch and richness. The nuts add a lovely texture contrast and earthy flavor that balances the bright lemon and tender zucchini.

Note: Toast walnuts briefly to enhance flavor and fold in gently at the end to avoid breaking them up.

Lemon Blueberry Zucchini Bread

Fold 1 cup fresh or frozen blueberries into the batter for bursts of juicy sweetness. This variation adds a fruity twist while keeping the bread moist and flavorful.

Note: If using frozen blueberries, toss them in a tablespoon of flour first to prevent sinking.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Store the lemon zucchini bread wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 2 days. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat sources to maintain moisture and prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Keep the bread in an airtight container or wrapped tightly in the fridge for up to 5 days. The bread may firm up when chilled, so bring it to room temperature or lightly toast slices before serving to refresh the texture.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and then aluminum foil, or place in a freezer-safe bag. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature. Reheat slices in a toaster or oven to restore softness and aroma.

Reheating

Reheat slices in a toaster or oven at 325°F (160°C) for 5-7 minutes until warm and slightly crisp on the edges. Avoid microwaving as it can make the bread rubbery. Adding a small dab of butter after reheating enhances flavor and moisture.

Make Ahead

You can grate the zucchini and zest lemons 1-2 days ahead and store them separately in airtight containers in the fridge. Mix and bake the day of serving for freshest flavor and texture. Alternatively, bake the bread ahead and freeze individual slices for quick breakfasts.

FAQ

Can I use frozen zucchini instead of fresh for this bread?
You can use frozen zucchini, but make sure to thaw it completely and squeeze out as much water as possible before adding it to the batter. Excess moisture from frozen zucchini can make the bread soggy and affect the texture negatively.
What can I substitute for sour cream if I don’t have any?
Plain full-fat Greek yogurt is the best substitute for sour cream as it provides similar moisture and acidity. Avoid using milk alone as it lacks the fat and tang, which are important for texture and flavor.
Can I make this bread without eggs?
Yes, you can replace eggs with flax eggs or chia eggs (1 tablespoon ground seeds + 3 tablespoons water per egg). Let the mixture gel before adding. The bread might be slightly denser but still moist and flavorful.
How do I prevent the bread from sticking to the pan?
Grease the pan thoroughly with butter or non-stick spray and lightly flour it if desired. Let the bread cool for at least 15 minutes before removing it from the pan to allow it to set and release more easily.
Can I make muffins instead of a loaf with this recipe?
Absolutely! Adjust baking time to 20-25 minutes, checking with a toothpick. Muffins will bake faster due to smaller size but maintain the same moist texture and flavor.
Is it necessary to sift the flour?
Sifting helps aerate the flour and evenly distributes leavening agents, which contributes to a lighter crumb. If you don’t have a sifter, whisk the flour and baking soda/powder together thoroughly.
Can I add nuts or other mix-ins?
Yes! Chopped walnuts, pecans, or poppy seeds work great. Fold them in gently with the zucchini to avoid overmixing. This adds texture and flavor complexity.
