Moist Lemon Zucchini Bread with a Tangy Lemon Pudding Glaze

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 3, 2026
15 min Prep Time
50 min Time
8 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This lemon zucchini bread is delightfully moist and fresh, thanks to a tangy lemon pudding glaze that keeps it tender. Perfectly bright and citrusy, it's my go-to for a sweet breakfast or afternoon tea treat.

Introduction

I first tried making lemon zucchini bread a few years ago when I wanted something fresh and moist that wasn’t too heavy. The twist that really won me over was adding a lemon pudding glaze right into the batter and as a drizzle on top. It keeps the bread incredibly tender and gives it a subtle tang that brightens every bite. I’ve made this loaf countless times for breakfast, snacks, and even tea parties—it just feels like a little burst of sunshine on a plate. The secret is not just the lemon zest or juice, but the pudding mix—it’s an easy ingredient that adds moisture and a silky texture without fuss. I remember one early attempt where I forgot to squeeze the zucchini well and ended up with a soggy mess, so drying it is key. When done right, this bread has a golden crust with a soft crumb inside and a glossy lemon glaze that’s lightly sweet and tart. It’s a recipe I always recommend to friends who want something bright and comforting without being too sweet or dense.

What You'll Need

One 9x5 inch loaf, 8 servings

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 1 tablespoon lemon zest (from about 2 lemons)
  • 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 1 cup grated zucchini, squeezed dry
  • 1/2 cup lemon pudding mix (instant, dry)
  • 1/4 cup milk

How To Do It

Step 1
Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C) and grease a 9x5 inch loaf pan well. This ensures the bread bakes evenly and releases easily without sticking. Greasing the pan thoroughly is important here because zucchini breads tend to be moist and can cling if you skip this step. You can also line the pan with parchment paper for extra insurance. Set aside while you prepare the batter.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pan is fully greased or lined, ready for batter
Step 2
In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, salt, and lemon pudding mix. Combining these dry ingredients first helps distribute the leavening agents and pudding evenly, which is key for consistent texture and rise. The pudding mix adds moisture and a subtle lemon flavor that makes this bread extra tender.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dry mixture is well combined, no lumps
Step 3
In a large bowl, cream the softened butter and sugar together with a mixer on medium speed until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes. This step incorporates air into the batter, giving the bread a lighter crumb. Don’t rush it; under-creaming can result in a denser loaf.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Butter and sugar mixture is pale and fluffy
Step 4
Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. This helps emulsify the batter and prevents curdling. Then stir in the vanilla extract, lemon zest, and fresh lemon juice. The fresh lemon juice combined with the zest gives this bread its vibrant citrus punch—don’t skip the zest as it holds essential oils that boost flavor.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is smooth, glossy, and fragrant with lemon
Step 5
Fold the dry ingredients into the wet mixture gently using a spatula until just combined. Overmixing here can develop gluten and make the bread tough, so stop as soon as you no longer see flour. The batter should be thick but pourable.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: No dry flour visible, batter slightly thick
Step 6
Grate the zucchini finely and squeeze out excess moisture using a clean kitchen towel or paper towels. Adding too much water from zucchini can make the bread soggy and heavy. Once drained, fold the zucchini into the batter carefully to distribute evenly.
⏱ Time: 7 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini is well drained and mixed evenly in batter
Step 7
Pour the batter into the prepared loaf pan and smooth the top with a spatula. Tap the pan gently on the counter to release any large air bubbles. This helps the bread bake evenly and prevents large holes inside the crumb.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Batter is evenly spread and surface is smooth
Step 8
Bake for 45-50 minutes or until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs. Baking time can vary depending on your oven and moisture level in the zucchini. Avoid opening the oven door too early to prevent sinking.
⏱ Time: 45-50 minutes
✓ Doneness: Loaf is golden brown and toothpick is clean
Step 9
While the bread cools in the pan for 10 minutes, prepare a quick lemon glaze by mixing 1/4 cup fresh lemon juice with 1/4 cup powdered sugar until smooth. Drizzle this over the warm loaf to soak in and add an extra burst of citrusy sweetness. The glaze cools and sets, forming a shiny top that keeps the bread moist longer.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Glaze is glossy and lightly set on the loaf

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out excess moisture from zucchini before adding to batter.
Zucchini contains a lot of water that can make the bread soggy if not drained properly. Removing this moisture helps maintain the bread’s structure and prevents it from becoming too dense or falling apart.
Cream butter and sugar until pale and fluffy for a lighter crumb.
Creaming traps air bubbles in the batter, which expand during baking and create a tender texture. Under-creaming results in a denser, heavier bread that lacks lift.
Don’t overmix the batter once the dry ingredients are added.
Overmixing develops gluten in the flour, making the bread tough and chewy rather than soft and tender. Mixing until just combined keeps the crumb light.
Use fresh lemon zest and juice for the best bright flavor.
Fresh zest contains essential oils that provide intense lemon aroma, while fresh juice adds natural acidity that balances sweetness and activates the baking soda for rise.
Let the bread cool slightly before glazing to avoid melting the glaze completely.
Applying glaze while the bread is warm helps it soak in without running off, giving a shiny, slightly set finish that keeps the loaf moist and attractive.
Use a 9x5 inch loaf pan for even baking and proper shape.
The right pan size ensures the batter bakes through evenly without undercooked centers or overly browned edges.
Test doneness with a toothpick to avoid overbaking.
Overbaking dries out the bread and reduces moisture, so checking with a toothpick helps pull the bread out at the perfect time for moist crumb.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not squeezing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many assume grated zucchini can be added as-is, but the excess water can weigh down the batter and create a soggy texture.

Fix After grating, place zucchini in a clean towel and wring out firmly to remove as much liquid as possible before folding into the batter.

Overmixing batter after adding dry ingredients

Why it happens In a rush to combine, it’s easy to mix too much, which develops gluten and toughens the bread.

Fix Mix gently and only until no dry flour remains; a few lumps are okay.

Using old or expired baking soda

Why it happens Baking soda loses potency over time, so the bread won’t rise properly if it’s stale.

Fix Replace with fresh baking soda and store in a cool, dry place.

Skipping the creaming step or doing it too quickly

Why it happens Not creaming properly reduces the air incorporated into the batter, resulting in a dense loaf.

Fix Use room temperature butter and beat with sugar until light and fluffy, about 3-4 minutes.

Applying glaze while bread is too hot

Why it happens Hot bread melts the glaze completely, causing it to run off and lose that shiny finish.

Fix Cool bread for 10 minutes before drizzling the glaze to allow it to set nicely.

Variations

Blueberry Lemon Zucchini Bread

Add 1 cup fresh or frozen blueberries folded gently into the batter before baking. The blueberries add bursts of sweetness and a lovely color contrast. Expect a slightly moister loaf with fruity pockets throughout.

Note: Frozen blueberries should be thawed and drained to avoid excess moisture; fold carefully to avoid breaking berries.

Gluten-Free Lemon Zucchini Bread

Substitute the all-purpose flour with a gluten-free baking blend that includes xanthan gum. This keeps the texture tender without the gluten. The bread will be a bit denser but still moist and flavorful.

Note: Ensure your baking blend includes leavening or add a bit more baking soda for rise.

Lemon Poppy Seed Zucchini Bread

Stir in 2 tablespoons of poppy seeds with the dry ingredients for a slight crunch and nutty flavor. This variation adds texture and a pretty speckled look to the bread.

Note: Poppy seeds don’t affect moisture but add a pleasant bite; mix evenly.

Vegan Lemon Zucchini Bread

Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon ground flaxseed mixed with 3 tablespoons water per egg) and use coconut oil instead of butter. Use plant-based milk for the pudding glaze. The flavor stays bright, but the texture is slightly denser.

Note: Allow flax eggs to thicken before mixing; baking time may be a few minutes longer.

Spiced Lemon Zucchini Bread

Add 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon and 1/4 teaspoon ground nutmeg to the dry ingredients for a warm, cozy flavor that pairs perfectly with lemon. This is great for cooler months or when you want a twist on the classic.

Note: Spices may deepen the color slightly and complement the citrus notes.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This bread keeps well at room temperature for up to 2 days if wrapped tightly in plastic wrap or stored in an airtight container. Keep it away from direct sunlight or heat to prevent drying out.

Refrigerator

Store the bread in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days. The flavor remains good but the texture may firm up slightly. Warm slices gently in the microwave for 10-15 seconds before serving to soften.

Freezer

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and then foil to prevent freezer burn. Freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature wrapped to preserve moisture.

Reheating

Reheat slices on a low oven setting (about 300°F) for 5-7 minutes or microwave for 15-20 seconds. Adding a tiny splash of lemon juice or butter before warming can help refresh moisture and flavor.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a day ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Bake fresh the next day for best results. Alternatively, bake and freeze the loaf, then thaw and glaze just before serving.

FAQ

Can I use yellow squash instead of zucchini?
Yes! Yellow squash works similarly to zucchini in this recipe because it has comparable moisture and texture. Just grate and squeeze out excess water as you would with zucchini to keep the bread from becoming soggy.
What if I don’t have lemon pudding mix?
If you don’t have lemon pudding mix, you can substitute with a teaspoon of cornstarch mixed with a bit of lemon zest to help with moisture retention. However, the pudding mix adds a unique texture and flavor that’s hard to replicate exactly.
How do I know when the bread is fully baked?
Insert a toothpick into the center of the loaf—if it comes out clean or with a few moist crumbs, the bread is done. If batter sticks to the toothpick, give it more time and test again every 5 minutes to avoid overbaking.
Can I make mini muffins instead of a loaf?
Absolutely! Use a mini muffin tin and reduce baking time to about 15-20 minutes. Keep an eye on them as they’ll bake faster. The texture and flavor remain the same, just in bite-sized portions.
Why is my bread dense or heavy?
Common causes include overmixing the batter, not creaming butter and sugar enough, or adding too much moisture from zucchini. Make sure to follow the steps carefully, especially squeezing out zucchini water and mixing gently.
Can I freeze the baked bread?
Yes, wrap the cooled bread tightly in plastic wrap and foil to prevent freezer burn. It freezes well for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge or at room temperature before serving.
Is this recipe suitable for gluten-free diets?
You can make it gluten-free by swapping the flour for a gluten-free baking blend that includes xanthan gum. The texture will be a bit different but still tasty. Just make sure your baking powder is gluten-free as well.
Can I add nuts or seeds?
Yes, folding in 1/2 cup chopped walnuts, pecans, or pumpkin seeds adds great crunch and flavor. Add them with the zucchini to distribute evenly.
