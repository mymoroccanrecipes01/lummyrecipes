Cheesy Tomato Rice That’s Quick, Cozy, and Totally Delicious

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 3, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This cheesy tomato rice is a simple, comforting dish with a rich tomato base and melty cheese throughout. It’s a quick fix that works great for busy weeknights or as a cozy side.

Introduction

I first stumbled upon this cheesy tomato rice recipe on a hectic weeknight when I needed something warm and satisfying without a lot of fuss. It quickly became a favorite in my house because it’s so comforting and easy to make. What I love about this version is the balance of a rich tomato base with two kinds of cheese that melt beautifully throughout the rice, creating a creamy texture without being heavy. I usually start by sautéing onion and garlic to build flavor, then simmer the rice in crushed tomatoes and broth for a vibrant taste. Adding mozzarella and Parmesan at the end is the trick that makes this dish irresistibly gooey and flavorful. It’s perfect for when you want a cozy side or a simple main dish. The final dish is bright, cheesy, and just a little tangy from the tomatoes, with a lovely herbaceous hint if you add fresh basil. Honestly, it’s one of those recipes I keep coming back to because it feels like a warm hug on a plate.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 1 cup long grain white rice, rinsed
  • 2 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 cup shredded mozzarella cheese
  • 1/2 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh basil leaves for garnish (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Heat the olive oil in a medium saucepan over medium heat. Add the chopped onion and sauté for about 3-4 minutes until it becomes translucent and soft. This step is crucial because cooking the onion first releases its natural sweetness and builds a flavor base for the dish. Avoid browning the onion too much; you want it soft but not caramelized, as that would change the flavor profile. Stir frequently to prevent sticking or burning.
⏱ Time: 4 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions look translucent and slightly glossy without browning
Step 2
Add the minced garlic to the pan and cook for 30 seconds to 1 minute, stirring constantly. Garlic cooks quickly and can burn easily, so watch it closely. You want it fragrant but not browned, which would make it bitter. This step infuses the oil with garlicky aroma, enhancing the overall taste of the rice.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Garlic is fragrant and golden but not dark brown
Step 3
Pour in the rinsed rice and stir it into the onion and garlic mixture. Toast the rice for 2-3 minutes, stirring often. Toasting the rice helps to slightly dry the grains and gives a nuttier flavor once cooked. It also helps the rice stay fluffy and less sticky later. Don’t rush this step; even color and gentle toasting are key.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice grains appear slightly translucent and smell nutty
Step 4
Add the crushed tomatoes, vegetable broth, dried oregano, salt, and pepper to the saucepan. Stir well to combine everything evenly. The liquid should cover the rice fully. This mixture will simmer and cook the rice, absorbing all the rich tomato and herb flavors. Using broth instead of water adds depth, and the oregano gives a subtle earthiness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture looks evenly combined with rice submerged
Step 5
Bring the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat, then reduce the heat to low and cover the saucepan with a tight-fitting lid. Let it simmer gently for 18-20 minutes without lifting the lid. The steam cooks the rice evenly. Resist the temptation to stir during this time to avoid breaking the grains or making the rice mushy.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice has absorbed all liquid and is tender but not mushy
Step 6
Once the rice is cooked, remove the saucepan from heat and let it sit, still covered, for 5 minutes. This resting period allows the rice to finish steaming and helps the texture become fluffier. It also lets the flavors meld together better, making the dish taste richer.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Rice looks fluffy and moist with no excess liquid
Step 7
Remove the lid and gently fluff the rice with a fork. Then immediately stir in the shredded mozzarella and grated Parmesan cheese. The residual heat will melt the cheeses perfectly, creating a creamy, cheesy texture without the need for extra cooking. Quick stirring helps the cheese distribute evenly throughout the rice.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cheese is melted and rice looks creamy and cohesive
Step 8
Taste the rice and adjust seasoning with extra salt and pepper if needed. Sometimes the tomato and broth can vary in saltiness, so this step ensures perfect balance. Also, if you want a little extra brightness, a squeeze of lemon juice or a sprinkle of fresh herbs like basil or parsley works wonders here.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced savory flavor with no bland or overpowering notes
Step 9
Serve the cheesy tomato rice warm, garnished with fresh basil leaves if desired. This dish pairs well with a simple green salad or roasted vegetables for a complete meal. Leftovers reheat beautifully, so it’s perfect for making ahead or meal prep.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Rice is hot, creamy, and garnished attractively

Pro Tips

Always rinse your rice before cooking.
Rinsing removes excess starch from the surface of the rice grains, which prevents them from sticking together and becoming mushy. This step ensures a fluffier texture and a cleaner flavor, especially important in a dish where each grain should remain distinct.
Don’t lift the lid during the simmering stage.
Keeping the lid on traps steam inside, cooking the rice evenly. Lifting the lid releases steam and can cause uneven cooking or dry out the rice. Patience here is key to perfect texture without mushiness or undercooked grains.
Use a tight-fitting lid for simmering.
A tight lid prevents moisture from escaping, ensuring the rice absorbs the correct amount of liquid. If your lid isn’t snug, steam will escape, potentially drying out the rice or requiring additional liquid, which can throw off cooking times and texture.
Add cheese off the heat to avoid grainy texture.
Adding cheese when the pot is off the heat but still warm lets it melt gently and evenly. High direct heat can cause the cheese to separate, becoming greasy or grainy. This technique keeps the rice creamy and smooth.
Use fresh herbs like basil for garnish.
Fresh herbs add a pop of color and a bright, fresh flavor that contrasts nicely with the rich tomato and cheese. Adding them at the end preserves their aroma and texture, enhancing the dish’s overall appeal.
Toast the rice before adding liquid.
Toasting slightly dries the grains and develops nutty flavors through Maillard reactions. This step improves texture by helping the rice stay fluffy and adds complexity to the dish’s flavor profile.
Use a good quality vegetable broth or homemade stock.
Broth adds savory depth and richness that water can’t provide. Quality broth enhances the tomato flavor and balances the dish, making it more satisfying and flavorful.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using too much liquid causing mushy rice.

Why it happens Many cooks assume more liquid means softer rice, but too much water makes the rice overcooked and mushy. It’s a common misunderstanding about rice-to-liquid ratios.

Fix Measure liquid carefully and follow the recipe ratios. Use a tight lid to trap steam so the rice cooks properly without excess moisture. If rice is mushy, try reducing liquid slightly next time and avoid stirring while cooking.

Not rinsing the rice before cooking.

Why it happens People often skip rinsing to save time or out of habit, unaware that starch buildup causes clumping.

Fix Rinse rice under cold water until water runs clear before cooking. This prevents sticky, gummy texture and results in fluffier rice.

Adding cheese too early or cooking it too long.

Why it happens Some add cheese before the rice is fully cooked, or keep heating it, causing cheese to separate or become grainy.

Fix Add cheese off the heat after rice has rested. Stir gently to melt cheese evenly without overheating, preserving creamy texture.

Lifting the lid during cooking.

Why it happens Curiosity or impatience leads to lifting the lid, releasing steam and disrupting cooking.

Fix Trust the process and keep the lid closed during simmering. This traps steam and cooks rice evenly.

Using low-quality canned tomatoes.

Why it happens Choosing cheap or overly processed tomatoes can make the dish taste bland or metallic.

Fix Use good-quality canned crushed tomatoes with no added preservatives or sugars. This ensures fresh, vibrant tomato flavor.

Variations

Spicy Cheesy Tomato Rice

Add 1/2 teaspoon of red pepper flakes or chopped jalapeño during the onion sauté step for a spicy kick. This variation adds warmth and depth, perfect for those who like a little heat. The cheese helps mellow the spice for balanced flavor.

Note: Monitor spice levels carefully; adding too early can intensify heat excessively. Adding during sautéing releases flavors better.

Herbed Cheesy Tomato Rice with Fresh Basil and Oregano

Stir in 2 tablespoons of chopped fresh basil and oregano just before serving for a fresh, herbal twist. This brightens the dish and adds a garden-fresh aroma that complements the tomato and cheese beautifully.

Note: Add fresh herbs off heat to preserve their delicate flavors and prevent bitterness.

Vegan Cheesy Tomato Rice

Replace mozzarella and Parmesan with vegan cheese alternatives or nutritional yeast for a dairy-free version. Use vegetable broth as usual and consider adding a tablespoon of cashew cream for extra creaminess.

Note: Vegan cheeses melt differently; stir gently and adjust amounts to avoid clumping or oily texture.

Tomato Rice with Roasted Vegetables

Mix in roasted vegetables like zucchini, bell peppers, or eggplant after cooking the rice. This adds texture, color, and extra nutrients, turning the dish into a hearty vegetarian main.

Note: Roast vegetables separately to maintain their texture and avoid sogginess in the rice.

Cheesy Tomato Rice with Chicken

Add cooked, shredded chicken breast during the cheese stirring step to make this a protein-packed meal. The chicken absorbs the tomato and cheese flavors, making it perfect for dinner.

Note: Ensure chicken is fully cooked before adding to avoid food safety issues and maintain texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This cheesy tomato rice can be safely kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely. Beyond that, bacteria growth increases, so refrigerate promptly. Cover with foil or a clean kitchen towel to keep dust out.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. The rice may firm up when cold; reheat gently with a splash of broth or water to restore creaminess. It’s best enjoyed warm but can also be eaten cold in salads.

Freezer

Freeze leftover rice in a freezer-safe container or heavy-duty zip-top bag for up to 2 months. Press out excess air before sealing to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently on the stovetop or microwave with added liquid to maintain texture.

Reheating

Reheat on the stovetop over low heat or in the microwave, adding 1-2 tablespoons of broth or water to loosen the rice and keep it creamy. Stir occasionally to heat evenly. Avoid overheating or reheating multiple times, which can dry it out or cause the cheese to separate.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the tomato and broth mixture 1-2 days ahead and store it in the fridge. Cook the rice fresh when ready to serve, then stir in cheeses at the end. This keeps the texture fresh and prevents the cheese from becoming rubbery.

FAQ

Can I use brown rice instead of white rice?
Yes, you can substitute brown rice, but it requires longer cooking time and more liquid. Use about 2 1/2 cups of broth and simmer for 40-45 minutes. The texture will be nuttier and chewier, and the dish will be heartier but less creamy.
What if I don’t have crushed tomatoes?
You can use whole peeled tomatoes and crush them by hand or with a spoon before cooking. Alternatively, tomato sauce works but may be smoother and less chunky. Adjust seasoning as tomato types vary in acidity and sweetness.
How can I make this recipe vegan?
Replace the mozzarella and Parmesan with plant-based cheese alternatives or nutritional yeast for cheesy flavor. Use vegetable broth as usual and consider adding soaked cashew cream for richness. Keep in mind that texture will differ slightly.
Can I prepare this dish in a rice cooker?
Yes, you can combine all ingredients except cheese in the rice cooker and cook on the white rice setting. Add cheese after cooking, stirring well to melt. Cooking times may vary by rice cooker model.
Why is my rice mushy or sticky?
This usually happens if you add too much liquid or stir the rice while cooking. Be sure to measure liquids carefully and avoid stirring during simmering. Also, rinsing the rice before cooking helps prevent stickiness.
Can I add other vegetables to this dish?
Absolutely! Roasted or sautéed vegetables like zucchini, bell peppers, or spinach can be stirred in after cooking. This adds nutrition and flavor without changing cooking times.
How do I store leftovers properly?
Cool leftovers to room temperature, then store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat gently with added broth or water to maintain creaminess. You can also freeze for up to 2 months.
What cheese works best if I can’t find mozzarella?
Mild, melty cheeses like Monterey Jack or fontina can substitute for mozzarella. Avoid hard cheeses or those that don’t melt well, as they’ll affect texture and flavor.
