Cozy Spiced Pear Dessert with Warm Cinnamon and Cloves

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 19, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This cozy spiced pear dessert is my go-to for chilly nights. The pears simmer slowly in a fragrant cinnamon-clove syrup that fills the kitchen with warmth. Perfect for a simple weeknight treat or impressing guests without fuss.

Introduction

I first made this spiced pear dessert on a crisp autumn evening when I wanted something warm and comforting without spending hours in the kitchen. What I love about this recipe is how it transforms simple pears into a fragrant treat with just a few pantry spices. The secret is gently simmering the pears in a syrup infused with cinnamon and cloves, which lets the fruit soak up all those cozy flavors without turning mushy. I’ve tried a few variations over the years, and the trick I learned is to add the lemon juice and vanilla right at the end — it brightens up the syrup and balances the sweetness beautifully. When it's done, the pears are tender but still hold their shape, and the syrup is rich and aromatic. It’s perfect served warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or just on its own with a cup of tea. Honestly, this dessert feels like a warm hug on a plate, and it’s become a staple whenever I want to celebrate the season without fuss. Plus, it’s easy enough to whip up on a weeknight but special enough to share with guests.

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 4 ripe pears, peeled, cored, and halved
  • 1/2 cup brown sugar, packed
  • 1 cup water
  • 2 cinnamon sticks
  • 4 whole cloves
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • Pinch of salt

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by peeling, coring, and halving the pears. It’s important to choose ripe but firm pears so they hold their shape while cooking. If the pears are too soft, they’ll fall apart in the syrup. Once prepped, set them aside while you prepare the spiced syrup.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pears are peeled and halved evenly without bruises or mushy spots
Step 2
In a medium saucepan, combine the water, brown sugar, cinnamon sticks, cloves, and a pinch of salt. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer over medium heat, stirring occasionally until the sugar dissolves. This syrup is the flavor base, so dissolving the sugar fully ensures an even sweetness throughout.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sugar is fully dissolved and the syrup is fragrant with spices
Step 3
Add the pear halves to the simmering syrup, placing them cut side down if possible. Reduce the heat to low and let the pears gently poach. Simmering low and slow helps the pears absorb the spice flavors without breaking down too quickly. Avoid boiling, or the pears will become mushy.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pears are tender when pierced with a fork but still hold shape
Step 4
Halfway through poaching, carefully turn the pears to ensure even cooking and flavor absorption. This step is key to avoid uneven texture or spots that are too soft. Use a slotted spoon to flip gently to prevent breaking the fruit.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pears are evenly coated and simmered on both sides
Step 5
Once the pears are tender, remove them gently from the syrup and set aside. Increase the heat to medium-high and let the syrup reduce slightly until it thickens to a glaze consistency. This intensifies the flavors and gives a lovely sheen to spoon over the pears.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Syrup coats the back of a spoon and is slightly thickened
Step 6
Remove the cinnamon sticks and cloves from the syrup. Stir in the lemon juice and vanilla extract off the heat. These final touches brighten the syrup and add depth to the flavor profile. Adding them too early can cause the vanilla to lose its aroma and the lemon to cook off.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Syrup smells fragrant with citrus and vanilla notes
Step 7
Place the pears back into the syrup briefly to warm through and absorb the brightened flavors. This final soak infuses the pears with the fresh notes and ensures every bite is flavorful. Don’t leave them too long or they’ll get too soft.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: Pears glisten with syrup and are warm to the touch
Step 8
Serve the pears warm, drizzled generously with the spiced syrup. They’re wonderful on their own, but also amazing with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a dollop of whipped cream. The contrast of cold creamy topping with warm spiced fruit is heavenly.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Pears plated attractively with syrup and optional toppings
Step 9
If you want to prepare ahead, cool the spiced pears and syrup completely before refrigerating. Reheat gently on the stove or in the microwave before serving to revive the warm, comforting flavors and texture.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Pears reheated just until warm, syrup glossy and fluid

Pro Tips

Use firm, ripe pears to keep the texture intact during poaching.
Firm pears hold their shape better when simmered gently in syrup. Overripe or soft pears tend to break down and become mushy, which can ruin the presentation and mouthfeel of the dessert. Choosing the right pear variety is key to success.
Simmer the pears gently, avoiding a hard boil.
A gentle simmer allows the pears to absorb the syrup’s flavors slowly without breaking apart. Boiling causes the fruit to disintegrate quickly and results in an uneven texture. Controlling the heat ensures tender, intact pears infused with spice.
Add lemon juice and vanilla extract off the heat to preserve brightness and aroma.
Adding these ingredients after removing the syrup from heat retains their fresh, vibrant qualities. Heat can degrade vanilla’s delicate aroma and cause lemon juice to lose its acidity, which would flatten the flavor balance in the syrup.
Flip pears halfway through cooking for even flavor absorption.
Turning the pears ensures all sides are equally exposed to the spiced syrup, preventing uneven cooking or flavor distribution. This simple step results in a more consistent texture and taste throughout each pear half.
Reduce the syrup after poaching to intensify flavor and achieve a glaze.
Reducing concentrates the sugar and spice flavors, thickening the syrup to coat the pears beautifully. Without this step, the sauce can be too thin and less flavorful, detracting from the dessert’s appeal.
Serve warm with vanilla ice cream or whipped cream for contrast.
The cold creaminess contrasts wonderfully with the warm, spiced pears, enhancing the overall eating experience. This temperature and texture contrast makes the dessert feel special and indulgent.
Store pears and syrup separately if making ahead to maintain texture.
Keeping the pears and syrup apart prevents the fruit from becoming overly soft as it sits. Reheating them together just before serving ensures the pears stay tender but not mushy, preserving the ideal texture.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using overly ripe pears that fall apart when cooked.

Why it happens Many cooks assume any ripe pear will work, but soft pears break down quickly during simmering, leading to a mushy dessert.

Fix Select firmer varieties like Bosc or Anjou and check pears for firmness before cooking. If pears are too soft, reduce cooking time and handle gently to avoid disintegration.

Boiling the syrup instead of simmering gently.

Why it happens Rushing the cooking process by increasing heat causes pears to cook too fast and lose shape.

Fix Maintain a low simmer, just a few bubbles around the edges. This slow cooking lets pears absorb flavors evenly and stay intact.

Skipping the syrup reduction step.

Why it happens Some skip reducing syrup to save time, resulting in a watery sauce with diluted flavor.

Fix After removing pears, simmer syrup until it thickens slightly to coat the back of a spoon. This intensifies flavor and improves texture.

Adding lemon juice or vanilla too early.

Why it happens Adding these while the syrup is boiling causes loss of aroma and acidity.

Fix Stir in lemon juice and vanilla only after removing syrup from heat to preserve their fresh flavors.

Not turning pears during cooking.

Why it happens Leaving pears on one side leads to uneven cooking and flavor absorption.

Fix Carefully flip pears halfway through poaching to ensure even exposure to syrup and uniform tenderness.

Variations

Spiced Pears with Orange Zest and Star Anise

Add a twist by including orange zest and a star anise pod in the syrup. The citrus adds brightness, and star anise brings a subtle licorice note that complements the cinnamon and cloves beautifully. Expect a more complex aromatic profile and slightly different flavor depth.

Note: Simmer the orange zest and star anise with the syrup from the start; remove before adding lemon and vanilla to avoid bitterness.

Maple Syrup Glazed Spiced Pears

Swap brown sugar for pure maple syrup for a richer, woodsy sweetness. This variation lends a deeper, more robust flavor and a slightly stickier glaze. It’s perfect if you want to add a natural sweetness with a hint of maple aroma.

Note: Reduce the syrup gently and monitor thickness closely, as maple syrup can caramelize faster than sugar.

Spiced Pears with Ginger and Cardamom

Introduce fresh sliced ginger and crushed cardamom pods to the syrup for a warming, slightly spicy twist. This variation adds complexity and a subtle heat that pairs wonderfully with the pear’s sweetness.

Note: Remove ginger slices and cardamom pods before serving to avoid overpowering bites.

Roasted Spiced Pears with Honey Drizzle

Instead of poaching, roast the pears with cinnamon and cloves, then drizzle with honey before serving. Roasting caramelizes the sugars in the pears, offering a deeper flavor and a slightly firmer texture than poaching.

Note: Roast at 375°F for 20-25 minutes; watch carefully to prevent burning the spices.

Vegan Spiced Pears with Coconut Cream

Serve the spiced pears topped with whipped coconut cream instead of dairy ice cream or cream. This keeps the dessert vegan-friendly while adding a creamy, tropical note that complements the warm spices.

Note: Chill coconut cream overnight and whip just before serving for best texture.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

The spiced pears can safely sit covered at room temperature for up to 2 hours. Beyond this, refrigeration is recommended to prevent spoilage. Keep the syrup and pears covered with plastic wrap or in a sealed container to avoid drying out.

Refrigerator

Store the pears and syrup separately in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 4 days. The syrup may thicken when cold; gently warm before serving. Pears may soften slightly but will remain delicious when reheated.

Freezer

Freeze the pears and syrup separately for up to 2 months. Wrap pears tightly in plastic wrap and place syrup in a freezer-safe container. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently to restore texture and flavor.

Reheating

Reheat the pears and syrup gently on the stovetop over low heat or in short bursts in the microwave. Add a splash of water if the syrup is too thick. Avoid overheating to prevent the pears from becoming mushy.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the syrup and poach the pears 1-2 days ahead. Store separately in the fridge and reheat together just before serving. This saves time while keeping textures intact and flavors fresh.

FAQ

Can I use canned pears instead of fresh pears?
Canned pears are softer and already cooked, so they won’t hold up well to poaching and may become mushy. For best texture and flavor, fresh firm pears like Bosc or Anjou are recommended. If using canned pears, add them to warmed syrup only at the end to avoid overcooking.
How do I make this dessert vegan?
The recipe is naturally vegan if you avoid serving it with dairy toppings. Use plant-based ice cream or coconut whipped cream instead of dairy ice cream or cream for a delicious vegan-friendly dessert.
Can I make this dessert ahead of time?
Yes! You can poach the pears and make the syrup 1-2 days in advance. Store them separately in the fridge and reheat gently before serving to maintain the best texture and flavor.
What if I don’t have whole cloves or cinnamon sticks?
You can substitute ground spices, but use them sparingly as they are more concentrated and can cause gritty texture. Add ground spices towards the end of cooking and stir well to avoid clumps.
Can I add other spices to the syrup?
Absolutely! Star anise, cardamom, or fresh ginger work beautifully with pears and warm spices. Just add them at the start of simmering and remove before serving to avoid overpowering flavors.
How do I know when the pears are done poaching?
Pears are ready when they are tender enough to pierce easily with a fork but still hold their shape. Overcooking will cause them to become mushy, so check regularly after 15 minutes of simmering.
Can I use white sugar instead of brown sugar?
Yes, but brown sugar adds a richer, caramel-like flavor that complements the spices. White sugar will work but results in a lighter flavor and color in the syrup.
Is it okay to eat this dessert cold?
Definitely! While it’s best served warm for maximum comfort, the spiced pears are delicious cold or at room temperature. Just note the syrup will be thicker and flavors a bit more muted when chilled.
