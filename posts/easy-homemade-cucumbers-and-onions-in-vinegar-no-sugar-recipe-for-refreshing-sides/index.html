Simple Cucumber and Onion Salad in Tangy Vinegar Dressing

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 5, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crisp cucumber and onion salad is a refreshing side with a tangy vinegar soak that softens onions but keeps cucumbers crunchy. Perfect for summer dinners or to cut through rich comfort food.

Introduction

I've been making this cucumber and onion salad for years, especially when summer hits and I want something light and refreshing. The first time I tried it was at a friend's barbecue, and I was hooked immediately. What I love about this version is how the vinegar soak perfectly softens the sharpness of the onions without making them mushy, while the cucumbers stay crisp and cool. Many recipes add sugar, but I prefer it without because it lets the natural tang shine through. The trick I learned is to slice everything thinly and let it marinate for at least 30 minutes before serving. That soak time really melds the flavors and creates a refreshing bite that pairs beautifully with grilled meats or heavier dishes. When you taste it, you get that perfect balance of acidity, crunch, and subtle sweetness if you decide to add a touch of sugar. It’s simple, fuss-free, and always a crowd-pleaser in my family. Plus, it’s so easy to whip up last minute or ahead of time, making it a go-to side for warm-weather meals.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 cups, 4 servings

  • 2 large cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup white vinegar
  • 1/2 cup cold water
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon sugar (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by washing your cucumbers thoroughly, then slice them as thinly as possible. Thin slices are key because they absorb the vinegar dressing better and create a delicate texture that’s easy to eat. Using a sharp knife or mandoline helps achieve uniform slices, which also makes the salad look prettier. Place the cucumber slices in a large bowl to prepare for the next steps. Avoid thick slices because they won’t absorb the flavors as well and can feel too bulky in the salad.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers should be uniformly thin and look translucent at the edges
Step 2
Peel and thinly slice the red onion into half-moon shapes. The thin slicing softens the onion’s sharp bite once it soaks in the vinegar. If you slice the onion too thick, it will remain overpowering and harsh in the salad. Add the onions to the bowl with cucumbers and mix gently to combine. The thin slices also blend better with the cucumbers, creating a balanced bite in every forkful.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onion slices are translucent and finely cut, not chunky
Step 3
In a separate bowl, whisk together the white vinegar, cold water, salt, pepper, and sugar (if using). The vinegar is the star here, providing that tangy zip. The water dilutes the vinegar slightly so it’s not too harsh. Salt enhances the flavors and draws some moisture from the cucumbers, helping them stay crisp. Pepper adds a subtle background heat to balance the acidity. Sugar, while optional, rounds out the sharpness if you prefer a milder taste.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing is well combined with no salt or sugar granules visible
Step 4
Pour the vinegar dressing over the cucumbers and onions, then toss gently but thoroughly. You want every slice coated to ensure consistent flavor. Use a large spoon or your hands to mix carefully so you don’t bruise the vegetables. This coating step is crucial because the vinegar starts softening the onions immediately and infuses flavor into the cucumbers.
⏱ Time: 3 minutes
✓ Doneness: All cucumber and onion slices glisten evenly with dressing
Step 5
Cover the bowl with plastic wrap or a lid and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes, preferably an hour. This resting time allows the flavors to meld and the vinegar to soften the onions without losing the crunch of the cucumbers. If you skip this step, the salad will taste sharp and less balanced. The chill also keeps everything crisp and refreshing.
⏱ Time: 30-60 minutes
✓ Doneness: Onions are visibly softer but cucumbers remain firm and crisp
Step 6
Before serving, give the salad a quick stir to redistribute the dressing. Taste and adjust seasoning if needed—sometimes a pinch more salt or pepper can brighten the flavors. If the salad seems too tangy, a splash more cold water can mellow it out. This final tweak personalizes the salad to your preference.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced flavor with no overpowering sharpness or blandness
Step 7
Serve the salad chilled as a crisp, tangy side dish. It pairs wonderfully with grilled chicken, pork, or even spicy dishes where you want a cooling contrast. The crunchy texture and bright vinegar notes help cut through richness and refresh the palate between bites.
⏱ Time: Immediate
✓ Doneness: Salad is cold, fresh, and vibrant in color
Step 8
If you want to elevate this salad, try adding fresh dill or chopped fresh herbs like parsley or chives before serving. Herbs add a lovely herbal aroma and complexity that pairs perfectly with the vinegar. Just sprinkle a tablespoon or two for a fresh twist.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Herbs look fresh and vibrant, complementing the salad visually and aromatically
Step 9
For a little extra texture and flavor, toss in some toasted sunflower seeds or thinly sliced radishes just before serving. They add crunch and a subtle nuttiness that makes the salad even more interesting. Be careful not to add too early or they’ll lose their crispness.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Seeds or radishes remain crunchy and fresh in the salad

Pro Tips

Always slice cucumbers and onions as thinly as possible for the best texture and flavor absorption.
Thin slices increase the surface area exposed to the vinegar dressing, allowing flavors to infuse evenly while keeping the cucumbers crisp and onions tender but not overpowering. Thick slices often lead to uneven flavor and a less pleasant texture.
Let the salad chill in the fridge for at least 30 minutes before serving.
Chilling allows the vinegar to mellow the sharpness of the onions and helps the cucumbers absorb flavors without losing crunch. Serving immediately means the flavors won’t meld and the onions will taste too harsh.
Use a sharp knife or mandoline to ensure uniform slices.
Uniform slices cook evenly in the sense of softening and flavor absorption. Jagged or uneven cuts can cause some pieces to become mushy while others remain tough, ruining the salad’s texture harmony.
Adjust salt and pepper at the end after marinating to fine-tune the seasoning.
Salt and pepper can intensify during marinating. Waiting until the end lets you balance the flavors perfectly without risking over-seasoning, which can mask the fresh, bright notes of the salad.
Add fresh herbs like dill or parsley for an aromatic boost.
Herbs add freshness and visual appeal, complementing the vinegar’s tang and balancing the salad’s acidity with herbal notes. They also make the salad feel more vibrant and homemade.
Optional sugar softens the vinegar's sharpness, but try it without first.
Sugar rounds out the acidity, making the salad more approachable for those sensitive to sourness. However, skipping sugar lets the natural brightness shine, which is great for those who prefer a cleaner, tart flavor.
Serve chilled and fresh for best crunch; avoid letting it sit out too long.
The salad’s appeal is its crisp texture and refreshing tang. Leaving it at room temperature for extended periods causes cucumbers to soften and flavors to dull, losing the key qualities that make this salad so enjoyable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using thick cucumber or onion slices

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how slicing thickness affects texture and flavor absorption, thinking chunkier pieces hold up better.

Fix Use a sharp knife or mandoline to slice ingredients thinly and evenly. This ensures the vinegar can penetrate properly, softening onions and keeping cucumbers crisp, creating a balanced texture every bite.

Skipping the marinating time

Why it happens Eagerness to serve often leads to skipping the chilling step, resulting in a sharp, unbalanced salad.

Fix Plan ahead and refrigerate the salad for at least 30 minutes. This resting period mellows the onion’s sharpness and allows flavors to meld, creating a more harmonious taste.

Over-salting before marinating

Why it happens Some think more salt always improves flavor, but salt concentration intensifies during soaking.

Fix Start with the recommended salt amount and adjust seasoning after marinating. This prevents an overly salty salad and preserves the natural crispness of cucumbers.

Using low-quality vinegar

Why it happens Buying the cheapest vinegar or flavored varieties can alter the salad’s taste negatively.

Fix Choose a good-quality, clear white vinegar with clean acidity. Avoid flavored or colored vinegars that can overpower or discolor the salad.

Not drying cucumbers before mixing

Why it happens Some add cucumbers straight after washing, which can water down the dressing and make the salad soggy.

Fix Pat cucumbers dry with a towel before slicing to prevent excess water from diluting the vinegar dressing and spoiling the salad’s crisp texture.

Variations

Add Fresh Dill and Lemon Zest

For a brighter, herbaceous twist, add chopped fresh dill and a teaspoon of lemon zest to the salad before serving. The dill complements the vinegar’s tang, while lemon zest adds a fresh citrus aroma. This variation pairs beautifully with seafood dishes or light summer meals.

Note: Add herbs and zest just before serving to preserve their freshness and avoid bitterness from over-soaking.

Spicy Kick with Red Pepper Flakes

If you like a little heat, sprinkle in 1/4 teaspoon of red pepper flakes into the vinegar dressing. This adds a subtle warmth that contrasts nicely with the cool cucumbers. Great for those who want a spicy side without overpowering the main dish.

Note: Add flakes to the dressing early so the heat infuses evenly, but use sparingly to keep balance.

Creamy Cucumber and Onion Salad

Mix in 2 tablespoons of sour cream or Greek yogurt before serving for a creamy version. This softens the acidity and gives the salad a rich texture while retaining the crispness of cucumbers. Perfect for a picnic or potluck.

Note: Add the dairy just before serving to prevent it from curdling or diluting the vinaigrette.

Sweet and Tangy with Honey and Apple Cider Vinegar

Swap white vinegar for apple cider vinegar and add a teaspoon of honey for a sweeter, fruitier profile. This variation is delicious with roasted meats or fall-inspired meals, offering a cozy twist on the classic salad.

Note: Honey dissolves better in warm water, so mix the dressing thoroughly before combining.

Crunchy Cucumber and Onion Slaw with Carrots

Add shredded carrots for extra color, crunch, and sweetness. This makes the salad heartier and visually appealing, perfect for serving alongside barbecue or sandwiches.

Note: Use fresh, crisp carrots shredded finely to match the texture of cucumbers and onions.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad should not be left out at room temperature for more than 2 hours as the vinegar soak can encourage bacterial growth and the cucumbers will lose crispness. Cover with plastic wrap if serving outdoors temporarily.

Refrigerator

Store the salad in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The flavor will deepen, but cucumbers may soften slightly over time. It’s best served chilled and fresh, so stir before serving.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers have high water content and become mushy when thawed. Instead, prepare fresh or store refrigerated for best texture.

Reheating

This salad is served cold and does not require reheating. Avoid warming as it will break down the crisp texture and alter flavor.

Make Ahead

You can slice the cucumbers and onions and prepare the dressing a day ahead, storing them separately. Combine and marinate for 30 minutes before serving for best freshness.

FAQ

Can I use other types of vinegar besides white vinegar?
Yes, you can use apple cider vinegar or rice vinegar for a different flavor profile. Apple cider vinegar adds a fruity sweetness, while rice vinegar is milder and less acidic. Just adjust the quantity to taste, as some vinegars are stronger and might overpower the salad if used in the same amount.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
Stored in an airtight container, this salad keeps well for up to 3 days. Over time, the cucumbers will soften and the flavors intensify. For best texture, consume within 24-48 hours. Stir before serving to redistribute the dressing and freshen the flavors.
Is it necessary to add sugar to the dressing?
Sugar is optional and helps balance the acidity of the vinegar, making the salad taste less sharp. If you prefer a more natural, tangy flavor, you can skip it. Some people like the brightness without any added sweetness, so it’s really up to your taste preference.
Can I substitute red onion with another type?
You can use yellow or white onions, but keep in mind they tend to be more pungent and less sweet than red onions. To reduce sharpness, soak the sliced onions in cold water for 10 minutes before adding to the salad. This helps mellow their flavor.
How thin should I slice the cucumbers and onions?
Aim for slices about 1/8 inch thick or thinner. Thin slices absorb the vinegar dressing better and create a more pleasant texture. Thick slices can taste raw and overpower the salad’s balance.
Why is the salad sometimes watery after marinating?
Cucumbers have high water content, so they release moisture when salted and soaked. This is normal and helps keep the salad crisp. To reduce excess liquid, you can drain some off before serving or pat dry cucumbers before mixing.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Absolutely! Shredded carrots, radishes, or bell peppers make great additions and add color and crunch. Just be sure to slice them thinly to maintain the salad’s light texture and allow flavors to meld well.
Is this salad suitable for meal prep?
Yes, it’s great for meal prep if you keep the dressing separate and combine just before eating. Cucumbers can soften if dressed too early, so storing components separately helps maintain crispness and freshness.
