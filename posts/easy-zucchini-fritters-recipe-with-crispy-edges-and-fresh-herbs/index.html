Easy Crispy Zucchini Fritters with Fresh Herbs for Light Lunch

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 15, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

These zucchini fritters are my go-to when I want something light but satisfying. Crispy on the outside, tender inside, and that fresh parsley really wakes it up. Great for lunch or a quick side anytime!

Introduction

I first stumbled upon zucchini fritters years ago when I had a surplus of zucchini from my garden and wanted something quick and tasty. What I love about this recipe is how the fritters come out crispy on the edges but stay tender and flavorful inside thanks to the fresh parsley and garlic. The trick I learned is to really squeeze out the zucchini moisture before mixing it in—that's what keeps the fritters from getting soggy. I also add a bit of Parmesan for a subtle savory depth that really makes these special. They’re perfect for a light lunch or a simple side dish, especially when you want something fresh but satisfying. Honestly, my family always asks for seconds, and they’re easy enough to whip up on a weeknight without any fuss. If you like a little crunch and bright herb flavor, you’re going to love these fritters as much as I do.

What You'll Need

Approximately 12 fritters, 4 servings

  • 3 medium zucchinis, grated and squeezed dry
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup grated Parmesan cheese
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, chopped
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Olive oil for frying

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by grating the zucchinis using the large holes of a box grater. Place the grated zucchini in a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth and squeeze out as much moisture as you can. This step is crucial because zucchini holds a lot of water, and removing excess liquid ensures your fritters will be crispy rather than soggy. If you skip this, the batter will be too wet, and the fritters might fall apart when cooking.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Zucchini feels dry to the touch and no more dripping liquid when squeezed
Step 2
In a large mixing bowl, combine the dried zucchini with salt, flour, grated Parmesan, minced garlic, chopped parsley, black pepper, and beaten eggs. Mix well until everything is evenly incorporated. The flour and eggs act as binders here, helping the fritters hold their shape during frying. If you rush this or don’t mix thoroughly, the fritters might crumble or cook unevenly.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Mixture is sticky and holds together when pressed
Step 3
Heat a generous layer of olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. You want enough oil to coat the bottom but not so much that the fritters are swimming. The right temperature is key here—too hot and the fritters will burn outside while staying raw inside; too low and they'll absorb too much oil and become greasy. Test a small spoonful of batter first to check the oil’s heat.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Oil shimmers gently and batter sizzles immediately when dropped in
Step 4
Spoon about 2 tablespoons of the batter per fritter into the hot oil, flattening gently with the back of the spoon to form small patties. Don’t overcrowd the pan because that lowers the oil temperature and prevents crisping. Fry in batches if needed. This shaping step ensures even cooking and the classic fritter shape.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fritters hold shape and sizzle steadily without sticking
Step 5
Cook the fritters for about 3-4 minutes on each side, flipping carefully with a spatula once the edges turn golden brown and crisp. The Maillard reaction is what gives the fritters their beautiful brown color and savory flavor. If you flip too early, they can fall apart; too late, and they might burn.
⏱ Time: 8 minutes
✓ Doneness: Golden brown crust forms on both sides and fritters feel firm
Step 6
Transfer the cooked fritters to a plate lined with paper towels to drain excess oil. This step keeps them from becoming greasy and helps maintain crispness. Let them rest for a minute or two before serving to allow the interior to set.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Fritters slightly cooled but still warm and crisp
Step 7
Taste and adjust seasoning if needed. Sometimes a little extra sprinkle of salt or pepper right after cooking brightens the flavors. Serve warm with a dollop of sour cream or your favorite dipping sauce for an extra touch.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Flavors are balanced and fritters are pleasantly warm
Step 8
If making ahead, you can keep the fritters warm in a low oven (around 200°F) on a baking sheet while you finish cooking the rest. This prevents them from getting soggy or cold before serving.
⏱ Time: Varies
✓ Doneness: Fritters remain crisp and warm without drying out
Step 9
For serving, garnish with extra chopped parsley or a squeeze of lemon juice if you like a zesty finish. The brightness of lemon complements the herbaceous fritters perfectly and adds a fresh contrast.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Fritters look vibrant and appetizing with garnish

Pro Tips

Always squeeze out as much moisture from the zucchini as possible.
Removing excess water from zucchini prevents soggy fritters and ensures they crisp up nicely when fried. Moisture dilutes the batter, making it harder for the fritters to hold together and crisp. This step is crucial for texture.
Use a non-stick or well-seasoned skillet for frying.
A good skillet prevents the fritters from sticking and breaking apart when flipping. It also distributes heat evenly, helping develop a golden crust without burning. Using too much oil can make fritters greasy, so balance is key.
Don’t overcrowd the pan during cooking.
Crowding the pan lowers the oil temperature, causing fritters to absorb excess oil and lose crispiness. Cooking in batches keeps the oil hot and ensures even browning, which is essential for the perfect fritter texture.
Let the batter rest for 5 minutes before cooking.
Resting allows the flour to hydrate fully and the flavors to meld. This results in a better batter consistency and improved fritter texture. Skipping this can make the batter uneven and affect cooking performance.
Use fresh herbs like parsley for brightness.
Fresh herbs add a burst of flavor and aroma that dried herbs can’t match. They keep the fritters tasting light and fresh, balancing the richness from the cheese and eggs. Adding herbs just before cooking preserves their vibrant taste.
Test oil temperature with a small dollop of batter first.
This simple test prevents wasting batter and ensures the oil is hot enough for frying. If the batter sizzles immediately and floats, the temperature is right. Too hot oil burns fritters; too cool oil makes them greasy.
Serve fritters immediately for best texture.
Fritters lose their crispness as they cool and absorb moisture from the air. Serving them right away preserves the crispy exterior and tender center, delivering the best eating experience.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not removing enough moisture from zucchini

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how watery zucchini is and skip or rush the squeezing step, thinking it’s unnecessary.

Fix Use a clean kitchen towel or cheesecloth to wring out zucchini thoroughly. Press firmly and repeat until no more liquid drips. This prevents soggy, falling-apart fritters.

Using too high heat when frying

Why it happens Eager to cook quickly, some people crank up the heat, causing the outside to burn while the inside remains raw.

Fix Maintain medium heat so fritters brown evenly. Adjust heat as needed if they’re browning too fast or cooking too slowly.

Overcrowding the pan

Why it happens Trying to save time, cooks put too many fritters in the pan at once, dropping the oil temperature.

Fix Cook in batches with enough space between fritters to keep oil hot and crisp them properly.

Skipping the resting time for batter

Why it happens Impatience or lack of knowledge about how resting improves texture leads to skipping this step.

Fix Let the batter rest 5 minutes after mixing to hydrate flour and meld flavors for better fritters.

Flipping fritters too early or too often

Why it happens Inexperience or nervousness can cause flipping before a crust forms, leading to breakage.

Fix Wait until edges are golden and firm before flipping once carefully with a spatula.

Variations

Cheesy Zucchini and Feta Fritters

Swap Parmesan for crumbled feta and add a teaspoon of dried oregano for a Mediterranean twist. These fritters turn out tangy and slightly creamier. They pair wonderfully with a dollop of tzatziki or Greek yogurt.

Note: Feta adds moisture, so squeeze zucchini extra dry and consider adding a tablespoon more flour to keep batter firm.

Spicy Zucchini Fritters with Chili Flakes

Add a pinch of red chili flakes and a dash of smoked paprika for a spicy kick. These are perfect if you like a little heat. Serve with a cooling cucumber dip to balance the spice.

Note: Spices don’t affect batter texture but adjusting salt slightly may be needed to balance flavors.

Gluten-Free Zucchini Fritters

Use a gluten-free flour blend instead of all-purpose flour. Almond or chickpea flour works well and adds a nutty flavor. These are great for those avoiding gluten but still want that crispy texture.

Note: Gluten-free flours absorb moisture differently; you might need to tweak quantities to get the right batter consistency.

Zucchini and Corn Fritters

Mix in 1/2 cup of fresh or frozen corn kernels for a sweet, crunchy addition that complements the zucchini. These fritters are colorful and fun, great for summer meals or kids.

Note: Corn adds moisture, so drain well and possibly add a bit more flour to maintain firmness.

Herb-Loaded Zucchini Fritters

Double the amount of fresh herbs by adding dill, chives, and basil along with parsley. This variation tastes extra fresh and vibrant, perfect for herb lovers craving a garden-fresh bite.

Note: More herbs won’t affect texture but can add moisture, so keep an eye on batter thickness.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

Fritters can safely sit at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered loosely with foil or a clean kitchen towel. Beyond this, they may lose crispness and freshness.

Refrigerator

Store fritters in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. They will lose some crispness but can be reheated in a skillet to restore texture. Serving cold is possible but less enjoyable.

Freezer

Freeze fritters in a single layer on a baking sheet, then transfer to a freezer bag for up to 2 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat in a hot skillet to regain crispiness.

Reheating

Reheat fritters in a non-stick skillet over medium heat for 3-4 minutes per side, adding a splash of oil if needed. Avoid microwaving as this makes them soggy.

Make Ahead

You can prepare the batter a few hours ahead and keep it covered in the fridge. Fry just before serving for best texture. Alternatively, cook fritters ahead and reheat in the oven or skillet.

FAQ

Can I make these fritters vegan?
Yes! Replace eggs with flax eggs (1 tablespoon flaxseed meal + 3 tablespoons water per egg) and omit Parmesan or use a vegan cheese alternative. Note the texture will be slightly different and may be less firm, so add a bit more flour to help binding.
What if I don’t have fresh parsley?
You can substitute with other fresh herbs like cilantro, basil, or dill for different flavor profiles. Dried herbs aren’t recommended as they lack the fresh brightness and can taste muted.
How do I prevent fritters from falling apart?
Make sure to squeeze out all excess moisture from zucchini and mix batter well with enough flour and eggs to bind. Also, avoid flipping too early and use a non-stick skillet for easier handling.
Can I bake these instead of frying?
Yes, bake fritters on a parchment-lined sheet at 400°F for about 15-20 minutes, flipping halfway through. They won’t be as crispy as fried but still tasty and a bit lighter.
How do I store leftovers?
Keep leftover fritters in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a skillet to restore crispiness. Avoid microwaving as it makes them soggy.
Can I add other vegetables?
Absolutely! Finely grated carrots, corn, or chopped spinach work well. Just be sure to adjust moisture levels by squeezing out extra liquid and possibly adding a bit more flour.
What’s the best oil for frying?
Use a neutral oil with a high smoke point like vegetable, canola, or light olive oil. Extra virgin olive oil has a lower smoke point and stronger flavor, which can affect frying.
Why are my fritters soggy or greasy?
This usually happens if the zucchini wasn’t dried enough or the oil temperature was too low. Make sure zucchini is well-drained and heat oil to medium, adjusting as needed to maintain temperature.
