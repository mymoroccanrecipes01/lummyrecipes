Refreshing Asian Cucumber Salad with Tangy Sesame Dressing

Lummy Hayes
Published on August 8, 2026
15 min Prep Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This Asian cucumber salad is light and crisp, with a tangy sesame dressing that makes every bite so juicy and fresh. The quick rice vinegar marinade is my secret weapon. Such a perfect side for warm days or a simple lunch!

Introduction

I stumbled on this Asian cucumber salad a few summers ago when I was craving something super fresh and easy to whip up. The first time I made it, I forgot the sugar and honestly, it was a bit too sharp, so I learned that little balance really matters here. What makes this salad stand out is the quick marinade — just rice vinegar and toasted sesame oil with a touch of soy sauce — that brings out the cucumbers’ natural crunch and adds a subtle nutty flavor. I love how it’s ready in no time, making it perfect for those warm days when you want something light but satisfying. Plus, the toasted sesame seeds sprinkled on top add a lovely texture and a hint of earthiness. Every time I serve this, it disappears fast, and it’s great alongside grilled meats or just on its own for a refreshing lunch. The cucumbers stay crisp but soak up all those bright flavors beautifully. Honestly, this recipe has become a go-to for me when I want something healthy, quick, and bursting with flavor.

What You'll Need

Approximately 4 servings

  • 3 medium cucumbers, thinly sliced
  • 2 tablespoons rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon toasted sesame seeds
  • 2 green onions, thinly sliced
  • Optional: pinch of red chili flakes

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by thinly slicing the cucumbers. I prefer using a mandoline slicer here to get super even, paper-thin slices that soak up the dressing better. If you don’t have one, a sharp knife works fine too — just try to keep the slices uniform so they marinate evenly. After slicing, sprinkle the cucumbers with salt and toss gently. Let them sit for about 10 minutes to draw out excess moisture. This step is key because it keeps the salad crisp and prevents it from becoming soggy once dressed.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers will look slightly wilted and release some liquid into the bowl
Step 2
While the cucumbers are resting, whisk together rice vinegar, toasted sesame oil, sugar, and soy sauce in a small bowl. The sugar balances the acidity of the vinegar, while the sesame oil adds a toasty depth. Make sure the sugar dissolves completely for a smooth dressing. If you like a little heat, add a pinch of red chili flakes here. This dressing is the flavor bomb that transforms the cucumbers, so don’t skip whisking it well.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Dressing should be smooth and slightly glossy with no grainy sugar
Step 3
After the cucumbers have released some liquid, drain off the excess water but don’t rinse them — you want to keep that salty flavor. Toss the cucumbers with the prepared dressing, making sure every slice is coated. The cucumbers will start to soak up the marinade immediately, becoming juicy and flavorful. Let the salad sit at room temperature for about 10 minutes before serving to allow flavors to meld.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers look glossy and slightly translucent from the marinade
Step 4
Just before serving, stir in the sliced green onions and sprinkle the toasted sesame seeds on top. The green onions add a fresh sharpness that brightens the whole dish, while the sesame seeds provide a lovely crunch and nutty aroma. Give everything one last gentle toss to combine. This final touch makes the salad visually appealing and adds layers of texture.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Salad has vibrant green onion pieces and evenly distributed sesame seeds
Step 5
Taste the salad and adjust seasoning if needed. Sometimes I like to add a little more soy sauce for saltiness or a splash more rice vinegar for extra tang. Remember, this salad is all about balance, so tweak it to your liking. Serve chilled or at room temperature — both work beautifully. It’s best enjoyed within a few hours for maximum crispness.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Balanced tangy, salty, and nutty flavor with fresh cucumber crunch
Step 6
For a little extra pop, try adding thinly sliced fresh herbs like cilantro or mint right before serving. They add a fresh aroma and complexity that pairs perfectly with the sesame dressing. Just don’t add herbs too early or they might wilt and lose their vibrancy.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Herbs look bright and fresh without wilting
Step 7
If you want to prep this salad ahead, keep the cucumbers and dressing separate until just before serving. This prevents the cucumbers from getting soggy overnight. Mix them together and add green onions and sesame seeds fresh for the best texture and flavor.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers remain crisp and dressing stays fresh
Step 8
Avoid over-marinating cucumbers for more than a few hours as they will soften too much and lose their refreshing crunch. This salad is best when the cucumbers still have that satisfying snap. If you need to keep it longer, refrigerate and add dressing just before serving.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Cucumbers retain firmness without becoming mushy
Step 9
Serve this salad as a side dish with grilled meats, steamed rice, or as a light lunch on its own. It’s great for picnics, potlucks, or anytime you want a fresh, healthy bite. The balance of tangy, nutty, and salty flavors makes it super versatile and crowd-pleasing.
⏱ Time: N/A
✓ Doneness: Salad is chilled or room temperature, flavors well blended

Pro Tips

Salt the cucumbers and let them sit to draw out moisture before dressing.
Salting pulls excess water from the cucumbers, which prevents the salad from becoming watery and helps the cucumbers soak up the marinade better. Skipping this step often leads to a soggy salad with diluted flavors.
Use a mandoline slicer for uniformly thin cucumber slices.
Evenly thin slices ensure the cucumbers marinate uniformly and absorb the dressing well. Uneven slices can cause some pieces to be under-marinated or overly soggy, affecting texture and taste.
Whisk the dressing ingredients until sugar dissolves completely.
Properly dissolving the sugar creates a smooth, balanced dressing that coats the cucumbers evenly. Grainy sugar can lead to uneven sweetness and a gritty texture.
Add green onions and sesame seeds just before serving.
Adding these fresh ingredients last preserves their crunch and vibrant flavor. If added too early, the green onions can wilt and the seeds may lose their crispness, diminishing texture contrast.
Keep cucumbers and dressing separate if making ahead.
Marinating cucumbers too long causes them to soften excessively. Storing them separately lets you preserve cucumber crunch and freshness, combining just before serving for best texture.
Opt for toasted sesame oil rather than plain sesame oil.
Toasted sesame oil has a richer, nuttier flavor that defines the salad. Plain sesame oil lacks this depth and results in a flatter, less aromatic dressing.
Taste and adjust seasoning before serving.
Flavor balance varies with ingredient brands and freshness. Tasting lets you tweak acidity, salt, or sweetness for a perfectly balanced salad that suits your palate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Skipping the salting step for cucumbers

Why it happens Many cooks rush to dress the cucumbers without salting, assuming it’s unnecessary or time-consuming.

Fix Take the extra 10 minutes to salt and drain cucumbers. This step removes excess water, preventing a watery salad and ensuring the cucumbers soak up the dressing without getting soggy.

Using plain sesame oil instead of toasted sesame oil

Why it happens Sometimes the difference isn’t obvious at first glance, and cooks grab whatever sesame oil is available.

Fix Always choose toasted sesame oil for its rich, nutty flavor. It’s the key to the salad’s distinctive taste and aroma. Using plain oil will result in a bland dressing.

Cutting cucumbers unevenly or too thick

Why it happens Without a slicer, uneven or thick slices are common, which affects marinating and texture.

Fix Use a mandoline or very sharp knife to slice thin and even cucumbers. This ensures even flavor absorption and a pleasant crunchy texture.

Over-marinating the salad for too long

Why it happens Some think the longer it marinates, the better the flavor, but cucumbers can get mushy.

Fix Limit marinating to about 10-15 minutes after dressing. If prepping ahead, keep cucumbers and dressing separate and combine just before serving.

Not dissolving sugar fully in the dressing

Why it happens Rushing the dressing prep leads to grainy sugar and uneven sweetness.

Fix Whisk the dressing ingredients thoroughly until the sugar dissolves completely for a smooth, balanced taste.

Variations

Spicy Asian Cucumber Salad

Add finely chopped fresh chili or a teaspoon of chili garlic sauce to the dressing for a spicy kick. The heat pairs wonderfully with the cool cucumbers, making it a great side for grilled dishes or barbecues.

Note: Adjust chili quantity to your spice tolerance and add it to the dressing during whisking for even distribution.

Cucumber and Carrot Ribbon Salad

Include thin carrot ribbons along with cucumbers for extra color and crunch. The carrots add sweetness and a beautiful contrast, making the salad more vibrant and nutritious.

Note: Use a vegetable peeler to create long, thin carrot ribbons and toss them with cucumbers before marinating.

Sesame Ginger Cucumber Salad

Incorporate freshly grated ginger into the dressing for a warm, zesty flavor that complements the sesame and vinegar. This variation adds a refreshing zing and depth to the salad.

Note: Add about a teaspoon of grated ginger to the dressing and whisk well to combine.

Cucumber Salad with Fresh Herbs

Mix in chopped fresh cilantro, mint, or basil just before serving for an herbal lift. These herbs brighten the salad and add complexity to each bite.

Note: Add herbs last to keep them fresh and vibrant, and avoid wilting.

Sweet and Sour Cucumber Salad

Increase sugar slightly and add a splash of lime juice to the dressing for a sweeter, tangier profile. This variation is a crowd-pleaser and great for those who enjoy a balance of sweet and sour flavors.

Note: Adjust sugar and lime juice to taste, whisking well to dissolve sugar completely.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This salad can sit safely at room temperature for up to 2 hours when covered. Beyond that, refrigeration is recommended to keep it fresh and crisp.

Refrigerator

Store in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Note that cucumbers will soften and lose some crunch when chilled. For best texture, serve chilled but add sesame seeds and green onions fresh before serving.

Freezer

Freezing is not recommended as cucumbers become watery and mushy upon thawing, losing their crisp texture.

Reheating

No reheating needed. Serve cold or at room temperature for optimal freshness and crunch.

Make Ahead

You can slice and salt cucumbers 1 day ahead, then store them drained in the fridge. Prepare dressing separately and combine just before serving to keep cucumbers crisp and flavorful.

FAQ

Can I use regular vinegar instead of rice vinegar?
You can, but regular white or apple cider vinegar is more acidic and sharper, which might overpower the delicate cucumber flavor. Rice vinegar is milder and lends a subtle sweetness that balances the salad perfectly. If using regular vinegar, reduce the quantity slightly and consider adding a bit more sugar to balance acidity.
How long can I store this salad in the fridge?
For best texture, eat within 1-2 days. The cucumbers tend to release water and soften over time, making the salad less crisp. To extend freshness, keep cucumbers and dressing separate and toss them together just before serving.
What can I substitute for toasted sesame oil if I don’t have any?
Toasted sesame oil has a unique nutty aroma that’s hard to replace. If you don’t have it, you can use a small amount of regular sesame oil or a mild nut oil like walnut oil, but the flavor will be less intense. Adding a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds can help boost nuttiness.
Why do I need to salt the cucumbers before marinating?
Salting draws out excess water from cucumbers, which prevents your salad from being watery and helps the dressing cling better. It also softens the cucumbers slightly, making them more tender but still crisp, improving the overall texture.
Can I add other vegetables to this salad?
Definitely! Thinly sliced carrots, radishes, or bell peppers work well and add color and crunch. Just make sure to slice them thinly so they marinate evenly and maintain the salad’s refreshing texture.
How do I make the salad spicier?
Add a pinch of red chili flakes or finely chopped fresh chili peppers to the dressing. Start with a small amount and adjust to your heat preference. The spicy contrast pairs wonderfully with the cool cucumbers.
Can I prepare this salad ahead for a party?
Yes, but keep cucumbers and dressing separate until just before serving to prevent sogginess. You can slice and salt cucumbers a day ahead and store them drained. Mix with dressing and fresh garnishes right before guests arrive for best results.
What’s the best way to slice cucumbers for this salad?
Using a mandoline slicer is ideal for thin, even slices that absorb dressing well and create a pleasing texture. If you don’t have one, use a sharp knife and aim for uniform thickness to ensure even marination.
