Easy Crispy Tofu Stir Fry with Fresh Veggies for Busy Weeknights

Lummy Hayes
Published on July 7, 2026
15 min Prep Time
30 min Time
4 Servings
Easy Difficulty

This crispy tofu stir fry is a quick, tasty vegetarian dinner that never feels boring. With fresh veggies and a simple but flavorful sauce, it’s perfect for those hectic weeknights when you want something wholesome and fast.

Introduction

I stumbled upon this crispy tofu stir fry when I was craving something quick, healthy, and totally satisfying after a long day. I’d tried tofu before, but it often turned out soggy or bland. The trick I learned? Pressing the tofu well and coating it lightly with cornstarch before frying, which gives it that perfect crunch every time. I also love how the fresh veggies add vibrant colors and textures, making this dish feel fresh and lively. The sauce is super simple yet balanced with salty, sweet, and tangy notes that tie everything together beautifully. This recipe has become a go-to for busy weeknights when I want something wholesome without spending forever in the kitchen. Plus, it’s totally vegetarian but still hearty enough to satisfy everyone at the table. You’ll end up with crispy, golden tofu, tender-crisp veggies, and a sauce that clings to every bite. Honestly, it’s one of those meals that feels like a treat but comes together so fast. If you’re new to tofu or looking for a reliable stir fry, this one’s a winner in my book!

What You'll Need

4 servings

  • 14 oz firm tofu, drained and pressed
  • 2 tablespoons cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 1 red bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 cup broccoli florets
  • 1 medium carrot, julienned
  • 3 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, grated
  • 3 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon maple syrup or honey
  • 1 teaspoon sesame oil
  • 2 green onions, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame seeds (optional)

How To Do It

Step 1
Start by pressing your tofu to remove excess moisture—wrap it in a clean kitchen towel and place a heavy object on top for at least 15 minutes. This step is crucial because removing water helps the tofu crisp up nicely when cooked. If you skip this, your tofu will steam instead of fry, resulting in a soggy texture. Once pressed, cut the tofu into 1-inch cubes and toss them gently with the cornstarch until evenly coated. The cornstarch creates a light crust that crisps up beautifully in the pan.
⏱ Time: 20 minutes (including pressing)
✓ Doneness: Tofu pieces feel dry to the touch and are fully coated with a fine powdery layer of cornstarch
Step 2
Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat until shimmering but not smoking. Carefully add the tofu cubes in a single layer, making sure not to overcrowd the pan. Fry the tofu without moving it for about 3-4 minutes on one side so it develops a golden brown crust. Then, gently flip and cook the other sides until all are crispy and golden. Avoid stirring too often here; letting the tofu sit undisturbed allows the Maillard reaction to work its magic, creating that perfect crispy exterior.
⏱ Time: 10 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tofu cubes are golden brown on all sides and have a firm, crisp texture
Step 3
Remove the crispy tofu from the pan and set aside on a paper towel-lined plate to drain any excess oil. Wipe out the pan if needed, then add a splash of oil and toss in the minced garlic and grated ginger. Sauté for about 30 seconds until fragrant but not browned. This quick step infuses the dish with warm, aromatic flavors that build the base for the sauce.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Aromatic smell of ginger and garlic fills the kitchen without any browning
Step 4
Add the sliced bell pepper, broccoli florets, and julienned carrot to the pan. Stir-fry over medium-high heat for 4-5 minutes until the vegetables are tender-crisp. Stirring constantly keeps the veggies evenly cooked and prevents burning. The goal is to maintain their bright colors and slight crunch, which contrasts nicely with the crispy tofu.
⏱ Time: 5 minutes
✓ Doneness: Vegetables are vibrant in color and just tender when pierced with a fork
Step 5
In a small bowl, whisk together the soy sauce, rice vinegar, maple syrup (or honey), and sesame oil. Pour this sauce over the vegetables in the pan and toss well to coat everything evenly. Let the sauce cook for 1-2 minutes to thicken slightly and meld the flavors. This simple sauce is the flavor heart of the dish, balancing salty, sweet, and tangy notes.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Sauce thickens slightly and glazes the vegetables with a shiny coating
Step 6
Return the crispy tofu to the pan and gently toss everything together to combine. Heat through for another 1-2 minutes so the tofu absorbs some sauce without losing its crunch. Be gentle here to keep the tofu cubes intact and crispy.
⏱ Time: 2 minutes
✓ Doneness: Tofu is warmed and coated with sauce but remains crispy
Step 7
Turn off the heat and sprinkle the sliced green onions and toasted sesame seeds over the stir fry. These final touches add freshness and a little nutty crunch that elevates the dish. Serve immediately over steamed rice or noodles for a complete meal.
⏱ Time: 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Fresh garnishes are evenly distributed and dish looks vibrant and ready to eat
Step 8
If you prefer your stir fry spicier, try adding a pinch of red pepper flakes or a drizzle of chili garlic sauce when adding the sauce. This small adjustment can add a nice kick without overpowering the other flavors.
⏱ Time: Optional, 1 minute
✓ Doneness: Spicy element is fully incorporated and balanced with other flavors
Step 9
Clean up your prep area while the stir fry cooks to save time. Having everything ready before starting helps keep the cooking process smooth and stress-free, especially when working with quick stir fry recipes like this one.
⏱ Time: During cooking
✓ Doneness: Prep area is tidy and you feel ready to eat without extra cleanup stress

Pro Tips

Press your tofu thoroughly before cooking.
Removing excess water from tofu is essential to achieve a crispy texture. Water causes steaming rather than frying, resulting in soggy tofu. Pressing ensures the tofu browns evenly and crisps up, which makes a big difference in mouthfeel.
Don’t overcrowd the pan when frying tofu.
If the pan is crowded, the tofu will steam instead of fry, preventing it from getting crispy. Fry in batches if needed and give each piece space to brown properly on all sides.
Use high heat for stir-frying vegetables.
High heat cooks vegetables quickly, preserving their crunch and vibrant color. Low heat makes them soggy and dull. Stir constantly to avoid burning while ensuring even cooking.
Add the sauce at the end and toss gently.
Adding the sauce last prevents the tofu from becoming soggy. Toss gently to coat the tofu and vegetables without breaking the tofu pieces, maintaining texture and presentation.
Use fresh garlic and ginger for best flavor.
Fresh aromatics release essential oils that transform simple dishes. Pre-minced or powdered versions lack the brightness and depth that fresh garlic and ginger provide in stir fries.
Toast sesame seeds before adding as garnish.
Toasting sesame seeds enhances their nutty flavor and adds a subtle crunch. Untoasted seeds taste bland and don’t contribute much to the dish’s texture.
Serve immediately for best texture.
This stir fry tastes best fresh because tofu loses its crispness as it sits. Delaying serving leads to soggy tofu and limp vegetables, diminishing the dish’s appeal.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Not pressing the tofu enough

Why it happens Many cooks underestimate how much water tofu holds or skip pressing to save time, assuming it won’t affect texture much.

Fix Take the time to press tofu well for at least 15 minutes using a towel and weight. This step is non-negotiable for crispy tofu. If rushed, use extra cornstarch but results won’t be as good.

Overcrowding the pan when frying tofu

Why it happens Trying to cook all tofu at once saves time but causes steaming instead of frying.

Fix Cook tofu in batches with space around each piece. This allows proper browning and crisping. Use a large pan or wok if possible.

Cooking vegetables too long

Why it happens Some think vegetables should be very soft in stir fry, but this loses texture and color.

Fix Cook vegetables quickly over high heat until just tender-crisp. They should still have a bit of bite and bright color.

Adding sauce too early

Why it happens Adding sauce before vegetables are cooked can make the dish watery and soggy.

Fix Wait until vegetables are cooked, then add sauce and toss briefly. This keeps the dish flavorful and crisp.

Using low-quality soy sauce

Why it happens Using cheap soy sauce can impart a harsh or overly salty flavor.

Fix Choose naturally brewed soy sauce with balanced saltiness and umami. Adjust quantities to taste.

Variations

Spicy Sriracha Tofu Stir Fry

Add 1-2 teaspoons of sriracha or chili garlic sauce to the stir fry sauce for a spicy kick. This variation adds heat without overwhelming the fresh veggies and crispy tofu. Serve with a sprinkle of fresh cilantro to cool things down.

Note: Be cautious adding spicy sauce early; add it with the soy sauce to avoid burning the chili.

Peanut Sauce Tofu Stir Fry

Swap the soy sauce mixture for a peanut sauce made from peanut butter, soy sauce, lime juice, and a touch of honey. This gives a creamy, nutty twist that pairs beautifully with tofu and vegetables.

Note: Thin the peanut sauce with warm water to get the right consistency for coating without clumping.

Tofu Stir Fry with Cashews

Add roasted cashews during the last minute of cooking for an added crunch and nutty flavor. This variation makes the dish more substantial and adds a lovely texture contrast.

Note: Toast cashews lightly before adding to enhance their flavor.

Gluten-Free Tamari Tofu Stir Fry

Use tamari instead of soy sauce to make this recipe gluten-free. Tamari has a similar rich flavor and works well as a substitute for those avoiding gluten.

Note: Check that all other ingredients, like vinegar and sesame oil, are gluten-free to keep the dish safe.

Winter Vegetable Tofu Stir Fry

Swap summer veggies for winter options like sliced Brussels sprouts, kale, and parsnips. Cook slightly longer to soften these heartier vegetables, creating a cozy, seasonal twist.

Note: Adjust cooking time to ensure root vegetables are tender without overcooking tofu.

Storage & Tips

Room Temperature

This stir fry can be kept at room temperature for up to 2 hours if covered, but the tofu will lose its crispness and veggies may soften.

Refrigerator

Store leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days. The tofu will soften in the fridge, so reheat gently to restore some texture. Reheating in a skillet with a splash of oil works best.

Freezer

Freeze the stir fry in a freezer-safe container for up to 2 months. Wrap tightly to prevent freezer burn. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before reheating. Note that freezing may change the tofu’s texture.

Reheating

Reheat in a skillet over medium heat with a splash of vegetable oil to crisp the tofu again. Avoid microwaving as it makes tofu soggy. Heat until steaming hot throughout, about 5 minutes.

Make Ahead

You can press and cube the tofu 1 day ahead and store it wrapped in the fridge. Chop vegetables the day before and keep them in airtight containers. Mix the sauce ingredients ahead to save time when cooking.

FAQ

Can I use soft or silken tofu instead of firm tofu?
Soft or silken tofu is not recommended for stir fry because it’s too delicate and will break apart during cooking. Firm or extra-firm tofu holds its shape better and crisps up nicely, giving you the texture you want.
How do I prevent the tofu from sticking to the pan?
Make sure your pan is hot enough before adding tofu and use enough oil to coat the bottom. Also, avoid moving the tofu too soon; letting it sear undisturbed for a few minutes helps form a crust that naturally releases from the pan.
Can I use frozen tofu for this recipe?
Yes! Frozen tofu has a chewier texture that some people prefer. Thaw it completely, then press out excess water before cooking. The freezing process creates a spongier texture that soaks up sauce wonderfully.
What can I substitute for cornstarch?
Potato starch or arrowroot powder are great substitutes that also create a crispy coating. All-purpose flour can be used but tends to produce a heavier crust and may absorb more oil.
How do I make this recipe gluten-free?
Replace soy sauce with tamari or coconut aminos, which are gluten-free. Double-check that other ingredients, like vinegar and any pre-made sauces, don’t contain gluten.
Can I add other vegetables to this stir fry?
Absolutely! This recipe is very flexible. Try snap peas, mushrooms, zucchini, or baby corn. Just adjust cooking times to ensure all veggies remain tender-crisp.
Is it better to use a wok or a skillet?
Both work well, but a wok heats quickly and allows for better tossing and even cooking of ingredients. If you don’t have a wok, a large nonstick or cast iron skillet will do the job just fine.
How do I store leftovers without losing tofu crispiness?
Store tofu and vegetables separately if possible. Reheat tofu in a hot skillet with a little oil to help regain crispness. Avoid microwaving leftovers as it makes tofu soggy.
